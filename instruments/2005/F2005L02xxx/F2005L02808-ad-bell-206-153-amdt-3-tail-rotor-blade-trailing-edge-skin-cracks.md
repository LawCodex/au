# AD/BELL 206/153 Amdt 3 - Tail Rotor Blade Trailing Edge Skin Cracks

**AIRWORTHINESS DIRECTIVE**

On the effective date specified below, and for the reasons set out in the background section, the CASA delegate whose signature appears below revokes Airworthiness Directive (AD) AD/BELL 206/153 Amdt 2 and issues the following AD under subregulation 39.001(1) of CASR 1998. The AD requires that the action set out in the requirement section (being action that the delegate considers necessary to correct the unsafe condition) be taken in relation to the aircraft or aeronautical product mentioned in the applicability section: (a) in the circumstances mentioned in the requirement section; and (b) in accordance with the instructions set out in the requirement section; and (c) at the time mentioned in the compliance section.

| Background: | Transport Canada received reports of three occurrences of skin cracks originating near the tail rotor blade trailing edge balance weight. Two of the occurrences resulted in loss of the weight and a strip of material along the trailing edge, leading to an imbalance, which caused fracture of three of the four tail rotor gearbox attachments. One of these occurrences resulted in the gearbox shifting that caused failure of the drive shaft, which resulted in loss of yaw control.<br>Amendment 1 introduced Agusta-Bell AB206A/B helicopters to the requirements of this Directive.<br>Amendment 2 introduced terminating action requiring affected blades to be inspected for acceptable tail rotor blade skin thickness.<br>Amendment 3 is issued in response to a revision of the related Transport Canada AD, which introduces more stringent tail rotor skin damage limits, and re-balance requirements.<br>Amendment 2 of this Directive became effective on 30 September 2004.<br>Amendment 1 of this Directive became effective on 6 May 2004.<br>The original issue of this Directive became effective on 26 March 2004. |
