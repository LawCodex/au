## Schedule 2—Description of ecological community

###### Illawarra-Shoalhaven shrublands on volcanic outcrops

The ecological community is the assemblage of plants, animals and other organisms associated with and dependent upon, a type of dense (at times patchy) shrubland typically dominated by *Melaleuca armillaris* (bracelet honey-myrtle) or *Kunzea ambigua* (tick bush). *Acacia* spp. (wattle) and *Platysace lanceolata* (shrubby platysace) can dominate or co-dominate the canopy in certain locations, particularly after disturbance events such as fire.

Illawarra-Shoalhaven shrublands on volcanic outcrops occur from south of Wollongong to north of Bateman’s Bay and is restricted to exposures of igneous rocky outcrops with shallow soils, typically associated with Bumbo Latite and Milton Monzonite but may occur on other igneous substrates such as Termeil Essexite. It mostly occurs on ridgetops, hills, benches and slopes between the coastal escarpment (the Great Eastern Escarpment) and the sea. Rocky outcrops can also support moist areas, for example, near creeks or where soils are less skeletal, where rocks provide protection and the shrub canopy is dense.

The diversity within the shrub layer varies but can include other shrub and small tree species such as *Acacia mearnsii* (black wattle), *A. parramattensis* (Parramatta wattle), A. parvipinnula (silver-stemmed wattle), *Indigofera australis* (Australian indigo) and *Notelaea venosa* (smooth mock-olive). Trees can occur as sparse or scattered emergents or on the margins of the ecological community and may include *Acacia implexa* (hickory wattle), *Alphitonia excelsa* (red ash), Eucalyptus tereticornis (forest red gum) and *Pittosporum undulatum* (sweet pittosporum).

The understorey supports greater floristic diversity and can vary from shrubby to grassy. A low shrub layer typically includes *Styphelia sieberi* (long-flower beard-heath) as a sub-dominant species. Other species may include *Breynia oblongifolia* (coffee bush), *Coleus graveolens* (fragrant spur-flower)*,* Hibiscus heterophyllus subsp. heterophyllus (native rosella), *Indigofera australis* (Australian indigo), *Prostanthera* nivea (snowy mint-bush), Solanum celatum, S. vescum (gunyang, green kangaroo apple), Phyllanthus gunnii (scrubby spurge) P. *parviflorus* (cockspur flower) and Zieria granulata (Illawarra zieria)*.*

Herbaceous species may include *Calandrinia pickeringii* (pink purslane), *Commelina cyanea* (scurvy weed), *Crassula sieberana* (austral stonecrop), *Euchiton japonicus* (clustered/creeping cudweed), *Pelargonium inodorum* (kapota), *Scaevola aemula* (fairy fanflower), *Veronica plebeia* (trailing speedwell), *Xerochrysum bracteatum* (golden everlasting) and *Irenepharsus trypherus* (Illawarra Irene). Orchids and lilies may occur, such as *Arthropodium minus* (small vanilla-lily), *Caesia parviflora* (pale grass-lily), *Caladenia tessellata* (thick-lipped spider orchid), *Calochilus pulchellus* (pretty beard orchid), *Dianella longifolia* (flax lily) and Geitonoplesium cymosum (scrambling lily).

Grasses can occur particularly where the shrub canopy is less dense. Ferns may be present, such as *Asplenium flabellifolium* (necklace fern), *Cheilanthes distans* (bristly cloak fern), *C. sieberi* (mulga fern) and *Pellaea falcata* (sickle fern). Moist areas of the ecological community can include mosses and sundews. Twining species may be present in the understorey and/or canopy.

The ecological community includes a variety of fauna species, including several species that are listed as threatened at a national or state level.
