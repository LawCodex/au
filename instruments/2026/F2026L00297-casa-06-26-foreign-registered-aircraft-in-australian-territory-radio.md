Pip Spence\

CASA 06/26 — Foreign Registered Aircraft in Australian Territory (Radio Altimeters) Direction 2026

1 Name

This instrument is *CASA 06/26 — Foreign Registered Aircraft in Australian Territory (Radio Altimeters) Direction 2026*.

*Note* In this instrument, certain terms and expressions have the same meaning as they have in the *Civil Aviation Act 1988* and the regulations. These include ***aerodrome***, ***AOC***, ***Australian territory***, ***foreign air transport AOC***, ***foreign registered aircraft***, ***low-visibility approach***, ***New Zealand AOC with ANZA privileges*** and ***operator***, of an aircraft.

2 Duration

This instrument:

(a) commences on 1 April 2026; and

(b) is repealed at the end of 31 March 2029.

*Note* For regulation 11.250 of CASR, the directions issued in this instrument cease to be in force at the end of 31 March 2029.

3 Application

This instrument applies to the operator of an aircraft if the aircraft is:

a foreign registered aircraft that is operating in Australian territory under a foreign air transport AOC; or

(b) a foreign registered aircraft that is operating in Australian territory under a New Zealand AOC with ANZA privileges that is in force for Australia.

4 Directions — Radio altimeters

(1) Subject to subsection (3), the operator of the aircraft must ensure that each radio altimeter fitted to an aeroplane used for the conduct of a low-visibility approach in Australian territory complies with the requirements for tolerance to radio frequency interference specified for a “radio altimeter tolerant airplane” in FAA Airworthiness Directive AD 2023-10-02 (***FAA AD 2023-10-02***) as existing on 26 May 2023.

*Note 1* In FAA AD 2023-10-02, a “radio altimeter tolerant airplane” is defined for the purposes of the AD as “one for which the radio altimeter, as installed, demonstrates the tolerances specified in paragraphs (g)(1)(i) and (ii) of this AD, using a method approved by the FAA.”.

*Note 2* 26 May 2023 is the date FAA AD 2023-10-02 was published in the Federal Register of the United States Government.

(2) Subject to subsection (3), the operator of the aircraft must ensure that each radio altimeter fitted to a rotorcraft used for the conduct of a low-visibility approach in Australian territory complies with the requirements for tolerance to radio frequency interference specified for a “radio altimeter tolerant rotorcraft” in FAA Airworthiness Directive AD 2023-11-07 (***FAA AD 2023-11-07***) as existing on 22 June 2023.

*Note 1* In FAA AD 2023-11-07, a “radio altimeter tolerant rotorcraft” is defined for the purposes of the AD as “one for which the radio altimeter, as installed, demonstrates the tolerances specified in paragraphs (g)(1)(i) and (ii) of this AD, using a method approved by the FAA.”.

*Note 2* 22 June 2023 is the date FAA AD 2023-11-07 was published in the Federal Register of the United States Government.

(3) Subsections (1) and (2) do not apply to the operator of an aircraft used for the conduct of a low-visibility approach at an aerodrome that is included in a written list of aerodromes, as the list exists from time to time, that is:

(a) maintained by CASA for the purposes of this instrument; and

(b) published on the CASA website.

*Note* As at commencement of this instrument, the list is available on CASA’s website at

&lt;https://www.casa.gov.au/operations-safety-and-travel/safety-advice/5g-and-aviation-safety&gt;

(4) Subsection (3) is in force until the end of 31 March 2027.
