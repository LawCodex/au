###### 5 Definitions

In these Guidelines:

***Act*** means the *National Measurement Act 1960*.

***Product Symbol*** *means a symbol used to indicate the product of 2 or more Australian legal units of measurement.*

***Regulations*** *mean the National Measurement Regulations 1999.*

***SI Prefix*** *means a prefix mentioned in Schedule 3 of the Regulations.*

###### 6 Objects

These Guidelines govern the way in which Australian legal units of measurement may be combined:

(a) to produce an Australian legal unit of measurement; and

(b) with prefixes to produce Australian legal units of measurement.

###### 7 Physical quantities

Australian legal units of measurement may be formed only for the physical quantities as prescribed under the Regulations.

###### 8 Combining Australian legal units of measurement

An Australian legal unit of measurement for a physical quantity may be formed by combining Australian legal units of measurement according to the mathematical relations linking the corresponding physical quantities.

The resulting Australian legal unit of measurement may be represented by using a name or symbol that is itself formed from derived units.

*Example* pascal (pressure in newtons per square metre): Pa = N/m2*.*

###### 9 Combining Australian legal unit of measurement and prescribed SI prefix

A decimal multiple, or submultiple, of an Australian legal unit of measurement must be formed by using a single SI Prefix.

*Example* Nanometre: 1 nm or 1 x 10-9 m, but not 1 mµm.

Subsection 9(1) of these Guidelines does not apply to *kilogram*.

*Example* 10-6 kg or 1 mg, but not μkg.

*Note* Kilogram is an SI base unit of measurement: see Regulations, Sch 1, item 1.1.

An additional derived unit of measurement, mentioned in Part 4 of Schedule 1 to the Regulations, may be combined with another Australian legal unit of measurement to form an Australian legal unit of measurement.

*Note* An exponent associated with a symbol for an Australian legal unit of measurement indicates that the multiple, or submultiple, of the unit is raised to the power expressed by the exponent.

The combination of the unit and a prefix may be combined with other Australian legal unit of measurement symbols to form the symbol for a compound unit that is an Australian legal unit of measurement.

*Example* Speed in kilometres per hour: km/h.

###### 10 Australian legal units of measurement that must not be combined with prefixes

The following Australian legal units of measurement must not be combined with SI prefixes:

kilogram

minute

hour

day

decibel

inch

foot

hectare

ounce

foot/minute

degree

minute

second of arc

radian

steradian

degree Celsius

A decimal multiple or sub-multiple of *kilogram* must be formed by attaching a prefix to *gram*.

*Note* For historical reasons, kilogram already contains a prefix.

###### 11 Australian legal units of measurement that may only be combined with prefixes that form multiples of the unit

*Tonne* may be combined only with an SI Prefix that gives a decimal multiple of *tonne*.

*Example* kilotonne, but not millitonne*.*

###### 12 Expression of Australian legal units of measurement

Names and symbols of Australian legal units of measurement must not be used together in the same expression.

*Example* km/h, but not kilometre/h.

An adjective or sign must not be added to the name or symbol of an Australian legal unit of measurement.

*Example* Wa cannot be used as the unit of acoustic power.

The symbol for an SI Prefix must be placed immediately before the symbol of the Australian legal unit of measurement to which it refers, without an intermediate space.

*Examples*

1 milliwatt: mW

2 kilometre: km

3 gigatonne: Gt

4 megajoule: MJ

For a decimal multiple or submultiple of an Australian unit of measurement that is expressed as a fraction, an SI prefix: may precede the Australian legal unit of measurement in the numerator or the denominator, or both if there is no other way to form an unambiguous expression; and should precede the Australian legal unit of measurement in the numerator.

*Examples*

1 km/s, but not mm/s or m/ms

2 mm/s, but not m/ks

Only a single SI Prefix may be used to form a multiple of a derived Australian legal unit of measurement.

The product symbol is: a dot (·); if the resulting expression is unambiguous — a full stop (.); or if the symbol for an Australian legal unit of measurement is not the same as an SI prefix — a space.

*Example* Thermal resistivity (metre kelvin per watt): m·K/W, not m K/W which may be confused with millikelvin per watt (mK/W).

The product symbol must be used only with symbols that represent Australian legal units of measurement.

*Example* N·m or N m, but not Newton·metre

An Australian legal unit of measurement derived from the division of 2 other Australian legal units of measurement may be expressed using an oblique stroke (/), a horizontal line or a negative exponent to produce an unambiguous expression.

The oblique stroke must not be repeated on a line unless ambiguity is avoided by the use of parentheses. In a complex expression, negative exponents or parentheses must be used.

[image: Example 1 start formula m divided by s superscript 2 end formula, start formula start fraction m over s superscript 2 end fraction end formula, or start formula m times s superscript minus 2 end formula, but not start formula m divided by s divided by s end formula Example 2 start formula m times kg divided by open brackets s superscript 3 times A close brackets end formula, or start formula m times kg times s superscript minus 3 times A superscript minus 1 end formula, but not m times kg divided by s superscript 3 divided by A end formula.]

A space must be left after the numerical value of an Australian legal unit of measurement and before the name or symbol of the unit of measurement.

*Examples*

1 22 m, but not 22m

2 27 volts, but not 27volts

3 15 °C, but not 15°C

*Note 1* When expressing temperature in degrees Celsius, a space must not be left between the symbol for degrees and the symbol for Celsius. See example 3.

However, subsection 12(10) of these Guidelines does not apply when expressing the numerical values and the symbols for degrees, minutes and seconds of: geographic coordinates; or

*Example* 33°43'03.0"S 151°09'37.1"E

plane angles

*Example* 45°, not 45 °
