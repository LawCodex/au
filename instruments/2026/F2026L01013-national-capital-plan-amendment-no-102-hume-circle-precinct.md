## Precinct location

The Hume Circle Precinct is located at the junction of Canberra Avenue, Sturt Avenue and Wentworth Avenue. Broadly, the outer boundaries of the circle are formed by McMillan Crescent and extend to the southern boundary of Mildura Street and the western boundary of Dalby Street.

## Background

The Griffins’ plans for Canberra envisaged the main avenues and approach routes as axial lines and principal ordering elements of the city. The sequence of arrival to the National Capital and the Central National Area was consciously orchestrated in the Griffins’ plans through a series of gateways and scenic approaches.

The junctions between the main avenues were planned to be important gateways to the National Capital marked by elegant nodes framed by mixed land use and urban form. Hume Circle is part of the major eastern approach route, and completes one corner of the triangle between Mount Ainslie, Capital Hill and Hume Circle.

Within the 1918 Griffin Plan, a circular street layout was adopted for the Hume Circle Precinct with surrounding blocks subdivided by a series of radial streets emanating from the centre of the Circle with urban form situated on land adjacent the Hume Circle circumference. By completing Hume Circle (through an extension of McMillan Crescent), establishing the radial geometry, and establishing an enhanced urban form, many aspects of the 1918 Griffin Plan can be retrieved in a modern context.

Urban renewal of the Hume Circle Precinct will provide an opportunity to revitalise, embed design excellence, and celebrate Canberra’s unique landscape character while placing high value on human scale public places that prioritise pedestrians, cycling, and public transport. It will enable more people to live in proximity to employment hubs, social and cultural infrastructure, and sustainable public and active travel transport options.

Hume Circle Precinct will become a prominent mixed-use precinct linking the central south-east to Parliament House via Canberra Avenue to Commonwealth Avenue into the city centre.

This will be supported by an integrated greenspace network with broad tree-lined corridors that emphasise the radial axis from Hume Place and integrate outdoor dining and street parking to create active and attractive spaces. It will ensure a unified landscape and built form that responds to vision, place, community, character, and function.

There is a National Capital interest in ensuring that:

The Hume Circle Precinct is developed as a high-quality urban precinct.

Building massing and form reflect the Griffin geometry of and surrounding Hume Place and the Causeway.

The symbolic importance of Canberra Avenue as a Main Avenue and its function as a key access corridor to the city is protected and supported through high quality design in architecture, landscape, and public realm.

## Objectives for the Hume Circle Precinct

Develop the Hume Circle Precinct as an exemplar of urban regeneration which will welcome visitors from the east and provide a gateway to the Central National Area.

Reinforce and realise the Griffins’ vision by:

extending McMillan Crescent to complete this circle

reinstating the intent of the radial geometry contemplated by the Griffin Plan including axial streets and a visual connection along the Causeway axis.

Establish higher order land uses on land currently used for light industry and road reservation, commensurate with the potential for urban renewal.

Transform the existing traffic character of Hume Circle to a people focused and connected precinct.

Showcase design excellence and sustainability in landscape, architecture, and the public realm.

Realise the advantages of the location as a public transport and employment node at the intersection of significant main avenues.

Optimise accessibility to surrounding public infrastructure, transport, employment and recreation areas afforded by the site’s proximity to key centres such as Kingston, Manuka, Parliament, Airport, Fyshwick, Kingston Foreshore and Civic.

Provide a mix of land-uses, open space and buildings to attract activity and investment.

## Indicative urban structure and opportunities

### General

The indicative urban structure for Hume Circle Precinct reflecting the objectives for the Precinct and the potential opportunities for the future of the precinct is illustrated in Figure 147.

New development within the Precinct should support the national significance of the Precinct, through reinforcing and reinstating Griffin Plan elements where possible.

There is opportunity for future changes to road alignments, block configuration and open space arrangements as development proposals contribute to the renewal of the Precinct and delivery of the indicative urban structure reflected in Figure 147.

Where road arrangements change, land may become available for other uses and would be considered under a Land Use category as appropriate to its relative location to other sites and their land use (refer Figure 146) and which would best contribute to achieving the indicative urban structure shown in Figure 147.

### Completion of the outer circle of McMillan Crescent

A new shared way through Sub-Precinct C completes the Griffin geometry of the Outer Circle of McMillan Crescent. The shared way will be primarily pedestrian in nature, with limited vehicle use accommodated within low-speed shared zones. It will have an asymmetric cross section with a one or two-way flush carriageway and landscape zone to the centre, and wide pedestrian walkways along the built form edges.

The New Shared way should:

be a low speed, shared street environment with pedestrian priority

provide flush paving treatment (no raised kerbs)

function as a linear park

provide pedestrian walkways to built-form edges

include significant tree and low-level planting.

### Reinforce the Causeway

The Causeway Radial will form the primary north-south visual connection reinstating Griffin’s original Causeway alignment. It will function primarily as a linear park with a strong landscape character and facilitate pedestrian and cycle movement through wide paths.

The Causeway Radial should:

provide a visual connection link

frame views to Mount Ainslie

create a central landscape zone

include significant tree and low-level planting.

### Create the east-west Radial

The East-West Radial emanating from Hume Place will form the primary east-west pedestrian link through Sub-Precinct C, from Hume Place toward Dalby Street. It will have a strong landscape character and function as a linear park with a generous central landscape zone and wide pedestrian walkways along the built form edges.

The East-West Radial should:

be a pedestrian and cycle link

functions as a linear park

provide pedestrian walkways to built-form edges

create a central landscape zone

include significant tree and low-level planting.

### Establish the Town Square

A Town Square open space located at the intersection of the Completed Outer Circle and the East West Radial will provide amenity and outdoor recreation facilities for residents and the public alike. It will contribute to the appeal of development and the well-being of residents. Its central location provides good visual and physical accessibility.

The Town Square should:

be publicly accessible at all times

be inclusive, and flexible to cater for day-to-day use and community events

include adequate lighting to promote safety and evening use

be framed by building frontages with active uses on the ground floor (cafés, restaurants, retail, community spaces). Active ground floor uses fronting onto the area may utilise areas of the public space for outdoor seating, provided this does not impede the free movement of people around the space

include deep soil zone for the full extent of the open space

include a high-quality landscape to provide a local park and passive recreational opportunities for a wide variety of users

include tree planting and low-level landscape to mitigate urban heat island effects

incorporate opportunities for public art and interpretation.

## Detailed conditions of planning, design and development

### General

The primary radial and circular structuring geometry of the Hume Circle Precinct should be developed and maintained to reinforce the Griffin Plan including the creation of a visual connection along the original Causeway alignment.

The completed circle, extension of McMillan Crescent through Sub-Precinct C, will be publicly accessible and provide a pedestrian-prioritised connection from Canberra Avenue through to Mildura Street.

The creation of a new east-west radial link emanating from the centre of Hume Circle through Sub-Precinct C will function as a publicly accessible pedestrian- and cyclist-prioritised link.

Canberra Avenue will be reinforced as an approach route from the east and Hume Circle reinforced as a gateway through urban renewal of the Precinct, in particular Sub-Precinct C.

Development of the Hume Circle Precinct will complement and be integrated with that in the broader East Lake area.

High standards of urban design, landscape and sustainability should define all new development, ensuring the area reflects the significance of the National Capital. Urban design should prioritise flexibility, allowing buildings and public spaces to adapt to changing uses and community needs over time.

Architecture should embrace contemporary styles and materials, with engaging street-level design and careful attention to relationships between buildings and the public realm.

Public spaces should be designed to be safe, comfortable, pedestrian-scaled, and well-connected, encouraging walking, public transport use, and a reduced reliance on cars. Streets and pathways should form a well-connected, accessible network linking new development to the surrounding areas and broader travel networks. Streets, buildings, and public spaces should be designed for accessibility, diversity and resilience, supporting long-term adaptability and innovation in urban development.

New development should respect and enhance the unique character of the Precinct while complementing existing residential areas and buildings and supporting future urban growth. Designs should be developed which are connected to country and which appropriately respond to the historic significance of the area.

The natural topography should be integrated with the built form to maintain the visual and symbolic links to key landmarks.

Leading practices with respect to environmentally sustainable development should be prioritised. Sustainable building design, landscape and green infrastructure should contribute to a low-carbon, climate-resilient urban neighbourhood. Appropriate deep soil planting should be incorporated into the design of both public and private spaces to enhance sustainability, resilience, and contribute to living infrastructure.

### Building height

Maximum permitted building heights in the Hume Circle Precinct should accord with the following principles:

building heights to the inner circle, surrounding the Canberra Avenue, Wentworth Avenue, and Sturt Avenue intersection, transition in scale to a maximum of 8 storeys (no more than 28 metres) in Sub-Precinct C to allow ‘buildings within a landscape’ to define the gateway

building heights to a maximum of 12 storeys (no more than 40 metres) on land surrounding the completed Hume Circle (McMillan Crescent extension) in Sub-Precinct C

building heights to transition in scale from a maximum of 8 storeys (no more than 28 metres) to Dalby Street in Sub-Precinct C as development moves away from the completed McMillan Crescent extension and to lower scale neighbouring areas

building heights to a maximum of 8 storeys (no more than 28 metres) in Sub- Precinct B to respond to the current neighbourhood context

building heights to a maximum of 3 storeys in Sub-Precinct D to reflect the existing residential character and provide a transition to the lower scale of the surrounding neighbourhood

building heights adjacent to heritage items (near the Dairy Site) appropriately respond to the heritage fabric, scale and context of the site, and transition in height up to a maximum of 8 storeys (no more than 28 metres) if appropriate.

Except for Sub-Precinct D, building height above these maximum height principles, may be considered where one or more of the below criteria are met and it is approved by the National Capital Authority:

the proposal is for a landmark site, a small footprint tower, which reinforces the urban structure of the Precinct

the proposed development provides a high percentage contribution of the total development site to open/public space

the proposed development contributes to housing diversity, including social / affordable housing

the proposed development delivers design excellence and exemplar sustainability approaches and a high sustainability rating through a recognised rating/certification system

the proposed development makes a positive contribution to the community or utility infrastructure and/or public realm in the precinct.

Building elements above the maximum height limits are permitted to accommodate roof top plant and equipment and to facilitate roof top access and enjoyment of roof top spaces including communal gardens and recreation spaces. No habitable room is permitted above maximum height limits.

In all circumstances (except for Sub-Precinct D) an absolute maximum height limit of 15 storeys (no more than 50 metres or RL617 whichever is lower) applies.

### Building form

Building form should address the key structuring elements of the Precinct and reinforce the proposed Griffin geometry.

Building design should consider the establishment of podium level structures with a street wall which reinforces the proposed geometry and provides legibility while supporting pedestrian movement and landscaping strategies.

Building massing should reinforce the proposed geometry at podium levels, with towers allowed to orient to maximise amenity. A defined street wall height of 4-6 storeys should create a clear framing of streets and open spaces.

Prominent urban markers may be created as wayfinding landmarks and key orientation points within the Precinct, supported by street-wall buildings that follow the radial or axial geometry that accentuates the pedestrian experience at ground level.

Building length should be minimised and articulated with a high level of visual interest, through site links and recesses to minimise perceived bulk, and provide visual and physical permeability through a site and the Precinct.

Built form should be articulated to respond to important building elements such as pedestrian entries and corners. Vertical and horizontal articulation should be incorporated into built form to create rhythm, visual interest and aesthetic appeal.

Vehicular access driveways should be minimised in width to minimise visual impact.

### Building design quality

Development proposals should demonstrate:

how the proposal responds to the objectives for the Precinct and contributes to the surrounding area

inclusive and universal design to ensure that Hume Circle Precinct is a place for everyone and that equitable access is provided

the durability of buildings and the public realm to provide a place that is long-lasting and adaptable for future needs

sustainable design that contributes to the local economy

Buildings should respond to their orientation through sun shading strategies where appropriate. The consideration of façade articulation, depth, and external sun shading elements should contribute to the overall architectural expression of buildings and create visual interest and human scale at the street interface.

### Landscape structure and network

The landscape structure and character of the Precinct should contribute to reducing the urban heat island effect and create cool, comfortable, shaded environments.

The Hume Circle Precinct should include a spectrum of publicly accessible spaces, including streets, public, communal spaces and private spaces that will be places of respite and recreation. They will support natural systems and biodiversity, whilst managing urban heat, water reticulation and quality.

Streets, publicly accessible and private communal open spaces should complement each other and collectively contribute to amenity, deep soil planting, and connectivity. The open space elements should together form a cohesive and connected public domain.

Private open space at ground level should complement the public open space network by adding to precinct connectivity, providing through-site links, enabling views of private open space from the public domain (and vice versa), and adding to precinct tree canopy cover, and landscape area.

### Movement and circulation

Streets and other connections through the Precinct should offer options for safe, attractive and convenient travel for pedestrians, cyclists and other modes of transport.

Where possible, the active travel network should cater to both commuter and recreational needs and provide separation both spatially and by visual character where appropriate to prevent conflicts between pedestrians and other modes of transport.

Pedestrian and cycle networks should be planned to connect to existing and future active travel infrastructure and offer connections to the surrounding neighbourhoods and local destinations.

The provision of shade, street furniture, lighting and wayfinding must be incorporated into the design of pedestrian and cycle networks.

Design of these networks must also demonstrate universal access and facilitate ease of movement for all users.

### Streetscape design

A series of tree lined streets, shared ways and pedestrian connections will provide access, amenity and connectivity throughout the Precinct.

The street hierarchy and structure should:

support the objectives for the Precinct by reinforcing the Griffin circular geometry (as far as practicable), the Causeway alignment and the east-west radial axis

prioritise pedestrian and cyclist movement, connectivity and comfort, followed by that for private vehicles

provide vehicular access to and circulation through the Precinct

provide secondary streets for vehicle servicing, on-street parking and loading zones within the Precinct

ensure that tree canopy cover is maximised across the street network to provide shade, visual amenity and ecological benefits, and ensure that sufficient tree soil volumes are provided to support tree canopy growth

include low level planting along streets that exhibits a local character and is suited to local growing conditions

prioritise low level plantings in verges in preference to turf and hard landscape treatments

be designed to accommodate all potential users and abilities

provide regular seating opportunities along streets, laneways and connections.

### Access and parking

Parking must be carefully planned and designed to avoid dominating the public realm or adversely impacting landscape amenity within the Precinct.

On the major avenues - Canberra Avenue, Wentworth Avenue, Sturt Avenue - carpark and service vehicle access should be avoided and structured or large surface car parks are not permitted.

Car parking should be accommodated in basements or in above‑ground structures concealed from public areas by habitable building façades. On‑grade parking is discouraged.

Consolidated basement parking should be prioritised between buildings where possible to support limited access points and reduced impact to the ground level of buildings. Consolidated basements will also support the achievement of deep soil planting and landscape amenity principles in publicly accessible spaces.

On-street car parking and conveniently located bicycle parking to support retail uses, pedestrian amenity, and after-hours activity should be provided. Additional parking should be provided for bicycles, motorcycles, and vehicles owned and operated under car-sharing schemes.

There is no mandatory minimum parking provision rate in the Precinct. Car parking for new developments is subject to individual assessment and approval by the National Capital Authority.

#### Electric vehicle infrastructure

Development proposals must demonstrate that buildings will be Electric Vehicle (EV) ready and accommodate EV charging infrastructure. This includes parking spaces that have appropriate wiring, a covered outlet, and a final connection point for an EV charger.

EV charging infrastructure should also be considered in public car parks and in streets.

### Sustainability and environment

Sufficient planting area, canopy trees, deep soil zones and water sensitive urban design measures will be provided to enhance living infrastructure, support healthy tree growth and minimise stormwater runoff.

Urban heat island effects will be reduced through limiting impervious surfaces and provision of canopy trees and plants.

Threats to biodiversity such as noise, light pollution, invasive species incursion or establishment, chemical pollution, or site disturbance should be avoided or minimised through good design.

Development must be respectful of heritage buildings.

Environmental risks, including noise, bushfire, flooding, contamination, air quality or hazardous materials should be appropriately considered for the development of site.

No new gas network connections are allowed to all new or existing developments including redevelopments.

### Residential apartment design

Development proposals that include plans for residential apartments will be required to meet the residential apartment design principles set out below.

#### Solar and daylight access

The floor or internal wall of a daytime living area of no fewer than 70% of apartments in a building receive a minimum of 3 hours direct sunlight between 9 am and 3 pm at the mid-winter solstice (on 21 June).

No more than 15% of apartments in a building receive no direct sunlight between 9 am and 3 pm on 21 June.

#### Natural ventilation

At least 60% of apartments in the first 9 storeys of a building achieve natural cross ventilation. Apartments at ten storeys or greater are deemed to be cross ventilated only if any enclosure of the balconies at these levels allows adequate natural ventilation and cannot be fully enclosed.

All habitable rooms, including bathrooms and toilets, should be naturally ventilated. Building design should demonstrate consideration of the building’s orientation to maximise capture and use of prevailing breezes, depths of habitable rooms, openable windows and the use of awnings and louvres to funnel breezes. Light wells must not be the primary air source for habitable rooms.

Overall depth of a cross-over or cross-through apartment should not exceed 18m, measured glass line to glass line.

#### Ceiling heights

Measured from finished floor level to finished ceiling level, minimum ceiling heights are:

3.3m at ground and first floors for future flexibility of use

2.7m for habitable rooms

2.4m for non-habitable rooms.

#### Apartment size and layout

Apartments are required to have the following minimum internal areas:

| Apartment type | Minimum internal area |
|---|---|
| Studio | 40m² |
| 1 bedroom | 50m² |
| 2 bedroom | 70m² |
| 3 bedroom | 95m² |

A work-from-home space will add a minimum of 6m² additional area.

In each case, an additional bathroom will add 5m² extra area, and an additional bedroom will add 12m² extra area.

The minimum floor area figures exclude area for balcony and car parking facilities.

Storage allowance within dwellings is included in the stated figures.

All habitable rooms have an operable window in an external wall with a total minimum glass area of not less than 15% of the floor area of the room. Courtyards, skylights and high-level windows (with sills above 1700mm) may only be used as a secondary light source in habitable rooms. ‘Snorkel’ arrangements are not permitted as the only means of providing solar access to habitable rooms.

#### Private open space and balconies

Requirements for private open space for apartments is as follows:

| Apartment type | Minimum area | Minimum depth |
|---|---|---|
| Studio | 8 m² | 2 metres |
| 1 bedroom | 8 m² | 2 metres |
| 2 bedroom | 10 m² | 2 metres |
| 3+ bedroom | 12 m² | 2.4 metres |

Apartments at ground level or on a podium are required to have private open space of a minimum area of 15m2 and minimum depth of 3 metres.

#### Apartment mix

Development should provide a mix of apartment typologies that provide housing choice and affordability to households of all shapes and sizes.

A range of apartment types and sizes should be provided to cater for different household types. Flexible apartment configurations should be considered to support diverse household types and stages of life including single person households, families, multi-generational families, and group households.

For developments with 40 or more dwellings, a combination of studios or 1-bedroom dwellings, 2-bedroom dwellings and dwellings with 3 or more bedrooms should be provided at the following rates:

Studio or 1-bedroom – maximum 40%

2-bedroom – maximum 40%

3 or more bedrooms – minimum 10%.

Larger apartment types should be located on the ground floor or uppermost level where there is potential for more open space and on corners where more building frontage is available.

### Development proposals – Detailed Conditions

For development proposals in the Hume Circle Precinct, the National Capital Authority may require detailed conditions of planning design and development specific to the development proposal (Detailed Conditions).

Where required, these Detailed Conditions will be subject to public consultation and approval by the National Capital Authority prior to an application for works approval.

Development proposals for which Detailed Conditions may be required include, for example, developments that extend over more than one block, involve the amalgamation or reconfiguration of blocks and/or which significantly contribute to the objectives for urban structure of the Precinct.

Detailed Conditions for a development proposal must address the requirements of this Precinct Code and provide development specific detail on, at a minimum:

urban structure:land usesubdivision and stagingaccess, road layout and car parking arrangementsstreet hierarchy and movement networkstreetscape.

built form:building height, massing, separation and setbacks apartment sizes and mix design quality and details for residential apartments, including natural ventilation and solar access architectural designhousing diversity, including social and affordable housingactive frontages and streetscape activation landscape requirements for development blocks.

public realm:

landscape structure

communal and open space

tree canopy and deep soil planting

water sensitive urban design

public art, signage, and lighting

heritage and cultural considerations

Development proposals should be supported by relevant studies and information addressing the impacts on and/or contribution to the Hume Circle Precinct and surrounding areas, including:

proposed additional number of dwellings, increase in population and expected demographics

analysis of impact on social, community and utilities infrastructure of the development and in the context of potential future urban development in the Precinct and area

transport analysis and traffic modelling to reflect the impact of the development and in the context of future urban development in the Precinct and area

integration of the development into the Precinct and adjoining areas.

#### Implementation

Proponents should approach the NCA on the intent to prepare a development proposal for the Hume Circle Precinct and the NCA will confirm if Detailed Conditions specific to that development proposal are required.

Where Detailed Conditions are required they will be subject to public consultation of a duration appropriate to the scale of the development.

Detailed Conditions may be subject to independent review by the National Capital Design Review Panel or alternative independent body.

Detailed Conditions are subject to approval by the National Capital Authority.

Subsequent applications for works must demonstrate that proposals are not inconsistent with the National Capital Plan, this Precinct Code and the approved Detailed Conditions specific to the development where relevant.
