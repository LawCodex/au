###### 5 Definitions

Note: A number of expressions used in this instrument are defined in the Act, including the following:

(a) Australian territory;

(b) biosecurity officer;

(c) human remains;

(d) landing place;

(e) listed human disease;

(f) port.

In this instrument:

***Act*** means the *Biosecurity Act 2015*.

***hermetically‑sealed container*** means an airtight container that bodily fluids cannot leak from when sealed.

***human biosecurity official*** has the same meaning as in the *Biosecurity Regulations 2026*.

***medical practitioner*** means a person registered or licensed as a medical practitioner under an Australian or foreign law that provides for the registration or licensing of medical practitioners.

### Part 2—Managing human remains

###### 6 Human remains brought into Australian territory

(1) For the purposes of subsection 110(2) of the Act:

(a) column 1 of an item of the following table specifies classes of human remains; and

(b) column 2 of that item specifies the requirements for either or both of the following:

(i) bringing human remains in those classes into Australian territory;

(ii) managing human remains in those classes after bringing them into Australian territory.

Note 1: Human remains do not include the cremated remains of a deceased human’s body or hair, teeth or bones of a deceased human’s body that have been separated from the deceased human’s body or a part of the deceased human’s body (see the definition of ***human remains*** in section 9 of the Act). There are no requirements under the Act for bringing the cremated remains of a deceased human’s body into Australian territory or managing such remains after bringing them into Australian territory.

Note 2: Hair, teeth or bones of a deceased human’s body that have been separated from the deceased human’s body, or a part of the deceased human’s body, must not be brought or imported into Australian territory unless the conditions (if any) specified in the determinations made under subsection 174(1) of the Act are complied with.

Note 3: An individual may also be required to answer questions or provide written information in relation to human remains (see paragraph 56(1)(c) of the Act).

Note 4: A biosecurity officer, human biosecurity officer or chief human biosecurity officer may also give a direction for managing specified human remains to the person who is responsible for the human remains (see section 111 of the Act).

Permission to bring body or part of body into Australian territory

(2) Bringing a body or part of a body into Australian territory is permitted under this subsection if:

(a) permission to bring the body or part of the body into Australian territory is given by a human biosecurity official before the body or part of the body is brought into Australian territory; or

(b) permission to bring the body or part of the body into Australian territory is given by a human biosecurity official at the first landing place or port in Australian territory where the body or part of the body is brought; or

(c) both of the following apply:

(i) permission (the ***interim permission***) is given by a biosecurity officer, at the first landing place or port in Australian territory where the body or part of the body is brought, for the body or part of the body to be transported from that landing place or port to a specified place under the control of a specified person;

(ii) within 24 hours after the interim permission is given, permission to bring the body or part of the body into Australian territory is given by a human biosecurity official.

Note: This subsection applies to paragraph (a) of column 2 of item 1, and subparagraph (a)(i) of column 2 of item 2, of the table in subsection (1).

Requirements for certificate from medical practitioner

(3) For the purposes of subparagraph (a)(iii) of column 2 of item 1 of the table in subsection (1), the certificate must state:

(a) if the body or part of the body has, or had before death, one or more signs or symptoms of a listed human disease—that fact and the listed human disease; or

(b) that the body or part of the body does not have, or did not before death have, one or more signs or symptoms of a listed human disease.

Requirements for declaration from person bringing in body or part of body

(4) For the purposes of subparagraph (a)(ii) of column 2 of item 2 of the table in subsection (1), the declaration must state:

(a) that the body or part of the body has been donated for scientific or research purposes; and

(b) either:

(i) if the person making the declaration is aware that the body or part of the body has, or had before death, one or more signs or symptoms of a listed human disease—that fact and the listed human disease; or

(ii) that, as far as the person making the declaration is aware, the body or part of the body does not have, or did not before death have, one or more signs or symptoms of a listed human disease.

###### 7 Individuals who have died in transit or on arrival

For the purposes of subsection 112(1) of the Act:

(a) column 1 of an item of the following table specifies classes of persons who bring into Australian territory, or who manage, the bodies of deceased individuals who die:

(i) in transit before arriving in Australian territory; or

(ii) on arrival in Australian territory; and

(b) column 2 of that item specifies the requirements for persons in those classes.

<td>The biosecurity officer must:<br>(a) identify any risk of a listed human disease entering, or emerging, establishing itself or spreading in, Australian territory or a part of Australian territory as a result of the body of a deceased individual being brought into Australian territory; and<br>(b) inform a human biosecurity official of any identified risk of a listed human disease entering, or emerging, establishing itself or spreading in, Australian territory or a part of Australian territory as a result of the body being brought into Australian territory; and<br>(c) follow infection control procedures, including appropriate use of personal protective equipment; and<br>(d) take the measures (if any) required by a human biosecurity official to prevent, or reduce the risk of, a listed human disease entering, or emerging, establishing itself or spreading in, Australian territory or a part of Australian territory as a result of the body being brought into Australian territory</td>

<td>The person must:<br>(a) identify any risk of a listed human disease entering, or emerging, establishing itself or spreading in, Australian territory or a part of Australian territory as a result of the body of a deceased individual being brought into Australian territory; and<br>(b) follow infection control procedures, including appropriate use of personal protective equipment; and<br>(c) take the measures (if any) required by a human biosecurity official to prevent, or reduce the risk of, a listed human disease entering, or emerging, establishing itself or spreading in, Australian territory or a part of Australian territory as a result of the body being brought into Australian territory</td>
</tr>

Note 1: An individual may also be required to answer questions or provide written information in relation to an individual who has died in transit before arriving, or on arrival, in Australian territory (see paragraph 56(1)(c) of the Act).

Note 2: A biosecurity officer, human biosecurity officer or chief human biosecurity officer may also give a direction for managing specified human remains to the person who is responsible for the human remains (see section 111 of the Act).

Note 3: An operator of an aircraft or vessel may also be required to include in a report details of any individuals on board who have died during the flight or voyage (see Part 1 of Chapter 3 of the *Biosecurity Regulations 2026*).
