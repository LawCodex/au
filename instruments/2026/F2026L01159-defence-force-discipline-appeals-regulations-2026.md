###### 3 Authority

###### 4 Schedules

Each instrument that is specified in a Schedule to this instrument is amended or repealed as set out in the applicable items in the Schedule concerned, and any other item in a Schedule to this instrument has effect according to its terms.

###### 5 Definitions

Note: A number of expressions used in this instrument are defined in the Act, including the following:

(a) appeal;

(b) appellant;

(c) member or member of the Tribunal;

(d) the Tribunal.

In this instrument:

***Act*** means the *Defence Force Discipline Appeals Act 1955*.

***Deputy Registrar*** means a Deputy Registrar appointed under subsection 19(2) of the Act.

***detention centre*** has the same meaning as in the *Defence Force Discipline Act 1982*.

***legal practitioner*** has the same meaning as in section 39 of the Act.

***naval vessel*** means:

(a) a warship or other vessel that:

(i) is operated for naval or military purposes by Australia; and

(ii) is under the command of a member of the Defence Force; and

(iii) bears external marks of nationality; and

(iv) is crewed by seafarers under armed forces discipline (however described); or

(b) a Government vessel (within the meaning of the *Navigation Act 2012*) that is used only on government non‑commercial service as a naval auxiliary.

***Registrar*** means the Registrar of the Tribunal, but does not include a Deputy Registrar.

***Registrar of Military Justice*** means the Registrar of Military Justice appointed under subsection 188FB(1) of the *Defence Force Discipline Act 1982*.

### Part 2—Appeals to the Tribunal

###### 6 Period for appeal from a decision of a single member of the Tribunal

For the purposes of subsection 17(2) of the Act, the prescribed period within which a person affected by a decision of a single member of the Tribunal referred to in that subsection may appeal to the Tribunal is 28 days beginning on the day after the person is notified of the decision.

###### 7 Lodgement of appeals etc.

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 21(1)(b) of the Act, the following persons are prescribed:

(a) a Deputy Registrar;

(b) the Registrar of Military Justice;

(c) if the appellant is in custody on board a ship (other than a naval vessel) on board which is also a body, contingent or detachment of the Defence Force—the officer commanding the body, contingent or detachment;

(d) if the appellant is confined in a detention centre—the officer in charge of the detention centre;

(e) if the appellant is confined in a civil prison in Australia—the Governor of the prison;

(f) if none of paragraphs (c), (d) and (e) applies to the appellant—the officer commanding any unit of the Defence Force.

(2) If an appeal, or an application for leave to appeal, is lodged with a person referred to in subsection (1), that person must, as soon as reasonably practicable, forward the following to the Registrar:

(a) the appeal or application;

(b) the date and time the appeal or application was lodged with that person;

(c) the name and position title of that person.

###### 8 Manner of enforcing order for payment of costs

For the purposes of subsection 37(4) of the Act, an order for the appellant to pay an amount under subsection 37(3) of the Act may be enforced in either or both of the following ways:

(a) by recovering the amount, or a part of the amount, as a debt due to the Commonwealth by action in a court of competent jurisdiction;

(b) by deducting the amount, or a part of the amount, from any pay or allowances earned by the appellant as a member of the Defence Force.

###### 9 Discontinuance of appeals etc.

An appellant may, at any time before the hearing of an appeal or application for leave to appeal commences, by giving notice in writing to the Registrar, do either of the following:

(a) discontinue the appeal;

(b) withdraw the application.

###### 10 Alternative means for appearances

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 60(a) of the Act, the Tribunal may, on application or on the Tribunal’s own initiative, direct or permit a person to appear before the Tribunal using any technology that allows the person to participate in a hearing, or part of a hearing, without being physically present at the hearing or part of the hearing in relation to the hearing of:

(a) an appeal; or

(b) an application for leave to appeal; or

(c) a matter preliminary or incidental to an appeal.

(2) For the purposes of paragraph 17(1)(g) of the Act, a prescribed matter of practice or procedure is the giving of a direction or permission under subsection (1) of this section.

###### 11 Matters of practice or procedure of the Tribunal

For the purposes of paragraphs 17(1)(g) and 60(a) of the Act, the following matters of practice or procedure of the Tribunal are prescribed:

(a) giving directions in relation to an appeal or an application for leave to appeal, including, but not limited to, the management, conduct and hearing of the appeal or application;

(b) making any of the following orders:

(i) to grant a person leave to amend a document in relation to an appeal or an application for leave to appeal;

(ii) to produce any document, exhibit or other thing in relation to an appeal or an application for leave to appeal;

(iii) to adjourn, from time to time, the hearing of an appeal to the Tribunal;

(iv) to dispose of an appeal to the Tribunal by consent of the appellant;

(v) to dismiss an appeal to the Tribunal for the appellant’s failure to comply with a direction or notice given by the Tribunal or to attend a hearing related to the appeal.

Note: A single member of the Tribunal may exercise the powers of the Tribunal with respect to the matters of practice or procedure referred to in this section (see paragraph 17(1)(g) of the Act).

###### 12 Legal aid

(1) For the purposes of paragraphs 17(1)(c) and 60(d) of the Act, the Tribunal may, by order, approve the granting of legal aid to an appellant if:

(a) the appellant makes an application for approval to the granting of legal aid within the period specified in subsection (2); and

(b) the application is accompanied by a statutory declaration made by the appellant setting out such information as is necessary to enable the Tribunal to determine whether the appellant’s means are insufficient to enable the appellant to prosecute the appeal or application for leave to appeal; and

(c) the Tribunal is satisfied that:

(i) the appellant has insufficient means to enable the appellant to prosecute the appeal or application for leave to appeal; and

(ii) it appears desirable in the interests of justice that legal aid should be granted to the appellant under this section.

Note: A single member of the Tribunal may exercise the power under this subsection to approve the granting of legal aid to an appellant (see paragraph 17(1)(c) of the Act).

(2) For the purposes of paragraph (1)(a), the specified period is 14 days after the day the appellant’s appeal, or application for leave to appeal, is lodged, or such longer period as the Tribunal allows.

(3) If the Tribunal approves the granting of legal aid to an appellant under subsection (1), the Attorney‑General may, in writing, determine:

(a) an amount of fees and disbursements that are payable by the Commonwealth for a legal practitioner to represent the appellant at the hearing of the appeal or application; and

(b) any conditions to which the payment of fees and disbursements by the Commonwealth are subject.

(4) If the Attorney‑General makes a determination under subsection (3), the Commonwealth must, subject to any conditions determined under paragraph (3)(b), pay the legal practitioner the amount determined under paragraph (3)(a).

###### 13 Appellant in custody

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 60(f) of the Act, if an appellant in custody is entitled to be present at the hearing of the appellant’s appeal, application for leave to appeal or matter preliminary or incidental to an appeal, the Registrar must give the person who has the custody of the appellant a certificate signed by the Registrar:

(a) stating that the appellant is so entitled; and

(b) specifying the times at which the hearing will be held; and

(c) specifying the details of:

(i) if the appellant is directed or permitted under section 10 to participate in the hearing, or part of the hearing, using technology—the technology; or

(ii) otherwise—the place where the hearing, or part of the hearing, will be held.

(2) The person given a certificate under subsection (1) must ensure that:

(a) the appellant is taken:

(i) to the place where the hearing, or part of the hearing, will be held, as specified in the certificate at the specified times; or

(ii) to a place from where the person can access the technology specified in the certificate at the specified times; and

(b) the appellant will continue to be present at that place throughout the hearing, or part of the hearing (as the case requires); and

(c) the appellant is under guard:

(i) during the time the appellant is travelling to, and returning from, that place; and

(ii) unless the Tribunal otherwise orders—during the time the appellant is present at that place.

###### 14 Giving records of court martial proceedings etc. to the Tribunal

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 60(g) of the Act, if an appeal, or an application for leave to appeal, against a conviction, or a prescribed acquittal, by a court martial or Defence Force magistrate is lodged under the Act, the Registrar of Military Justice must, on request by the Registrar, give the following to the Tribunal for the purposes of the appeal or application:

(a) a record of the proceedings of the court martial or Defence Force magistrate;

(b) a record of any review with respect to the proceedings of the court martial or Defence Force magistrate;

(c) documents that were before the court martial, Defence Force magistrate or reviewing authority in connection with the proceedings, or the review of the proceedings.

(2) If the record or documents given to the Tribunal are no longer required for the purposes of the appeal or application, the Registrar must return the record or documents to the Registrar of Military Justice.

### Part 3—Administration of the Tribunal

###### 15 Registry

(1) For the purposes of subsection 19(3) of the Act, the Registrar must maintain a Registry of the Tribunal.

(2) The Registry must be located at such place as the President directs in writing.

(3) The Registrar is to have custody of:

(a) any records of the Tribunal kept in the Registry; and

(b) documents lodged or filed with the Registrar or a Deputy Registrar.

(4) The records and documents kept in the Registry may be electronic.

(5) The Registrar must keep proper records of:

(a) proceedings before the Tribunal; and

(b) documents in the custody of the Registrar.

###### 16 Fees to witnesses

For the purposes of subsection 34(1) of the Act, the allowances that are payable to a witness (other than a member of the Defence Force rendering service) are the fees, including witness’ travelling time, specified in item 17 of Schedule 2 to the *High Court Rules 2004*, as in force from time to time.

###### 17 Acting Registrar

(1) The assistant Registrar is to act as the Registrar:

(a) during a vacancy in the office of Registrar (whether or not an appointment has previously been made to the office); or

(b) during any period, or during all periods, when the Registrar:

(i) is absent from duty or from Australia; or

(ii) is, for any reason, unable to perform the duties of the office.

Note: For rules that apply to persons acting as the Registrar, see section 33A of the *Acts Interpretation Act 1901*.

(2) For the purposes of this section, the assistant Registrar is:

(a) if there is only one Deputy Registrar—the Deputy Registrar; or

(b) if there is more than one Deputy Registrar—the Deputy Registrar nominated by the President to be the assistant Registrar.

###### 18 Notification of hearing

For the purposes of paragraph 60(a) of the Act, as soon as practicable after a time, date and place (or technology) for a hearing before the Tribunal is fixed, the Registrar must give written notice of the time, date and place (or technology) to:

(a) either:

(i) the appellant; or

(ii) if the appellant is to be represented by a legal practitioner at the hearing—the legal practitioner; and

(b) the Chief of the Defence Force, or service chief, whose duty it is to undertake the defence of the appeal, application or matter the subject of the hearing; and

(c) the Registrar of Military Justice.

Note: The Tribunal, or a single member of the Tribunal, may direct or permit a person to participate in a hearing, or part of a hearing, using technology (see section 10).

###### 19 Procedure following decision of Tribunal

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 60(a) of the Act, as soon as practicable after making a decision on a question of law, an appeal, an application for leave to appeal or a matter preliminary or incidental to an appeal, the Tribunal must prepare a written statement that sets out:

(a) the Tribunal’s decision on the question of law, appeal, application or matter; and

(b) the reasons for the decision.

(2) As soon as practicable after preparing the written statement, the Tribunal must provide a copy of the statement to the Registrar.

(3) As soon as practicable after receiving the written statement, the Registrar must give the Registrar of Military Justice:

(a) a copy of the statement; and

(b) a copy of the orders of the Tribunal to which the decision relates.

###### 20 Fees for supplying copies

For the purposes of paragraph 60(b) of the Act, the fee for the supply, by the Registrar to an appellant, of a hard copy of a document relating to the appeal is:

(a) if the document is not more than 50 pages—$12; or

(b) if the document is more than 50 pages—$12 plus 10 cents for each page in excess of 50 pages.

### Part 4—Application, saving and transitional provisions

###### 21 Application—appeals and applications made on or after commencement

The *Defence Force Discipline Appeals Regulations 2026* apply in relation to an appeal, or an application for leave to appeal, lodged on or after the commencement of this instrument.

###### 22 Saving—pending appeals and applications

The *Defence Force Discipline Appeals Regulation 2016*, as in force immediately before the commencement of this instrument, continues to apply on and after that commencement in relation to an appeal, or an application for leave to appeal, that had been lodged before that commencement, if the Tribunal had not made a decision on the appeal or application before that commencement.

###### 23 Transitional—things done under the Defence Force Discipline Appeals Regulation 2016

(1) Subject to section 22, if:

(a) a thing was done for a particular purpose under the *Defence Force Discipline Appeals Regulation 2016*, as in force immediately before that instrument was repealed; and

(b) the thing could be done for that purpose under this instrument;

the thing has effect for the purposes of this instrument as if it had been done for that purpose under this instrument.

(2) Without limiting subsection (1), a reference in that subsection to a thing being done includes a reference to an application for approval to the granting of legal aid being made.
