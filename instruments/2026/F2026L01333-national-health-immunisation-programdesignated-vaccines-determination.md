###### 5 Definitions

In this instrument:

***µg*** means microgram.

***Aboriginal person*** means a person of the Aboriginal race of Australia.

***Act*** means the *National Health Act 1953*.

***CCID50*** means cell culture infectious dose 50%, being the quantity of an infectious agent that when inoculated onto a number of susceptible cell cultures will infect 50% of the individual cultures.

***FHA*** means filamentous haemagglutinin.

***FIM 2+3*** means fimbrial agglutinogens 2+3.

***IU*** means International Unit.

***medical risk condition***: see section 6.

***PFU*** means plaque forming units.

***PRN*** means pertactin.

***PT*** means pertussis toxoid.

***severe immunocompromise*** means a severely immunocompromising condition as described in the chapter titled *COVID‑19* in the Australian Immunisation Handbook, published by the Department, as existing on 1 October 2026.

Note: The Australian Immunisation Handbook could in 2026 be viewed on the Department’s Immunisation Handbook website (https://immunisationhandbook.health.gov.au).

***table item***, in relation to Schedule 1, means an item of a table in a clause in Schedule 1.

**TCID50** means tissue culture infectious dose 50%, being the quantity of an infectious agent that when inoculated onto a number of susceptible tissue cultures will infect 50% of the individual cultures.

***Torres Strait Islander*** means a descendant of an Indigenous inhabitant of the Torres Strait Islands.

###### 6 Meaning of medical risk condition

A ***medical risk condition*** is any of the following:

(a) functional or anatomical asplenia, including sickle cell disease, other haemoglobinopathies, congenital or acquired asplenia (e.g. splenectomy) or hyposplenia;

(b) immunocompromising conditions, including congenital or acquired immune deficiency including symptomatic IgG subclass or isolated IgA deficiency, haematological malignancies, solid organ transplant, haematopoietic stem cell transplant or HIV infection;

(c) chronic respiratory disease, including suppurative lung disease, bronchiectasis and cystic fibrosis or chronic lung disease of prematurity;

(d) chronic renal disease, including end stage renal disease (eGFR &lt;15mL/min) or relapsing or persistent nephrotic syndrome;

(e) proven or presumptive cerebrospinal fluid leak;

(f) cochlear implants;

(g) intracranial shunts;

(h) previous episode of invasive pneumococcal disease;

(i) born less than 28 weeks gestation;

(j) trisomy 21;

(k) chronic heart disease, including cyanotic heart disease or heart failure.

### Part 2—Designated vaccines and circumstances

#### Division 1—Designated vaccines

###### 7 Designated vaccines

For the purposes of subsection 9B(2) of the Act, a vaccine specified in Schedule 1 is determined to be a designated vaccine for the purposes of the Act.

#### Division 2—Circumstances

###### 8 Circumstances

For the purposes of subsection 9B(5) of the Act, the circumstances in which a designated vaccine may be provided are those specified for the vaccine in:

(a) Schedule 1; or

(b) another section of this Division.

###### 9 Catch‑up doses for persons less than 10 years old

A circumstance for a designated vaccine is that:

(a) the table item in Schedule 1 that specifies the vaccine specifies circumstances in which the vaccine may be provided to a person specified in those circumstances to be an age that is no more than 4 years; and

(b) the person did not receive the vaccine at the specified age; and

(c) the person is less than 10 years old.

Example: A vaccine is intended to be provided for the immunisation of a person at 2 months, 4 months and 6 months old but the person does not receive the vaccine at those ages, the doses of vaccine may be provided at any time before the person turns 10 years old.

###### 10 Catch‑up doses for persons at least 10 years but less than 20 years old

A circumstance for a designated vaccine is that:

(a) the vaccine is specified in table item 101, 102, 106, 202, 219, 220, 221, 222, 223, 225 or 226 in Schedule 1; and

(b) the table item specifies circumstances in which the vaccine may be provided to a person specified in those circumstances to be a certain age; and

(c) the person did not receive the vaccine at the specified age nor in accordance with the circumstance specified in section 9; and

(d) the person is at least 10 years old but less than 20 years old.

Note: The designated vaccines covered by paragraph (a) are Boostrix, Adacel, Nimenrix, H‑B‑Vax II, M‑M‑R II, Priorix, Priorix‑Tetra, ProQuad, IPOL, Varilrix and Varivax Refrigerated.

###### 11 Catch‑up doses for visa‑holders at least 20 years old

A circumstance for a designated vaccine is that:

(a) the vaccine is specified in table item 101, 102, 202, 219, 220, 223, 225 or 226 in Schedule 1; and

(b) the table item specifies circumstances in which the vaccine may be provided to a person specified in those circumstances to be a certain age; and

(c) the person did not receive the vaccine at the specified age nor in accordance with the circumstance specified in section 9 or 10; and

(d) the person is at least 20 years old; and

(e) the person holds any of the following visas granted under the *Migration Act 1958*:

(i) Subclass 200 (Refugee) visa;

(ii) Subclass 201 (In‑country Special Humanitarian) visa;

(iii) Subclass 202 (Global Special Humanitarian) visa;

(iv) Subclass 203 (Emergency Rescue) visa;

(v) Subclass 204 (Woman at Risk) visa.

Note: The designated vaccines covered by paragraph (a) are Boostrix, Adacel, H‑B‑Vax II, M‑M‑R II, Priorix, IPOL, Varilrix and Varivax Refrigerated.
