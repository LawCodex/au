## Schedule 2— Description of ecological community

**River Murray downstream of the Darling River, and associated aquatic and floodplain systems**

The ecological community consists of the assemblage of native flora, fauna and other organisms associated with and dependent upon the River Murray lowland floodplain-river system, below the river's confluence with the Darling River, and downstream to the sea. The ecological community is associated with a complex of interconnected aquatic, semi-aquatic and terrestrial habitats that form a meta-ecosystem, including: the main river channel and estuary, lakes, associated tributaries, floodplain wetlands and woodlands-forests-shrublands; and groundwater.

The area in nature for the ecological community occurs across six bio-geographical sub-units along the length of the main channel that have characteristic ecological identities and are highly connected. These sub-units are summarised as follows:

*Top Valley Section* - from the Darling junction near Wentworth, to Overland Corner. This section includes two Ramsar-listed wetland complexes, the Riverland and Banrock Station.

*Murray Gorge Section* - from Overland Corner to Mannum. The Marne and Saunders rivers enter this section from the Eastern Mount Lofty Ranges.

*Lower Swamplands Section* - from Mannum, past Murray Bridge and Tailem Bend to Wellington. Reedy Creek flows into this section.

*Eastern Mount Lofty Ranges Watershed Section* - incorporates the perennial and ephemeral streams within the region defined by the Prescribed Water Resources Areas for the Eastern Mount Lofty Ranges and the Marne-Saunders.

*Lower Lakes Section* - between Wellington and the Coorong there is a shallow lake system with marginal wetlands and adjacent ephemeral saline ponds (including Lake Alexandrina and the terminal Lake Albert).

*Coorong and Murray Mouth Section* - the estuarine terminus of the River Murray (and the MDB), including associated islands and coastal sand dune systems.

The general boundary for most of the ecological community is the 1956 flood line.

The vegetation throughout the ecological community exhibits considerable variation, both along the river and across the floodplain, depending on varying habitat types. The overstorey vegetation is typically dominated by two tree species, *Eucalyptus camaldulensis* (river red gum) and/or *Eucalyptus largiflorens* (black box), whilst *Acacia stenophylla* (river cooba) is another important canopy species in some areas. Floodplain habitats generally range from forest and woodland, through to shrubland, herbland, sedgeland and grassland areas. There is also fringing native vegetation in many areas and open water also may contain expansive and permanent stands of native aquatic vegetation (macrophytes), especially when in good condition.

The ecological community includes a variety of fauna species, both aquatic (freshwater to estuarine) and terrestrial. This includes several functionally important species, endemic species and species that are listed as threatened at a national level.
