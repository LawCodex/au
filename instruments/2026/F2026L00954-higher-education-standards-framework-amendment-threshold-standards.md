# Higher Education Standards Framework Amendment (Threshold Standards) Instrument 2026

I, Jason Clare, Minister for Education, make the following instrument.

Dated 9/7/26

Jason Clare

Minister for Education

Contents

###### 1 Name

This instrument is the *Higher Education Standards Framework Amendment (Threshold Standards) Instrument 2026*.

###### 2 Commencement

This instrument commences the day after it is registered on the Federal Register of Legislation.

###### 3 Authority

This instrument is made under subsection 58(1) of the *Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency Act 2011*.

###### 4 Schedules

Each instrument that is specified in a Schedule to this instrument is amended or repealed as set out in the applicable items in the Schedule concerned, and any other item in a Schedule to this instrument has effect according to its terms.

## Schedule 1—Amendments related to racism

##### Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021

**1 Provision numbered “2.3(4)” under heading “Wellbeing and Safety”**

Repeal the provision, substitute:

#### 4. A safe and inclusive environment is promoted and fostered for students, staff and visitors, including by:

##### a. having policies and procedures on student, staff and visitor safety with clear accountabilities for implementation

##### b. advising students and staff on actions they can take to enhance safety and security on campus and online.

**2 Provision numbered “2.4(3)” under heading “Student Grievances and Complaints”**

Repeal the provision, substitute:

#### 3. Institutional complaints-handling and appeals processes for formal complaints:

##### a. include provision for confidentiality, independent professional advice, advocacy and other support for the complainant or appellant, and provision for review by an appropriate independent third party if internal processes fail to resolve a grievance, and

##### b. are transparent and designed, and periodically reviewed, in consultation with students and staff, including those with lived experience, of or being directly affected by, discrimination, harassment, victimisation or violence, to ensure that these processes are accessible, easy to navigate, promote cultural safety, and administrative barriers are minimised.

**3 After provision numbered “3.2(5)” under heading “Staffing”**

Insert:

#### 6. Staffing policies including recruitment, retention, promotion, and training and development are inclusive and support diversity.

**4 After provision numbered “6.3” under heading “Academic Governance”**

Insert:

### 6.4 Preventing and responding to racism

#### 1. The provider develops and maintains a safe and inclusive institutional environment for staff, students and visitors that prevents and responds to racism, and other discriminatory conduct including by:

##### a. having definitions of racism and specific forms of racism relevant to a provider’s higher education operations, including racism towards Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, antisemitism, Islamophobia and any other forms of racial discrimination and vilification which are specific to the needs of the provider’s community,

##### b. developing, monitoring and maintaining an institutional environment where the nature and scope of racism, marginalisation and other discriminatory conduct, including systemic, cultural and interpersonal forms, is clearly specified and understood,

##### c. addressing the ways racism and marginalisation may manifest across the provider’s institutional environments and activities,

##### d. implementing policies, procedures, and practices including transparent complaints handling processes, that embed safe, respectful, and inclusive behaviours across all operations,

##### e. supporting equity and diversity, enabling representation and participation in institutional planning, review, and decision-making, and

##### f. providing clear guidance to students and staff on actions that enhance safety and security on campus and online.

**5 Standard 6 titled “Governance and Accountability”**

Insert in the appropriate position in the Standard:

### 6.10 Inclusive and Responsive: Consultation and engagement

#### 1. The provider actively seeks to understand the legitimate needs and expectations of students and staff, and other stakeholders that could reasonably be expected to be directly or indirectly affected by a provider’s decisions, through structured and ongoing engagement that demonstrates respect and responsiveness to those needs and expectations.

#### 2. Students and staff have meaningful, supported and appropriate opportunities to participate in academic governance, ensuring that academic decision-making reflects a diversity of discipline, student and professional staff perspectives.

#### 3. The provider has mechanisms for effective consultation with, and participation of students and staff regarding institutional planning, review and decision-making on matters that directly affect them.

**8 Provision numbered “6.2” in Standard 6 titled “Governance and Accountability”**

Repeal the provision, substitute:

### 6.2 Corporate Monitoring, Assurance and Controls

#### 1. The provider is able to demonstrate, and the corporate governing body assures itself, that the provider is operating lawfully, ethically, effectively and sustainably, including:

##### a. the governing body and the entity comply with the requirements of the legislation under which the provider is established, recognised or incorporated, any other legislative requirements and the entity’s constitution or equivalent,

##### b. the provider’s future directions in higher education have been determined and realistic performance targets have been established,

##### c. the provider’s performance, including progress against targets, is monitored and action is taken to correct underperformance

##### d. the provider is financially viable and applies, and has the capacity to continue to apply, sufficient financial and other resources to maintain the viability of the entity and its business model, to meet and continue to meet the requirements of the Higher Education Standards Framework, to achieve the provider’s higher education objectives and performance targets and to sustain the quality of higher education that is offered,

##### e. the financial position, financial performance and cash flows of the entity are monitored regularly and understood, financial reporting is materially accurate, financial management meets Australian accounting standards, effective financial safeguards and controls are operating and financial statements are audited independently by a qualified auditor against Australian accounting and auditing standards,

##### f. risks to higher education operations have been identified, the provider’s risk appetite and risk management strategy is defined, and material risks are being effectively managed and mitigated,

##### g. mechanisms for competent academic governance and leadership of higher education provision and other academic activities have been implemented, and these are operating according to an institutional academic governance policy framework and are effective in maintaining the quality of higher education offered,

##### h. educational policies and practices support participation and decision-making by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people and are respectful to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge and cultures,

##### i. qualifications are awarded legitimately,

##### j. there are credible business continuity plans and adequately resourced financial and tuition safeguards to mitigate disadvantage to students who are unable to progress in a course of study due to unexpected changes to the higher education provider’s operations, including if the provider is unable to provide a course of study, ceases to operate as a provider, loses professional accreditation for a course of study or is otherwise not able to offer a course of study,

##### k. the occurrence and nature of formal complaints, allegations of misconduct, breaches of academic or research integrity and critical incidents are monitored, addressed and action is taken to address underlying causes, and

##### l. lapses in compliance with the Higher Education Standards Framework are identified and monitored, and prompt corrective action is taken.

**9 Provision numbered “6.3” in Standard 6 titled “Governance and Accountability”**

Repeal the provision, substitute:

### 6.3 Academic Governance

#### 1. Processes and structures are established that are transparent, ensure accountability and independence, and responsibilities are assigned that collectively:

##### a. achieve effective and accountable academic oversight of the quality of teaching, learning, research and research training,

##### b. set and monitor clearly defined institutional benchmarks for academic quality and outcomes,

##### c. establish and maintain competent academic governance leadership at an institutional level, consistent with the types and levels of higher education offered, and

##### d. provide competent, transparent, timely and evidence-based advice to the corporate governing body and management on academic matters, including advice on academic outcomes, policies and practices.

#### 2. Academic oversight assures the quality of teaching, learning, research and research training effectively, including by:

##### a. developing, monitoring and reviewing academic policies and their effectiveness,

##### b. confirming that delegations of academic authority are clearly documented, understood, exercised appropriately and reviewed periodically,

##### c. critically scrutinising, approving and, if authority to self-accredit is held, accrediting or advising on approving and accrediting, courses of study and their associated qualifications,

##### d. maintaining oversight of academic and research integrity, including monitoring of potential risks,

##### e. monitoring and initiating action to improve performance against institutional benchmarks for academic quality and outcomes,

##### f. critically evaluating the quality and effectiveness of educational innovations or proposals for innovations,

##### g. evaluating the effectiveness of institutional monitoring, review and improvement of academic activities, and

##### h. monitoring and reporting to the corporate governing body on the quality of teaching, learning, research and research training.

**10 At the end of Standard 6 titled “Governance and Accountability”**

Insert:

### 6.5 Accountability: Governance structures and accountabilities

#### 1. The governing body actively oversees and is accountable for the provider’s strategy, performance, risk management, culture and compliance consistent with its purpose.

#### 2. The distinct roles and responsibilities of a provider’s governance bodies including the governing body, academic body or equivalent and senior management are clearly delineated, understood and respected.

#### 3. The provider has processes and structures that provide a clear and discernible separation of accountabilities between corporate governance, academic governance and senior management reporting to, and enabling, governing body oversight.

### 6.6 Diversity of Perspectives: Governing body composition

#### 1. The governing body collectively has the skills, knowledge, capabilities, experience, independence of mind and diversity of perspectives to perform its role and discharge its responsibilities consistent with the provider’s purpose and scale.

#### 2. The governing body is inclusive and seek continuous improvement of its performance and that of its committees.

#### 3. The governing body supports members to participate in meetings effectively, including through the provision of induction and development programs and ensuring a culture of fairness, respect, and constructive discussion.

### 6.7 Independence: Academic standards and freedom

#### 1. The governing body develops and maintains an institutional environment:

##### a. in which freedom of speech and academic freedom are upheld and protected,

##### b. where academic and research standards are upheld, and

##### c. where faculty and students are able to teach, pursue knowledge, conduct research and express scholarly ideas without undue influence, interference, or pressure from external political or commercial pressures.

### 6.8 Transparency: Purpose, strategy and performance

#### 1. The provider’s purpose, strategic and short-term objectives are clear and openly communicated.

#### 2. Governance of, and performance against, the provider’s purpose, strategic priorities and objectives are transparent to stakeholders.

#### 3. Governing bodies of higher education providers that are:

##### a. registered in the ‘Australian University’ category in Part B of the Higher Education Standards Framework, and

##### b. Table A universities as specified in subsection 16-15(1) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003,

#### publish:

##### a. outcomes of governing body meetings and its decisions taken,

##### b. consultancy spending, its purpose, value and justification,

##### c. Vice-Chancellors’ and senior executive external roles, including the management of any perceived or real conflicts of interest, and

##### d. annual remuneration reports in line with requirements for public companies.

### 6.9 Trustworthy: lawful, ethical and responsible

#### 1. The provider operates lawfully, ethically, responsibly, and consistent with its public purpose.

#### 2. The governing body is accountable for the provider’s culture and ensuring the provider develops and maintains an institutional culture of acting lawfully, ethically and responsibly, and consistent with its values and purpose.

#### 3. The governing body monitors provider culture and timely action is taken to respond to cultural issues that arise.

**11 Standard 6 titled “Governance and Accountability”**

Insert in the appropriate position in the Standard:

### 6.11 Sustainable: Risk management

#### 1. The governing body proactively and effectively oversees risks to the achievement of the provider’s purpose and objectives, consistent with the provider’s strategy and risk appetite, including risks to social license.

#### 2. The governing body ensures processes are in place to evaluate the effectiveness of its governance, risk management, and internal control processes.

### 6.12 Responsible: Workforce and remuneration

#### 1. Governing bodies of higher education providers that are:

##### a. registered in the ‘Australian University’ category in Part B of the Higher Education Standards Framework, and

##### b. Table A universities as specified in subsection 16-15(1) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003,

#### ensure:

##### a. Vice-Chancellor remuneration is publicly available, financially responsible, and aligned with publicly available remuneration frameworks, and

##### b. the provider maintains a documented, clear and sustainable workforce and remuneration strategy.

#### 1. There is a repository of publicly-available current information about the higher education provider’s operations that includes:

## Schedule 3—Other amendments

##### Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021

**1 After section 6**

Insert:

###### 7 Saved effect of standards

(1) Despite the amendments made by Schedule 1 of the *Higher Education Standards Framework Amendment (Threshold Standards) Instrument 2026*, a provision of the *Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021* that is amended by Schedule 1 continues to apply, until 1 January 2027, as if the amendments made by Schedule 1 had not been made.

(2) Despite the amendments made by Schedule 2 of the *Higher Education Standards Framework Amendment (Threshold Standards) Instrument 2026*, a provision of the *Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021* that is amended by Schedule 2 continues to apply, until the relevant dates specified in subsection 8(2), as if the amendments made by Schedule 2 had not been made, but taking into account any amendments made by Schedule 1.

###### 8 Application of amendments made by Higher Education Standards Framework Amendment (Threshold Standards) Instrument 2026

(1) The amendments made by the *Higher Education Standards Framework Amendment (Threshold Standards) Instrument 2026* in Schedule 1 of that instrument apply to all registered higher education providers from 1 January 2027.

(2) The amendments in Schedule 2 of the *Higher Education Standards Framework Amendment (Threshold Standards) Instrument 2026* apply to:

(a) registered higher education providers specified in subsection 16-15(1) of the *Higher Education Support Act 2003* (referred to as Table A providers) that are also registered in the ‘Australian University’ category in Part B of the *Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021* from 1 January 2027; and

(b) all other registered higher education providers from 1 July 2027.

(3) The amendments in Schedule 3 of the *Higher Education Standards Framework Amendment (Threshold Standards) Instrument 2026* apply to all registered higher education providers from the date that instrument commences.

**2 Definitions**

Under the heading “Definitions”, repeal the definition of “Higher education qualification”, substitute:

***Higher education qualification*** has the same meaning as *Higher education award* in section 5 of the TEQSA Act and includes the qualifications listed in Table 1 below, other than an award at an equivalent level in the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) that is offered or conferred for completion of a vocational education and training course:

**Table 1 – Australian Higher Education Qualifications**

| **AQF level** | **Qualification** |
|---|---|
| 5 | Diploma |
| 6 | Advanced Diploma |
| 6 | Associate Degree |
| 5–7 # | Undergraduate Certificate |
| 7 | Bachelor Degree |
| 8 | Bachelor Honours Degree |
| 8 | Graduate Certificate \* |
| 8 | Graduate Diploma \* |
| 9 | Masters Degree (Coursework) \* |
| 9 | Masters Degree (Extended) \* |
| 9 | Masters Degree (Research) \*□ |
| 10 | Doctoral Degree (Professional) \*□ |
| 10 | Doctoral Degree (Research) \*□ |
| 10 | Higher Doctoral Degree \*□ |

\* ‘Higher Degrees’, also known as ‘postgraduate degrees/qualifications’

□ ‘Higher Degrees by Research’

\# Undergraduate Certificate is not allocated to a single AQF level but recognises completion of a short course equivalent to 0.5 Effective Full Time Study Load (EFTSL) toward an existing qualification at AQF levels 5-7.
