###### 3 Authority

This instrument is made under the following:

(a) the *Commonwealth Electoral Act 1918*;

(b) the *Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act 1984*.

###### 4 Simplified outline of this instrument

This instrument complements the *Commonwealth Electoral Act 1918* and the *Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act 1984* by:

(a) prescribing matters relating to the use and marking of electoral Rolls; and

(b) prescribing matters relating to enrolment and provisional enrolment; and

(c) providing electronically assisted voting methods to enable sight‑impaired persons and Antarctic electors to vote.

###### 5 Schedule 3

Each instrument that is specified in Schedule 3 to this instrument is amended or repealed as set out in the applicable items in that Schedule, and any other item in that Schedule has effect according to its terms.

###### 6 Definitions

Note: A number of expressions used in this instrument are defined in the Act or the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act, including the following:

(a) approved list;

(b) certified list of voters;

(c) Electoral Commissioner;

(d) prescribed authority;

(e) Roll.

In this instrument:

***Act*** means the *Commonwealth Electoral Act 1918*.

***authorised call centre*** means a call centre established in accordance with arrangements made by the Electoral Commissioner under subsection 21(1).

***call centre operator*** means a person who:

(a) works in an authorised call centre; and

(b) is a pre‑poll voting officer under subsection 4(1) of the Act or subsection 73(1) of the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act.

***electronically assisted vote*** means a vote cast by a registered electronically assisted voter using the electronically assisted voting methods set out in Part 4.

***electronically assisted voting*** means voting by an electronically assisted voting method set out in Part 4.

***medicare card*** has the same meaning as in Part VII of the *National Health Act 1953*.

***medicare number*** has the same meaning as in Part VII of the *National Health Act 1953*.

***referendum*** has the same meaning as in the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act.

***Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act*** means the *Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act 1984*.

***registered electronically assisted voter*** means:

(a) a sight‑impaired person; or

(b) an Antarctic elector;

who is registered by the Electoral Commissioner under subsection 22(3) for the purpose of voting by an electronically assisted vote.

Note: Sight‑impaired person has the meaning given by section 202AA of the Act.

***scrutineer*** means a person appointed as a scrutineer under subsection 27(1) or (2).

### Part 2—Electoral roll matters

###### 7 Simplified outline of this Part

This Part prescribes matters in relation to electoral Rolls, including by providing that certain persons are ***prescribed authorities*** who may be given, and use, electoral Roll information for prescribed purposes.

###### 8 Prescribed authorities

For the purposes of the definition of ***prescribed authority*** in subsection 4(1) of the Act, the Agencies and authorities of the Commonwealth mentioned in the column headed “Agency or authority” of the table in clause 1 of Schedule 1 to this instrument are specified.

Note: The table also prescribes permitted purposes for which a prescribed authority may use certain information (see section 11).

###### 9 Arrangements with States—form of Roll

For the purposes of paragraph 84(2)(a) of the Act, the prescribed manner to indicate that a person is not enrolled as a Commonwealth elector is to put a circle (o) before the person’s name on the Roll.

###### 10 Authorisation of provision of information to a prescribed authority

For the purposes of item 4 of the table in subsection 90B(4) of the Act, the provision to a prescribed authority of the information mentioned in that item is authorised.

###### 11 Permitted purposes for use of information—prescribed authorities

For the purposes of paragraph 91A(2AA)(b) of the Act, the purposes mentioned in an item of the table in clause 1 of Schedule 1 to this instrument are permitted purposes for the prescribed authority mentioned in the item.

Note: See also section 8 (prescribed authorities).

###### 12 Permitted purposes for use of information—Commonwealth funded blood service

For the purposes of paragraph 91A(2A)(c) of the Act, the following purposes are prescribed for a blood service funded by the Commonwealth:

(a) contacting any blood donor who has tested positive for a blood‑borne infection;

(b) contacting any person who has received a transfusion of blood donated by a person mentioned in paragraph (a) of this section;

(c) contacting any donor whose blood was transfused to a person who has since tested positive for a blood‑borne infection;

(d) contacting any donor whose blood was transfused to a person who has had a suspected adverse reaction to the blood.

###### 13 Permitted purpose for use of information—Australian Consortium for Social and Political Research Incorporated

For the purposes of paragraph 91A(2A)(c) of the Act, the purpose of inviting persons to participate in the Australian Survey of Social Attitudes is prescribed for the Australian Consortium for Social and Political Research Incorporated.

###### 14 Permitted purposes for use of information—other persons or organisations

For the purposes of paragraph 91A(2A)(c) of the Act, the following purposes are prescribed for a person or organisation that conducts medical research or provides a health screening program:

(a) the conduct of medical research in accordance with guidelines issued under section 95 of the *Privacy Act 1988*;

(b) the provision of a population‑based health screening program funded (in whole or in part) by the Commonwealth.

### Part 3—Enrolment matters

###### 15 Simplified outline of this Part

This Part provides for matters related to enrolment and provisional enrolment for the purposes of Part VIII and section 382 of the Act.

###### 16 Additional evidence of identity requirements

For the purposes of paragraph 98AA(2)(d) of the Act, the following kinds of evidence of a person’s identity are prescribed:

(a) the evidence number included in a notice given to the person under section 37 of the *Australian Citizenship Act 2007*;

(b) if the person is covered by a medicare card—the person’s medicare number.

###### 17 Provisional enrolment by applicant for citizenship—prescribed electors

For the purposes of subparagraph 99B(4)(b)(i) of the Act and subparagraph 38(4)(b)(i) of the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act, the classes of electors mentioned in the table in clause 1 of Schedule 2 to this instrument are prescribed.

###### 18 Enrolment‑related claims or notices do not have to be signed in certain circumstances

For the purposes of subsection 382(7) of the Act, the requirement is that the person include on the claim or notice:

(a) the person’s date of birth; and

(b) one of the following:

(i) if the person holds a driver’s licence issued under the law of a State or Territory, or a law in force in Norfolk Island—the number of that driver’s licence;

(ii) if the person holds an Australian passport—the number of that Australian passport;

(iii) the evidence number included in a notice given to the person under section 37 of the *Australian Citizenship Act 2007*;

(iv) if the person is covered by a medicare card—the person’s medicare number.

### Part 4—Electronically assisted voting methods

###### 19 Simplified outline of this Part

Sight‑impaired persons and Antarctic electors may register for, and vote using, electronically assisted voting methods.

The Electoral Commissioner must arrange for the establishment of authorised call centres for the purposes of the registration of, and voting by, such voters.

The Electoral Commissioner must determine the procedures to be followed by a call centre operator at an authorised call centre.

Call centre operators must follow those procedures to ensure that a person who claims to be a registered electronically assisted voter entitled to vote in a particular election or referendum is, in fact, that voter.

A candidate for an election, or a person who may appoint scrutineers in relation to a referendum, may appoint scrutineers to attend an authorised call centre to monitor electronically assisted voting.

###### 20 Purpose of Part

This Part provides for electronically assisted voting methods for the purposes of:

(a) Part XVB of the Act; and

(b) Part IVB of the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act.

###### 21 Authorised call centres

(1) The Electoral Commissioner must make arrangements for the establishment of one or more call centres for the purposes of enabling sight‑impaired persons or Antarctic electors to:

(a) register for the purpose of voting by an electronically assisted vote; or

(b) vote by an electronically assisted vote.

(2) The Electoral Commissioner may approve forms and other documents to be used by call centre operators and other officers for the purposes of:

(a) registering a sight‑impaired person or Antarctic elector as eligible to vote by an electronically assisted vote; and

(b) assisting a registered electronically assisted voter to vote by an electronically assisted vote.

Note 1: Sight‑impaired person has the meaning given by section 202AA of the Act.

Note 2: Examples of other documents that may be approved are statements and responses that allow call centre operators to explain to a sight‑impaired person or Antarctic elector how to register or vote.

###### 22 Registration

(1) The Electoral Commissioner must determine, in writing:

(a) the days on which and times when a person may apply to be registered by the Electoral Commissioner as a registered electronically assisted voter; and

(b) procedures for assessing whether a person may be registered by the Electoral Commissioner as a registered electronically assisted voter.

Note: The procedures may include requirements for the person to:

(a) be on an approved list of voters or a certified list of voters for a particular Division; and

(b) affirm that the person is sight‑impaired or an Antarctic elector.

(2) A sight‑impaired person or Antarctic elector who wishes to apply to be registered by the Electoral Commissioner as a registered electronically assisted voter must:

(a) contact an authorised call centre on a day and time determined under paragraph (1)(a); and

(b) comply with the procedures determined under paragraph (1)(b).

(3) The Electoral Commissioner must:

(a) register a person who complies with subsection (2) as a registered electronically assisted voter; and

(b) make and keep a register for that purpose.

###### 23 Electoral Commissioner to make arrangements relating to electronically assisted voting

The Electoral Commissioner:

(a) must determine, in writing, the days on which and times when electronically assisted voting is to be available for an election or referendum; and

(b) must determine, in writing, procedures for a call centre operator to use in assessing whether a person is a registered electronically assisted voter; and

(c) may give directions to officers in relation to requests for voting by an electronically assisted vote.

Note: For paragraph (b), the procedures may, for example, include a requirement that the call centre operator must, if not satisfied that a person is the registered electronically assisted voter whose name the person has used, ask the person one or more questions about the voter using information provided on an approved list of voters or a certified list of voters that includes the name of the voter.

###### 24 Voting by electronically assisted vote

Elections and referendums at which electronically assisted voting is available

(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), a registered electronically assisted voter is entitled to vote by an electronically assisted vote at any of the following in which the voter is otherwise entitled to vote:

(a) a general election;

(b) a Senate election;

(c) a by‑election;

(d) a referendum.

Note 1: Subsection 4(1) of the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act deals with a person’s entitlement to vote at a referendum.

Note 2: For paragraph (c), ***by‑election*** is defined in section 202AA of the Act.

When registered electronically assisted voter may vote by electronically assisted vote

(2) Subject to subsection (3), a person who is a registered electronically assisted voter may vote by an electronically assisted vote at an election or referendum only if:

(a) the person calls an authorised call centre on a day on which and at a time when electronically assisted voting for the election or referendum is available; and

(b) the person tells a call centre operator that the person wishes to vote in the election or referendum by way of an electronically assisted vote; and

(c) the call centre operator is satisfied, in accordance with the procedures determined under paragraph 23(b), that the person is a registered electronically assisted voter; and

(d) the person’s name is on an approved list of voters or a certified list of voters; and

(e) if the election is for a member of the House of Representatives for a Division—the list mentioned in paragraph (d) is for the Division; and

(f) if the election is a Senate election for a State or Territory—the list mentioned in paragraph (d) is for a Division that is included in the State or Territory.

Persons ineligible for electronically assisted voting

(3) A person is not entitled to vote by an electronically assisted vote at an election or referendum if:

(a) the person is not a registered electronically assisted voter; or

(b) the person refuses to fully answer a question asked by a call centre operator in accordance with the procedures determined under paragraph 23(b); or

(c) the person answers a question asked in accordance with subsection 25(3) in a way that indicates the person has already voted in the election or referendum; or

(d) the person has already voted in the election or referendum; or

(e) the person is provisionally enrolled; or

(f) on the basis of the procedures mentioned in paragraph 23(b), a call centre operator is not satisfied that the person is the voter whose name the person has used; or

(g) the person does not otherwise comply with the procedures for:

(i) assessing whether the person is a registered electronically assisted voter; or

(ii) voting by an electronically assisted vote.

###### 25 Enabling registered electronically assisted voter to vote

(1) The Electoral Commissioner must determine, in writing, procedures for enabling a registered electronically assisted voter to vote by an electronically assisted vote.

Example 1: The procedures may require a call centre operator to ensure that the voter:

(a) receives the same information (in the same order), and has the same voting options, as would appear in the ballot paper for the election that the voter would be given if the voter were voting under Part XVI of the Act; and

(b) is able to indicate the voter’s vote in a way that, if the voter were marking a ballot paper, would satisfy the requirements of section 239 or 240 of the Act.

Example 2: The procedures may permit a voter’s ballot paper (or papers) to be checked to ensure that they have been marked in accordance with the voter’s instructions.

(2) A call centre operator who is satisfied that a registered electronically assisted voter is entitled to vote by an electronically assisted vote must assist the voter in accordance with the procedures determined under subsection (1).

(3) In determining whether a registered electronically assisted voter is entitled to vote by an electronically assisted vote, the call centre operator must ask the voter whether the voter has voted before in the election or referendum (as the case requires).

(4) The voter may tell a call centre operator how the registered electronically assisted voter wants the ballot paper (or ballot papers) to be marked.

(5) If the registered electronically assisted voter tells a call centre operator how the voter wants the ballot paper (or papers) to be marked, the call centre operator, or another call centre operator permitted to do so by the procedures determined under subsection (1), must:

(a) initial the voter’s ballot paper (or papers) on the top front of each ballot paper; and

(b) mark the voter’s ballot paper (or papers) in accordance with the voter’s instructions; and

(c) read the voter’s voting preferences back to the voter; and

(d) put the voter’s ballot paper (or papers) in an envelope marked with the name of the voter’s Division; and

(e) place the envelope in a ballot‑box used at the authorised call centre for electronically assisted voting.

###### 26 Requirements relating to ballot‑boxes

(1) For an election for which electronically assisted voting is available, the requirements in relation to ballot‑boxes in Subdivision C of Division 3 of Part XVA of the Act are taken to apply to ballot‑boxes used at an authorised call centre for electronically assisted voting as if the electronically assisted voting were pre‑poll ordinary voting, other than:

(a) subsection 200DP(2) of the Act, to the extent necessary to comply with paragraph 30(1)(a) of this instrument; and

(b) section 200DR of the Act.

(2) For a referendum for which electronically assisted voting is available, the requirements in relation to ballot‑boxes in Subdivision C of Division 3 of Part IVA of the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act are taken to apply to ballot‑boxes used at an authorised call centre for electronically assisted voting as if the electronically assisted voting were pre‑poll ordinary voting, other than:

(a) subsection 73CP(2) of that Act, to the extent necessary to comply with paragraph 30(1)(a) of this instrument; and

(b) section 73CR of that Act.

###### 27 Scrutineers at authorised call centres

Appointment of scrutineers

(1) A candidate in an election for which electronically assisted voting is available may appoint one or more persons as a scrutineer for the candidate in relation to an authorised call centre.

(2) A person (the ***appointer***) who may appoint persons to act as scrutineers under section 27 of the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act for a referendum for which electronically assisted voting is available may appoint one or more persons as a scrutineer for the appointer in relation to an authorised call centre.

(3) The appointment of a scrutineer in relation to an authorised call centre must be made by notice, in writing:

(a) addressed to the Returning Officer; and

(b) signed by the candidate or the appointer (as the case requires); and

(c) stating the scrutineer’s name and address.

Role of scrutineer

(4) A scrutineer in relation to an authorised call centre may attend the authorised call centre for the purpose of monitoring the performance of the duties of call centre operators.

(5) No more than one scrutineer for a candidate or appointer in relation to an authorised call centre may attend the authorised call centre for the purposes mentioned in subsection (4) at any given time.

Undertaking by scrutineers

(6) A scrutineer appointed under subsection (1) must not attend an authorised call centre for the purpose mentioned in subsection (4) unless the scrutineer has:

(a) signed an undertaking in the approved form for subsection 202A(3) of the Act; and

(b) submitted the undertaking to the Electoral Commission.

(7) A scrutineer appointed under subsection (2) must not attend an authorised call centre for the purpose mentioned in subsection (4) unless the scrutineer has:

(a) signed an undertaking in the approved form for subsection 16A(3) of the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act; and

(b) submitted the undertaking to the Electoral Commission.

###### 28 Offences—interference and communication by scrutineers

(1) A scrutineer commits an offence if:

(a) the scrutineer attends an authorised call centre; and

(b) the scrutineer interferes with the performance of the duties of a call centre operator or an Assistant Returning Officer.

Penalty: 30 penalty units.

(2) A scrutineer commits an offence if:

(a) the scrutineer attends an authorised call centre; and

(b) the scrutineer communicates with a person in the authorised call centre; and

(c) the communication is not reasonably necessary for the discharge of the scrutineer’s functions.

Penalty: 30 penalty units.

###### 29 Recording of names of electronically assisted voters

(1) The Electoral Commissioner must make a record of registered electronically assisted voters who cast an electronically assisted vote.

(2) The record of a registered electronically assisted voter who casts an electronically assisted vote must be:

(a) a mark on an approved list of voters or a certified list of voters beside the voter’s name; or

(b) a record on a document other than an approved list of voters or a certified list of voters which states:

(i) the voter’s name (whether or not it also states the voter’s address or date of birth); and

(ii) the Division in which the voter is enrolled to vote.

(3) If the record is in the form mentioned in paragraph (2)(b):

(a) it must be forwarded to a Divisional Returning Officer for the Division in which the registered electronically assisted voter is enrolled to vote; and

(b) the Divisional Returning Officer must place a mark beside the voter’s name on an approved list of voters or a certified list of voters.

###### 30 Sorting and forwarding of ballot papers

(1) As soon as reasonably possible after the close of the poll for all Divisions, an Assistant Returning Officer must:

(a) open each ballot‑box mentioned in paragraph 25(5)(e); and

(b) sort the envelopes, unopened, into bundles corresponding to Divisions.

(2) An Assistant Returning Officer must forward each bundle to the Divisional Returning Officer for the corresponding Division for the conduct of a scrutiny.

(3) A person commits an offence of strict liability if the person:

(a) is not an Assistant Returning Officer, or a person performing tasks under the direction of an Assistant Returning Officer; and

(b) does something mentioned in subsection (1) or (2).

Penalty: 5 penalty units.

###### 31 Scrutiny of ballot papers

(1) The Divisional Returning Officer for a Division must conduct the scrutiny of the ballot papers for that Division forwarded by the Assistant Returning Officer under subsection 30(2).

(2) For a general election, Senate election or by‑election, the procedures in Part XVIII of the Act apply to the scrutiny with the modifications necessary to ensure that:

(a) no preliminary scrutiny mentioned in section 266 of the Act is to be conducted; and

(b) the electronically assisted vote is taken to be a pre‑poll ordinary vote; and

(c) it is irrelevant that the registered electronically assisted voter did not complete the ballot paper personally; and

(d) it is irrelevant that the vote can be identified as being cast by a sight‑impaired person or Antarctic elector.

(3) For a referendum, the procedures in Part VI of the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act apply to the scrutiny with the modifications necessary to ensure that:

(a) no preliminary scrutiny mentioned in section 89A of that Act is to be conducted; and

(b) the electronically assisted vote is taken to be a pre‑poll ordinary vote; and

(c) it is irrelevant that the registered electronically assisted voter did not complete the ballot paper personally; and

(d) it is irrelevant that the vote can be identified as being cast by a sight‑impaired person or Antarctic elector.

(4) A person commits an offence of strict liability if the person:

(a) is not the Divisional Returning Officer, or a person performing tasks under the direction of the Divisional Returning Officer; and

(b) conducts the scrutiny.

Penalty: 5 penalty units.

###### 32 Offences related to electronically assisted voting

(1) A person commits an offence if the person:

(a) interferes with a registered electronically assisted voter while the voter votes by an electronically assisted vote; or

(b) communicates with such a voter, while the voter is voting by an electronically assisted vote, about the voter’s vote; or

(c) does anything to find out how a voter who votes by an electronically assisted vote voted.

Penalty: 10 penalty units.

Defences

(2) Subsection (1) does not apply if the registered electronically assisted voter:

(a) requests the person’s assistance to vote by an electronically assisted vote; and

(b) consents to the conduct or communication.

Note: A defendant bears an evidential burden in relation to the matter in subsection (2): see subsection 13.3(3) of the *Criminal Code*.

###### 33 Protection of electronic voting hardware or software

(1) A person commits an offence if:

(a) the person destroys or interferes with:

(i) a computer program; or

(ii) a data file; or

(iii) an electronic device; and

(b) the program, file or device is used, or intended to be used, for or in connection with electronically assisted voting.

Penalty: 50 penalty units.

(2) Subsection (1) does not apply if the person is an officer acting in the course of the person’s duties as an officer.

Note: A defendant bears an evidential burden in relation to the matter in subsection (2): see subsection 13.3(3) of the *Criminal Code*.

### Part 5—Application and transitional matters

#### Division 1—Provisions relating to the commencement of this instrument

###### 34 Applications made before commencement

Section 12 of, and Schedule 2 to, the *Electoral and Referendum Regulation 2016*, as in force immediately before the commencement of this Part, continue to apply in relation to an application made under subsection 99B(1) of the Act or subsection 38(1) of the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act before that commencement, if the application was not finally determined before that commencement.

###### 35 Electronically assisted voting

If writs are issued before the commencement of this Part for an election to be held after the commencement of this Part, Division 1 of Part 4 of the *Electoral and Referendum Regulation 2016*, as in force immediately before the commencement of this Part, applies in relation to electronically assisted voting for the election.

###### 36 Transitional arrangements—prescribed authorities and permitted purposes

Transitional—agencies and authorities no longer specified for the definition of **prescribed authority**

(1) Subsection (2) applies if:

(a) immediately before the commencement of this Part, an agency or authority was specified in the table in clause 1 of Schedule 1 to the *Electoral and Referendum Regulation 2016*; and

(b) before that commencement, the Electoral Commission gave the agency or authority information under subsection 90B(4) of the Act for one or more purposes (the ***permitted purposes***) of the agency or authority mentioned in that table; and

(c) immediately after that commencement, the agency or authority is not specified in the table in clause 1 of Schedule 1 to this instrument.

(2) During the transition time, the agency or authority may continue to use the information for the permitted purposes as if it were a prescribed authority mentioned in paragraph 91A(2AA)(b) of the Act.

Transitional—agency or authority with changed permitted purposes

(3) Subsection (4) applies in relation to an agency or authority if:

(a) immediately before the commencement of this Part, the *Electoral and Referendum Regulation 2016* provided one or more permitted purposes in relation to the agency or authority for the purposes of paragraph 91A(2AA)(b) or 91A(2A)(c) of the Act; and

(b) immediately after the commencement of this Part, this instrument provided one or more different permitted purposes in relation to the agency or authority.

(4) Despite the repeal of the *Electoral and Referendum Regulation 2016*, during the transition time, the permitted purposes in relation to the agency or authority for any information provided to the agency or authority by the Electoral Commission under section 90B of the Act before the commencement of this Part includes any permitted purposes mentioned in paragraph (3)(a).

Meaning of **transition time**

(5) For the purposes of this section, the ***transition time*** is the period of 6 months beginning on the day this Part commences.
