CASA 40/26 — Operation of RPA Within 3 Nautical Miles of a Controlled Aerodrome (CASA-Verified App) Instrument 2026

1 Name

This instrument is the *CASA 40/26* — *Operation of RPA Within 3 Nautical Miles of a Controlled Aerodrome (CASA-Verified App) Instrument 2026*.

2 Duration

This instrument:

(a) commences on the day after it is registered; and

(b) is repealed at the end of 20 September 2029.

*Note* For regulation 11.250 of CASR, the directions under section 5 cease to be in force at the end of 20 September 2029.

3 Definitions

*Note* In this instrument, certain terms and expressions have the same meaning as they have in the *Civil Aviation Act 1988* and the regulations. These include the terms, ***aerial work operation***, ***AGL***, ***air traffic control***, ***controlled aerodrome***, ***large RPA***, ***military aerodrome***, ***night***, ***operated within the visual line of sight***, ***Part 101 Manual of Standards***, ***remote pilot licence***, ***RPA***, ***RPAS***, ***small RPA*** and ***very small RPA***.

In this instrument:

***ADS-B*** means automatic dependent surveillance – broadcast.

***approved area*** has the meaning given by regulation 101.236 of CASR.

***ATC*** means air traffic control.

***CASA 20/25*** means the *CASA 20/25 – Operation of Certain Unmanned Aircraft and Rockets – Directions Instrument 2025*.

***CASA RPAS digital platform*** means the CASA digital platform, accessible on the internet by an app developer, which provides data in relation to aeronautical information relevant to RPA operations within 3 nautical miles of a relevant controlled aerodrome.

***documented practices and procedures***, of an RPA operator, has the meaning given by subsection 1.04(2) of the Part 101 Manual of Standards.

***FIMS*** (short for ***flight information management system***) means the Airservices Australia digital platform, accessible on the internet by an app developer, which provides data in relation to aeronautical information relevant to RPA operations within 3 nautical miles of a relevant controlled aerodrome.

***measurement point*** has the meaning given by subsection 1.04(2) of the Part 101 Manual of Standards.

***operating area*** means the segment of airspace, described on a relevant app, defined by reference to horizontal and vertical limits within a relevant approved area, in which an RPA may be operated under subsection 4.03(7) of the Part 101 Manual of Standards.

***populous area*** has the same meaning as in regulation 101.025 of CASR.

***relevant app*** means an app that is:

(a) connected to the CASA RPAS digital platform or FIMS; and

(b) listed as a verified app on the CASA website, for the purposes of this instrument.

*Note* The relevant CASA website address is &lt;https://www.casa.gov.au/drones&gt;.

***relevant approved area*** means an area approved under section 4.

***relevant controlled aerodrome*** means a controlled aerodrome that is not a military aerodrome.

***remote pilot*** means a person who is:

(a) the holder of a remote pilot licence, or is taken to hold a remote pilot licence under subregulation 202.461(3) of CASR; and

(b) an RPA operator or a member of an RPA operator’s personnel.

***RPA operator*** means a person who is certified as an RPA operator under regulation 101.335 of CASR.

4 Approval

(1) On application by Airservices Australia, ARN 202210, under subregulation 101.030(1) of CASR, CASA approves each area described in subsection (2) as an area for the operation of an RPA.

(2) For subsection (1), the area consists of any areas and airspace that are:

(a) below 400 ft AGL; and

(b) within 3 nautical miles, in any direction, from the measurement point of any runway of a relevant controlled aerodrome.

*Note 1* The areas and airspace described in subsection (2) accord with the areas and airspace described in paragraph (a) of the definition of the term, ***no-fly zone of a controlled aerodrome*** in section 4.02 of the Part 101 Manual of Standards.

*Note 2* A remote pilot may, under subsection 4.03(7) of the Part 101 Manual of Standards, fly an RPA in a relevant approved area in accordance with the approval under this section, including the condition of the approval stated in subsection (3).

(3) The approval under subsection (1) is subject to the condition that a remote pilot who uses the approval, in relation to the operation of an RPA in a relevant approved area, must comply with the directions under section 5.

5 Directions

(1) The remote pilot (the ***pilot***) must only operate the RPA, in the RPA operation, in an operating area.

(2) The pilot must:

(a) if the pilot is an RPA operator — be registered, using a relevant app, to be authorised to operate the RPA in the operating area during a period notified to the pilot on the app; or

(b) if the pilot is a member of an RPA operator’s personnel — ensure that the operator has registered, using a relevant app, to be authorised to operate the RPA in the operating area during a period notified to the operator on the app.

(3) The pilot must, during the RPA operation, comply with any operating limitations, relevant to the operation, which are notified to the pilot or RPA operator, as applicable, on the relevant app in relation to the registration mentioned in subsection (2).

(4) The pilot must operate a very small RPA, or small RPA, in the RPA operation.

(5) The pilot must conduct the RPA operation only as an aerial work operation.

(6) Subsection (7) applies if the pilot may, under the approval under section 5 of *CASA 01/17 – Approval – operation of RPA at night*, operate an RPA, other than a large RPA, at night.

*Note* As far as is relevant, under subregulation 101.095(1) of CASR, a person may operate an unmanned aircraft at night only if permitted by another provision of Part 101 of CASR, or in accordance with an ATC direction. Under subregulation 101.095(1A) of CASR, subregulation (1) does not apply if the person holds an approval under regulation 101.029 of CASR for the purposes of subregulation (1A).

(7) Despite the approval, the pilot must not, during the RPA operation, operate the RPA at night.

(8) The pilot must, during the RPA operation:

(a) subject to subsection (9), take reasonable steps to ensure that:

(i) reliable VHF coverage exists in the operating area, which would allow the pilot to communicate with ATC; and

(ii) a listening watch is maintained on the relevant ATC frequency for the operating area, from 15 minutes before the start of a flight of the RPA until the RPA has landed at the end of the flight; and

(b) take reasonable steps to ensure that reliable mobile phone coverage exists in the operating area; and

(c) comply with any ATC direction in relation to the operation.

*Examples* For paragraph (c), the ATC direction could be to temporarily move the location of the operation within the operating area or temporarily cease the operation.

(9) Paragraph (8)(a) does not apply if the pilot is operating the RPA within 100 metres laterally from, and below, an obstacle, other than an aircraft.

(10) Subsection (11) applies if the pilot:

(a) holds an approval, under regulation 101.029 of CASR, to operate an RPA beyond the pilot’s visual line of sight, for the purposes of subregulation 101.073(2) of CASR; or

*Note* Under subregulation 101.073(1) of CASR, a person commits an offence of strict liability if the person operates an unmanned aircraft, and the aircraft is not operated within the person’s visual line of sight.

(b) may, under an exemption under section 4 of the *CASA EX63/26 — Remotely Piloted Aircraft Operations Beyond Visual Line of Sight Exemption 2026*, operate an RPA beyond the pilot’s visual line of sight; or

(c) meets the requirements stated in subregulation 101.300(4) of CASR, allowing the pilot to operate an RPA beyond the pilot’s visual line of sight.

*Note* Under subregulation 101.300(4) of CASR, it is a condition of a remote pilot licence that an RPA must be operated within the visual line of sight of the licence holder unless the holder meets stated requirements.

(11) Despite the approval or exemption, or meeting the requirements, as applicable, the pilot must not, during the RPA operation, operate the RPA beyond the pilot’s visual line of sight.

(12) Subsection (13) applies if:

(a) the pilot holds an approval, under regulation 101.029 of CASR, to operate more than 1 RPA at a time, for the purposes of subregulation 101.300(5) of CASR; or

*Note* Under subregulation 101.300(5) of CASR, it is a condition of a remote pilot licence that the licence holder must not operate more than 1 RPA at a time unless the holder holds an approval under regulation 101.029 of CASR to operate more than 1 RPA at a time, and the conditions (if any) imposed on the approval are complied with.

(b) the pilot may under an authorisation (however it is called), or exemption, granted under CASR operate more than 1 RPA at a time, for the purposes of paragraph 8(2)(b) of CASA 20/25.

(13) Despite the approval, authorisation, or exemption, as applicable, the pilot must not, during the RPA operation, operate more than 1 RPA at a time.

(14) If during the RPA operation, the RPA leaves the operating area, whether or not under the pilot’s control, the pilot must notify ATC of the event immediately after the happening of the event.

(15) The pilot must not transmit on the relevant ATC frequency for the operating area, during the RPA operation, unless:

(a) directed by ATC to do so; or

(b) the pilot does so to give a notification to ATC under subsection (14).

(16) The pilot must, during the RPA operation, ensure the RPA is equipped and operated with an active fail-safe mode that will ensure, in the event of a data‑link loss between the RPA and its associated remote pilot station during a flight of the RPA, the RPA will:

(a) adjust its altitude to the minimum safe level, to provide obstacle clearance and the minimum potential for collision with other aircraft; and

(b) transit to a pre-defined safe landing area or flight termination area; and

(c) land or otherwise end the flight.

(17) Unless directed by ATC to do so, the pilot must not, during the RPA operation, activate:

(a) any transponder fitted to the RPA; or

(b) the transmission of ADS-B position information by the RPA.

(18) If there is an inconsistency between the relevant RPA operator’s documented practices and procedures and a direction under this section, the pilot must comply with the direction.

*Note 1* Subsection (18) applies despite the statement in the relevant RPA operator’s documented practices and procedures, which is required under paragraph 1.04(3)(f) of the Part 101 Manual of Standards to be included in the documented practices and procedures.

*Note 2* Under regulation 101.245 of CASR, the pilot must not operate the RPA within 30 metres of a person who is not directly associated with the RPA operation, unless subregulation 101.245(2), (3) or (5) of CASR applies in relation to the operation.

*Note 3* Under subregulation 101.280(2) of CASR, the pilot must not operate the RPA over a populous area at a height less than the height from which, if any of its components fails, it would be able to clear the area, unless subsection 5(3) of the *CASA EX45/24 — Operation of Remotely Piloted Aircraft Over Populous Area Exemption 2024* applies in relation to the RPA operation.

*Note 4* Under subsection 7(1) of CASA 20/25, the pilot is directed to ensure that the RPA is not operated in, or over, another person’s safety zone (as described in subsection 7(2) of CASA 20/25), unless the other person has duties essential to the control or navigation of the RPA, or paragraph 7(3)(a) or (b) of CASA 20/25 applies in relation to the RPA operation.

*Note 5* Under subsection 6(1) of CASA 20/25, the pilot is directed not to operate the RPA over an area where a fire, police, or other public safety or emergency, operation is being conducted, unless subsection 6(2) of CASA 20/25 applies in relation to the RPA operation.

6 Repeal of instrument number CASA 66/24

The *CASA 66/24* — *Operation of RPA Within 3 Nautical Miles of a Controlled Aerodrome (CASA-Verified Drone Safety App) Instrument 2024* is repealed.
