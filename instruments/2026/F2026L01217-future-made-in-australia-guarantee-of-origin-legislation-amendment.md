###### 9 Monitoring and measuring requirements—general

(1) A parameter is monitored or measured in accordance with this section if the parameter is monitored or measured using:

(a) the direct measurement method in subsection (2); or

(b) a method mentioned in subsection (3).

Direct measurement method

(2) For the purposes of paragraph (1)(a), the parameter must be monitored or measured:

(a) directly using calibrated measuring equipment; or

(b) by calculating the value of the parameter using one or more variables that are monitored or measured directly using calibrated measuring equipment.

Example: For the purposes of paragraph (b), calculating the mass of a gas in a storage tank by directly measuring the temperature and pressure of the gas using calibrated measuring equipment.

(3) For the purposes of paragraph (1)(b), a parameter may be monitored or measured in accordance with this section using:

(a) the invoice method in subsection (4) if the parameter is:

(i) mentioned in column 2 of an item in the table in Part 1 of Schedule 2; and

(ii) an input acquired because of a commercial transaction; or

(b) if the parameter is mentioned in column 2 of an item in the table in Part 2 of Schedule 2—the mapping tool method in subsection (5).

Invoice method

(4) For the purposes of paragraph (3)(a), the parameter must be:

(a) evidenced by invoices issued by the vendor of the input to which the parameter relates (***supplier invoices***); or

(b) if the parameter cannot be accounted for using supplier invoices—monitored or measured using a combination of:

(i) evidence provided by supplier invoices; and

(ii) direct measurement using the method in subsection (2) of related variables to enable the accurate calculation of the part not accounted for using supplier invoices.

Example: The parameter ***Qwater,x,b*** in subsection 17(1) is the quantity of water used for a production pathway. If the water has been acquired because of a commercial transaction, subsection (4) provides that the quantity may be evidenced using invoices issued by the vendor of the water. If a small part of the invoiced amount has not been used for the production pathway for the batch, paragraph (4)(b) provides that the amount of unused water may be directly measured using calibrated measuring equipment and then deducted from the invoiced amount to calculate the amount of water used for the batch. Measuring the smaller portion of water allowed the use of a flow meter that caters to low flows rather than large flows.

Mapping tool method

(5) For the purposes of paragraph (3)(b), a distance required to be monitored or measured for the purposes of the parameter must be estimated using a tool designed to estimate the distance of a route taken by a vehicle between 2 or more places.

Example: The National Key Freight Routes Map application published by the Department of Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development, Communication, Sport and the Arts may be used to estimate the distance of key road and rail routes within Australia.

Note: The National Key Freight Routes Map could in 2026 be viewed on the Department of Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development, Communication, Sport and the Arts’ website (https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/).

(6) In this section:

***calibrated measuring equipment*** means monitoring or measuring equipment calibrated:

(a) to monitor or measure one or more physical properties in accordance with:

(i) a measurement requirement provided for under the *National Measurement Act 1960*, or any instrument made under that Act, for that equipment; or

(ii) a measurement requirement under an equivalent standard for that physical property (however described); and

(b) if the monitoring or measuring equipment cannot be calibrated in accordance with paragraph (a)—by an accredited technician at intervals, and using methods, that are in accordance with the manufacturer’s specifications.

Examples: In subparagraph (a)(ii), physical properties include weight, length, mass, temperature, pressure and volume.

###### 9A General principles for formulas and methods

(1) This section applies if:

(a) this instrument provides alternative methods or formulas for a person to comply with a requirement; and

(b) it is reasonably practicable for the person to use more than one of the methods or formulas.

Example: To determine the emissions associated with the use of a gas the person has purchased from an input supplier, the person has a choice of calculating the volume of gas by direct measurement or by invoice method. The person suspects that the information provided in the invoice is inaccurate and already has calibrated measuring equipment capable of directly measuring the volume of gas for the purposes of calculating emissions.

(2) The person must use the formula or method, the use of which would best apply the following principles:

(a) transparency—information used to work out the formula or method is documented and verifiable;

(b) comparability—the formula or method is one that:

(i) would be performed by persons in corresponding, or substantially corresponding industries; and

(ii) best conforms to what is commonly accepted as standard for the production pathway, including Australian Standards, or international standards;

(c) integrity—information used to work out the formula or method is credible and traceable, including where that information has been obtained from another person;

(d) accuracy—the formula or method is worked out in a way that minimises uncertainties by employing accurate methods that comply with the latest available scientific evidence.

###### 14A NGER Method 2 and 3 sampling and analysis

NGER Method 2 sampling

(1) If a provision of this instrument provides for a parameter to be determined in accordance with ***NGER Method 2 sampling***, sampling and analysis must be done in accordance with the method set out in the following provisions of the NGER Measurement Determination:

(a) subsection 2.10(1) of that Subdivision does not apply; and

###### 80 Iron ore production pathway

(1) The ***iron ore production pathway*** is a production pathway for iron ore.

(2) Subject to subsection (4), the ***iron ore production module*** is the process of extracting iron ore from the earth by mining (the ***mining process***).

(3) Without limiting subsection (2), the iron ore production module includes any of the following processes:

(a) preparing the ground for excavation, including by removing overburden;

(b) dewatering;

(c) breaking up the iron ore and waste rock;

(e) blending the iron ore with externally sourced iron ore.

(a) electricity supply and use for the pathway;

(b) fuel supply and combustion for the pathway;

(c) feedstock supply for the pathway;

(d) water supply for the pathway;

(e) synthetic gas use for the pathway;

(f) explosives supply for the pathway.

(1) Subject to subsection (3), the ***iron ore processing module*** is the process of physically transforming and improving the quality of the iron ore so that the product is ready to be delivered or consumed.

(a) blending externally sourced iron ore with the product;

(b) transporting the iron ore for the purposes of processing the iron ore, including transporting the ore:

(i) to a location where the ore is processed; and

(ii) to the production gate;

###### 90 Delivery modules

(1) Each of the following methods for storing or transporting iron ore is a ***delivery module*** for iron ore:

## Schedule 6—Phase-in remission

### Part 6.1—Eligibility for remission

###### 1.1 General eligibility for remission

(1) Subject to subclause (2), a person is eligible for remission of a cost-recovery charge if the charge is payable for one of the following:

(a) the registration of a production profile that specifies an eligible production pathway;

(b) the registration of a profile, the registration of which the Regulator is satisfied is to deliver or consume a product produced using an eligible production pathway;

(c) the registration of a certificate created by the holder of a production profile that specifies an eligible production pathway;

(d) the addition of consumption information to a certificate created by the holder of a production profile that specifies an eligible production pathway;

(e) a charge under the Charges Act payable in relation to an eligible profile.

(2) A charge payable in relation to a delivery profile or consumption profile is only eligible for remission if the Regulator is satisfied that product could reasonably pass from the production gate specified in a production profile that specifies an eligible production pathway to a delivery gate of the delivery profile or consumption profile.

(3) In this clause:

***eligible production pathway*** means any one of the following production pathways determined for a product under section 29 of the Act:

(a) biogas by anaerobic digestion production pathway;

(b) biomethane by biogas upgrading production pathway;

(c) hydrogen by electrolysis production pathway;

(d) hydrogen by gas reforming production pathway;

(e) hydrogen by solid gasification and pyrolysis production pathway;

(f) aluminium production pathway;

(g) iron ore production pathway.

***eligible profile*** means the following:

(a) a production profile that specifies an eligible production pathway;

(b) a delivery profile, the holder of which is authorised under section 54 of the Act to add post-production information to a certificate that was created by the holder of a production profile that specifies an eligible production pathway;

(c) a consumption profile, the holder of which is authorised under section 57 of the Act to add post-production information to a certificate that was created by the holder of a production profile that specifies an eligible production pathway.
