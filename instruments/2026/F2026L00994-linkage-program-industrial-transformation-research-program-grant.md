Contents

Linkage Program processes

The Linkage Program is designed to achieve the Australian Government’s objectives for research and innovation

This grant opportunity sits within the Linkage Program under the National Competitive Grants Program (NCGP), which contributes to the ARC’s Outcome 1: growth of knowledge and innovation through managing research funding schemes, measuring research excellence and providing advice.

The ARC works with stakeholders to plan and design the grant program according to the *Commonwealth Grants Rules and Principles 2024 (CGRPs).*



The grant opportunity opens

We (the ARC) publish the grant opportunity guidelines and advertise on GrantConnect.



You (the Administering Organisation) complete and submit an application

You work with Partner Organisations to complete an application form on the ARC’s Research Management System (RMS), and address all of the eligibility requirements, the National Interest Test and the assessment criteria to be considered for a grant.



We manage the assessment of all applications

We manage the assessment of applications against eligibility criteria and assessment criteria.

Your application will be assigned to Detailed Assessors to undertake in-depth assessments.

Selection Advisory Committee (SAC) members will then assess Your application, consider the Detailed Assessors’ ratings and comments, and assign a final score.

The ARC may seek and consider additional information, including information relating to research security and research integrity, and from third party sources including other Commonwealth agencies, to provide advice to the decision maker in relation to Your application.



We make grant recommendations

The SAC considers applications and makes recommendations to the ARC Board on which are to be funded, including the level and duration of funding.

The ARC Board then makes an assessment and recommendation to the Minister. In making recommendations to the Minister, the ARC Board considers the SAC’s advice, compliance with the eligibility criteria and grant application process, alignment with Australian Government priorities and other advice relevant to the application. The ARC Board may also consider advice from other Commonwealth agencies and make its own independent enquiries.

If the Minister decides not to fund a project for reasons relevant to security, defence or international relations of Australia, the Minister must notify the ARC Board, advise the Parliamentary Joint Committee on Intelligence and Security and table a statement in Parliament as required under the ARC Act.



Grant decisions are made

The Minister decides which applications are to receive funding, including the level and duration of funding.



We notify You of the outcome

We advise You if Your application was successful or unsuccessful through RMS.



We complete assurance checks and enter into a grant agreement with You

If You are successful, You and all named participants must complete assurance and compliance checks in RMS. Completion of these checks is required before the grant offer can be accepted and a grant agreement entered into through RMS.



Delivery of the grant

You undertake the grant activity as set out in Your grant agreement.\
We manage the grant, by working with you, monitoring Your progress and making payments.



Evaluation of the grant opportunity

We evaluate Your specific grant activity and the individual grant opportunity as a whole.\
We base this on information You provide to Us through Your reports and additional information that we collect from various sources.

About the grant program

2.1. The ARC administers funding of excellent pure basic, strategic basic and applied research in all disciplines under the National Competitive Grants Program (NCGP), excluding medical research. The ARC does not fund experimental development.

2.2. The ARC considers the alignment of projects funded under the NCGP with Australian Government priorities, including the National Science and Research Priorities:

Priority 1: Transitioning to a net zero future

Priority 2: Supporting healthy and thriving communities

Priority 3: Elevating Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge systems

Priority 4: Protecting and restoring Australia’s environment

Priority 5: Building a secure and resilient nation.

2.3. Information about the Linkage Program is available on the ARC website.

About the Industrial Transformation Research Program grant opportunities

2.4. The Industrial Transformation Research Program (ITRP) encourages and supports university-based researchers and industry to work together to address a range of strategic government priorities to transform Australian industries.

2.5. The intended outcome of ITRP is to increase Australia’s research and innovation capacity to generate new knowledge and result in the development of new technologies, products and ideas, the creation of jobs, economic growth and an enhanced quality of life in Australia.

2.6. The current Industrial Transformation Priorities identified by Us align with the priority funding areas under the National Reconstruction Fund (NRF):

renewables and low emissions technologies

medical science\*

transport

value-add in the agriculture, forestry and fisheries sectors

value-add in resources

defence capability

enabling capabilities.

Further detail about the NRF priorities can be found at the Federal Register of Legislation.

\* Applications which address the ‘Medical Science’ industrial transformation priority must take the ARC *Medical Research Policy* (2025 version) into consideration.

2.7. There are two schemes under the ITRP referred to in these grant opportunity guidelines:

Industrial Transformation Research Hubs (Part A); and

Industrial Transformation Training Centres (Part B).

2.8. The grant commencement date for each Research Hubs and Training Centres grant opportunity will be available on the ARC website.

2.9. Research Hubs engage Australia's best researchers to develop collaborative solutions to the strategic Industrial Transformation Priorities. The focus is on the creation of industry and academic partnerships working together on research and development projects to create innovative and transformative solutions for industry.

2.10. Training Centres foster close partnerships between university-based researchers and industry, through creating and delivering innovative Higher Degree by Research (HDR) and postdoctoral training. Training Centres are to develop researchers with capability in end user research that is vital to Australia's future. In delivering this training, the Training Centre focuses its researchers on developing solutions relevant to the Industrial Transformation Priorities.

Grant amount and grant period

3.1. For each ITRP grant opportunity, applications will be considered for the levels and durations of funding listed in Parts A and B of these grant opportunity guidelines.

Eligibility criteria

Who is eligible to apply for a grant?

4.1. We will only accept applications from Eligible Organisations listed in Appendix B. The Eligible Organisation that applies will be the ‘Administering Organisation’ (i.e. ‘You’).

What are the eligibility requirements for applications?

4.2. The organisation roles available under the ITRP are:

Administering Organisation;

Other Eligible Organisations;

Partner Organisations; and

Other Organisations.

4.3. Your application must:

directly address one or more of the current Industrial Transformation Priorities;

nominate at least one Australian Partner Organisation; and

meet the participant requirements in Part A - Industrial Transformation Research Hubs or Part B - Industrial Transformation Training Centres.

4.4. You and each Other Eligible Organisation and Partner Organisation on an application must each commit a significant contribution of cash and/or in-kind and/or other material resources to the application having regard to the total cost of the proposed Research Hub or Training Centre and the relative contribution of any Chief Investigator(s) (CIs) or Partner Investigator(s) (PIs) at the organisation.

4.5. The minimum contribution required from each Eligible Organisation, Partner Organisation and/or Other Organisation is explained in the relevant Part of these grant opportunity guidelines.

4.6. You must ensure that any additional certification requirements, applied by the ARC as a result of findings of breaches of the *Australian Code for the Responsible Conduct of Research* (2018 version), are provided at the grant opportunity closing date for any named participant on an application.

4.7. Your application must include a certification by Your Deputy Vice-Chancellor (Research) or equivalent in the Application that You have in accordance with the University responsibilities defined in the *ARC Research Security Framework* (2026 version) assessed research security risks in relation to the application, including in relation to participation of all named participants, and where risks are identified, have ensured appropriate mitigations are in place for the duration of the project.

Who is eligible to be a Partner Organisation?

4.8. A Partner Organisation is an Australian or overseas organisation, other than an Eligible Organisation, which satisfies the eligibility requirements for a Partner Organisation in 4.8-4.11.

4.9. A Partner Organisation cannot be:

included on the National Redress Scheme’s website on the list of ‘Institutions that have not joined or signified their intent to join the Scheme’ (www.nationalredress.gov.au);

an Eligible Organisation;

a controlled entity of any Eligible Organisation; or

an entity (for example a joint venture) where more than 50% is owned by one or more Eligible Organisations.

4.10. Each Partner Organisation must:

provide evidence of new or on-going collaboration directly with You and/or with an Other Eligible Organisation on the application; and

make a contribution of cash and/or in-kind and/or other material resources that is specific to the project, and having regard to the total cost of the project and not be part of a broader contribution to Your organisation or an Other Eligible Organisation

provide a Letter of Partner Organisation Certification, using the proforma provided on GrantConnect.

4.11. Partner Organisation cash contributions cannot:

be sourced from funds awarded or appropriated by the Commonwealth or an Australian State or Territory Governments for the purposes of research, nor from funds previously used to leverage government research or research infrastructure funding; or

be a contribution to salaries for CIs and/or PIs on the application.

Who is eligible to be an Other Organisation?

4.12. Organisations that are not Eligible Organisations and not Partner Organisations but that are named as organisational participants on an application will be Other Organisations. Other Organisations are not required to make a cash or in-kind contribution to the project, but must be relevant to and involved in the research and training programs of the Research Hub or Training Centre.

4.13. An Other Organisation cannot be included on the National Redress Scheme’s website on the list of ‘Institutions that have not joined or signified their intent to join the Scheme’ (www.nationalredress.gov.au).

Who is eligible to be a named participant?

4.14. Roles that named participants may be nominated for under these grant opportunity guidelines are:

Research Hub Director (Part A);

Training Centre Director (Part B);

Chief Investigators (CIs) (Parts A and B); and

Partner Investigators (PIs) (Parts A and B).

4.15. A Project cannot commence until all named participants meet the eligibility criteria in these grant opportunity guidelines for the selected role they are to perform.

4.16. An application will be deemed ineligible if a named participant is the subject of a decision by the ARC’s CEO or delegate, that any application they make to the ARC will be excluded from consideration for a period of time, whether or not they otherwise meet the eligibility requirements, for research integrity reasons. Such decisions will generally reflect consequential action taken by the ARC in response to findings of a serious breach of the *Australian Code for the Responsible Conduct of Research* (2018 version) (including a finding of research misconduct, where this term is used). This is an ongoing criterion, including findings made during the application and assessment process.

4.17. As at the grant commencement date, or if successful, at any time during the project activity period, named participants who:

meet the eligibility criteria to be a CI, cannot elect to be a PI; and

do not meet the eligibility criteria to be a CI, must be a PI.

4.18. All named participants in an application must have met their obligations regarding previously funded projects, including submission of satisfactory final reports to the ARC at the grant opportunity closing date and any relevant consequential actions that may have been applied under the *ARC Research Integrity and Other Misconduct Policy* (2026 version).

4.19. All named participants must also have an up‑to‑date RMS profile that includes declarations relating to foreign affiliations, relationships and financial interests as outlined in the *ARC Research Security Framework* (2026 version). This ongoing obligation applies at each stage of the assessment process and throughout the duration of the project. If during the assessment process, We find that any named participant’s RMS profile does not include all required declarations relating to foreign affiliations, relationships and financial interests, We may recommend to the ARC Board that Your application not be considered further.

Research Hub and Training Centre Directors

4.20. To be eligible, the nominated participant for the Research Hub or Training Centre Director role must, as at the grant opportunity closing date and, if successful, for the project activity period:

meet the eligibility criteria to apply as a CI;

be employed by the Administering Organisation a minimum of 50% of Full Time Equivalent (0.5 FTE); and

commit a minimum of 0.5 FTE to the activities of the Research Hub or Training Centre.

Chief Investigators

4.21. CIs who are not the Research Hub or Training Centre Director must meet at least one of the following criteria as at the grant commencement date and, if the application is successful, for the project activity period:

be an employee for at least 0.2 FTE at an Eligible Organisation; or

be a holder of an honorary academic appointment (as defined in the Glossary) at an Eligible Organisation.

4.22. CIs must reside in Australia for more than 50% of the project activity period. Any significant absences including fieldwork or study leave directly related to the project must have approval from You and must not total more than half the project activity period. In extraordinary circumstances, a change may be approved by Us via a formal Variation request.

4.23. CIs must not undertake an HDR during the project activity period.

Partner Investigators

4.24. PIs may, or may not, be an employee of a Partner Organisation.

What are the limits on the number of applications and projects per named participant?

4.25. The limits in clauses 4.26 to 4.29 only apply to Directors and CIs and are designed to ensure that named participants have the capacity to undertake each project.

4.26. A named participant can apply for and be concurrently funded through the Linkage Program for a maximum of 4 Linkage Program projects as a CI or Director. For the purposes of counting project limits, Linkage Program projects:

include CIs on Linkage Projects, ITRP and ARC Centres of Excellence projects;

include Directors on ITRP, Special Research Initiatives and ARC Centres of Excellence projects;

do not include CIs on LIEF, Special Research Initiatives, or LASP projects; and

do not include Fellows under the Industry Fellowships Program.

4.27. A named participant can be funded for a maximum of one Director role for a Linkage Program project. This includes ITRP, ARC Centres of Excellence and Special Research Initiatives.

4.28. A named participant on a Research Hub or Training Centre application may be named on other ARC grant opportunity applications and subject to the separate limits on those opportunities, those applications may be retained until the outcome of the relevant ITRP application is known.

4.29. All named participants on a successful ITRP application must meet the project limit requirements identified at 4.26 and 4.27 above before the project can start. Project limits can be met by relinquishing existing project(s), or withdrawing role(s) on existing projects, or withdrawing application(s) that would exceed the project limits. We reserve the right to determine which grants are to be relinquished.

4.30. It is Your responsibility to determine if applying for, or holding, a project under these grant opportunity guidelines will affect an individual researcher’s eligibility for other ARC grant opportunities as other ARC grant opportunities may have different project limits. We reserve the right to change project and application limits in future grant opportunities.

Security, Defence or International Relations of Australia

Pursuant to the ARC Act, the Minister must refuse to approve a grant if the Minister considers that, for reasons relevant to the security, defence or international relations of Australia, the approval should be refused.

What the grant money can be used for

Eligible grant activities

5.1. The Linkage Program supports research activities that meet the definition of ‘research’, as defined in the Glossary.

5.2. You can only request and spend the grant on eligible expenditure items that directly support the project and in accordance with any additional conditions or special conditions in the grant agreement.

5.3. Eligible expenditure items may include:

salary support for other personnel, for example, research associates and assistants, a Manager, technicians and laboratory attendants but excluding the Director, CIs and PIs. Salary support must be at an appropriate level, including 30% on-costs, at the employing organisation;

stipends for HDR students, at an appropriate level for the Eligible Organisation, but not less than the level indicated on the ‘Salaries and Stipends’ page of the ARC website;

equipment (and its maintenance) and consumables, including specialised computer equipment and software essential for the project;

travel costs essential to the project, including economy travel costs for domestic and/or international travel and accommodation, not exceeding an average of $25,000 per year of the project. The following travel costs are not counted towards the average of $25,000 per year limit:

i. expenditure on field research essential to the project, including technical and logistical support, travel expenses (including accommodation, meals and incidental costs);

ii. reasonable essential costs to allow a participant who is a carer, or who personally requires care or assistance, to undertake travel essential to the project;

5.4. The Research Hubs and Training Centres grant opportunities include specific budget items for salary support, stipends and/or teaching relief as stated in Parts A and B of these grant opportunity guidelines.

5.5. Research Hubs and Training Centres are led by a Research Hub/Training Centre Director and may be supported by a Research Hub/Training Centre Manager to undertake the business management, corporate governance and administrative responsibilities. We encourage You to carefully consider including support for a Research Hub/Training Centre Manager in your application, to allow the Director to prioritise the strategic, research and training leadership tasks. It is up to You to determine the most appropriate team for your application.

Ineligible grant activities

5.6. Unless the following activities meet the definition of research, the Linkage Program does not support production of:

computer programs, research aids and tools;

data warehouses, catalogues or bibliographies; or

teaching materials.

5.7. You cannot request or use grant funds for the following activities/expenditures:

research activities, infrastructure or projects previously funded or currently being funded through any other Commonwealth grant;

medical research, as detailed in the ARC *Medical Research Policy* (2025 version) on the ARC website;

experimental development, as defined in the Glossary;

activities leading solely to the creation or performance of a work of art, including visual art, musical compositions, drama, dance, film, broadcasts, designs and literary works, unless those works are directly related to the project’s research activities and demonstrably research based;

contracted research or consultancy arrangements where one or more Organisation(s) is seeking expert external assistance not available within their own organisation, in order to develop specific applications or outputs that involve little innovation or are low risk;

basic facilities that must be provided (where relevant) and funded by You, Eligible Organisation and/or Partner Organisation and are not funded by the grant:

i. standard refurbishment costs of a laboratory;

ii. bench fees or similar laboratory access fees;

iii. access to a basic library collection;

iv. access to film or music editing facilities;

v. work accommodation (for example, laboratory and office space, suitably equipped and furnished);

vi. basic computer facilities such as desktop computers, portable computer devices, printers, word processing, and other standard software; and

vii. standard reference materials or funds for abstracting services;

capital works and general infrastructure costs;

costs not directly related to the project, including but not limited to professional membership fees, professional development courses, fees for patent application and maintenance, equipment for live music or drama performances, equipment for gallery and museum exhibitions, visas, relocation costs, entertainment costs, purchase of alcohol, insurance, mobile phones (purchase or call charges) and other indirect costs;

fees for international students or the Higher Education Contribution Scheme (HECS) and Higher Education Loan Program (HELP) liabilities for students; and

salaries and/or on-costs, in whole or in part, for Directors, CIs or PIs.

The assessment criteria

6.2. The Assessment Criteria for each scheme are available at:

Industrial Transformation Research Hubs (Part A)

Industrial Transformation Training Centres (Part B).

How to apply

Application process

7.1. Instructions To Applicants will be made available on GrantConnect on the opening date of the grant opportunity and applicants must follow the processes described in those Instructions, including regarding attachments to the application.

7.2. Your application must be completed in RMS. We will not accept applications received after the closing time, other than in exceptional circumstances (such as due to natural disasters) in which case We will discuss this with You, and if We agree, invite You to make a late application.

7.3. You cannot change Your application after the closing date and time, unless invited to by the ARC.

7.4. We reserve the right to seek evidence to support the certification of applications at any point.

7.5. Questions during the application period should be directed to the Administering Organisation’s Research Office. Answers to Frequently Asked Questions will be posted on GrantConnect.

7.6. If You wish to withdraw Your application, You must inform Us in writing.

Participant’s responsibilities

7.7. All named participants in an application must:

take responsibility for the authorship and intellectual content of the application, appropriately citing sources and acknowledging all significant contributions, including from third parties;

ensure that any additional requirements or requests for information, applied by Us are provided at the grant opportunity closing date; and

have an up-to-date RMS profile that includes declarations relating to foreign affiliations, relationships and financial interests as outlined in the *ARC Research Security Framework* (2026 version). This ongoing obligation applies at each stage of the assessment process and through the duration of the project. If, during the assessment process We find that any named participant’s RMS profile does not include all required declarations relating to foreign affiliations, relationships and financial interests, We may recommend to the Minister that Your application not be considered further.

7.8. Each Organisation must participate in the project for the project activity period unless otherwise approved by Us.

Your research security and research integrity responsibilities

7.9. You must, consistent with the *ARC Research Security Framework* (2026 version):

ensure that all named participants comply with clause 7.7.c (up-to-date RMS profile) at each stage of the assessment process;

before lodging an application, assess research security risks in relation to the application, including in relation to participation of all named participants;

where risks are identified, ensure appropriate mitigations are in place for the duration of the project; and

not submit applications that You consider pose high risk to Australia’s security, defence and international relations.

7.10. You must comply with, and ensure that all named participants on Your application comply with, all applicable requirements of the *ARC Research Integrity and Other Misconduct Policy* (2026 version) and the *Australian Code for the Responsible Conduct of Research* (2018 version).

Director responsibilities

7.11. The Research Hub or Training Centre Director must:

take responsibility for developing and implementing the strategies and managing the research project; and

coordinate the research effort and reporting structures across the participating organisations.

Chief Investigator responsibilities

7.12. Chief Investigators must:

take significant intellectual responsibility for the conception and conduct of the project and for any strategic decisions required in its pursuit and the communication of results;

make a commitment to carrying out the project and not assume the role of a supplier of resources for work that will largely be undertaken by others; and

ensure effective supervision, support and mentoring at all times of research personnel, including HDR candidates and postdoctoral researchers for whom they are responsible.

Partner Investigator responsibilities

7.13. Partner Investigators must:

take significant intellectual responsibility for the planning and conduct of the project and for any strategic decisions required in its pursuit and the communication of results;

have the relevant skills and experience to contribute to the project;

make a commitment to carrying out the project and not assume the role of a supplier of resources for work that will largely be undertaken by others; and/or

provide effective supervision, support and mentoring of research personnel, as required.

National Interest Test statement

7.14. Applicants must provide a National Interest Test statement: a brief statement that articulates the benefits of the proposed research in plain English in general terms beyond the period of the grant.

Timing of the grant opportunities

7.15. Schemes under these guidelines typically accept one round of applications each year.

7.16. You must submit Your application between the grant opportunity opening and closing date and time specified on GrantConnect.

The grant selection process

Assessment of grant applications

8.1. We will review Your application against the eligibility criteria. If ineligible, We must not recommend the application for funding.

8.2. All applications will be considered through a competitive peer review process, based on:

how well it meets the weighted assessment criteria;

how it is ranked against other applications; and

whether it provides value with money (as defined in the Glossary).

8.3. Applications will undergo peer review assessment and recommendations will be made to the Minister from a Selection Advisory Committee, along with advice, when necessary, from the ARC.

8.4. You are responsible for ensuring that your application is complete and accurate. Giving false or misleading information is a serious offence under the *Criminal Code Act 1995* (Cth). We will investigate any incomplete, inaccurate, false or misleading information and may recommend to the Minister that Your application not be considered further.

8.5. For the avoidance of doubt, this includes failure to disclose any:

conflict of interest of any named participant, including but not limited to matters described in sections 3.1 and 3.3 of the *ARC Conflict of Interest and Confidentiality Policy* (2025 version) on the ARC website; and

foreign affiliations as outlined in the *ARC Research Security Framework* (2026 version).

8.6. We may seek advice and information on security or other matters from Commonwealth agencies and third-party sources at any time during the process. We may consult with You about this information.

8.7. During the assessment, We may request additional information, which does not change the nature of Your application.

Due diligence

8.8. Due diligence activity may be undertaken at eligibility, assessment or decision-making stages. The ARC may consider the outcomes of due diligence activity and provide advice to the ARC Board and/or Minister.

8.9. We may seek additional information about you, project partners, related entities and associated entities, and related personnel from third party sources, including other Commonwealth agencies, for due diligence purposes. The ARC may also consider information that is discovered through the normal course of business. See clauses 13.9 to 13.17 (Privacy) for information on how we use the information provided to us.

8.10. If the ARC identifies risks which would affect the assessment, it may ask You to respond to these, subject to security considerations. If the ARC identifies risks that cannot be adequately mitigated, it may recommend that the Minister refuse your application under section 48(6) of the ARC Act.

8.11. The Minister may approve applications pending specific conditions based on the advice from the ARC.

###### Research integrity

The ARC may consider the relevance of any Research Integrity Event to the proposed research outlined in the application.

We may undertake actions, and exercise any rights, expressed as being able to be undertaken or exercised by the ARC under the *Research Integrity and Other Misconduct Policy* (2026 version) (including precautionary and consequential actions) to address or mitigate any potential or proven risks regarding any named participant on Your application.

###### Research security

The ARC may provide advice to the Minister on whether the applicant or any named participant has affiliations, relationships or financial interests with any foreign entities which may be contrary to Australia’s sovereignty, values and national interests.

In order to protect against risks relevant to the security, defence or international relations of Australia, We may share relevant outcomes of due diligence with other Commonwealth agencies for the other agencies’ grant-related purposes or research security purposes in relation to specified individuals, entities or projects. This may include information in Your application, and personal information of named participants in RMS.

Requests not to assess process

8.16. You may name up to three persons whom You do not wish to assess an application by submitting a ‘Request Not to Assess’ (RNTA) form in RMS as detailed on GrantConnect and on the ARC website.

8.17. The RNTA form must be received by Us in RMS two weeks prior to the closing date.

8.18. Only one request containing the names of up to three individual assessors may be submitted per application.

8.19. We have discretion about whether We accept or refuse an RNTA. We will not notify you of the outcome.

Who will approve grants

8.20. The ARC Board will make recommendations for funding to the Minister, after considering the advice from peer review and any advice from the ARC, and alignment with Australian Government Priorities.

8.21. The Minister will decide which grants to fund and may at any time decide not to approve a grant for reasons relevant to the security, defence or international relations of Australia. The Minister’s decision is final in all matters.

8.22. The outcome of all applications will be published in RMS.

Notification of application outcomes

9.1. If Your grant application is successful, We:

will give You a copy of the Minister’s approval no more than 21 calendar days after the Minister’s decision;

may give You earlier notice of the Minister’s approval and may impose a short embargo on announcements in order to enable parties to co-ordinate announcements; and

will list Your grant on GrantConnect no more than 21 calendar days after the date of effect.

Successful grant applications

Grant Agreements

10.1. You must enter into a grant agreement with Us to receive a grant.

10.2. We use the ARC’s template grant agreement which contains standard terms and conditions that cannot be changed. A sample grant agreement for these grant opportunities is available on GrantConnect. Any special conditions will be identified in the grant offer.

10.3. You will have 60 calendar days from the date of the grant offer to execute this grant agreement.

10.4. We must execute a grant agreement with You before We can make any payments. We are not responsible for any of Your project expenditure unless and until a grant agreement is executed.

10.5. You must not start any activities until all third-party agreements are executed, unless otherwise specified in the grant agreement.

Assurance and Compliance At Award

10.6. If your grant application is successful, You and all named participants on the project must complete compliance and assurance checks before You are able to accept the grant offer in RMS.

10.7. These checks will include, but not be limited to:

information about the project’s start and end dates;

confirmation that governance and compliance with ARC policies are in place as set out at clauses 12.9 to 12.16;

declaration of actual, potential and/or perceived conflicts of interest in relation to the project;

updated person profile and confirmation that ORCID is linked within your RMS profile; and

confirmation that any additional research security requirements outlined in special conditions have been met.

10.8. All successful applications must complete the at-award stage to confirm compliance with the above clauses within 60 days of the announcement before the offer can be accepted, in line with the grant agreement.

How We pay the grant

Payments will be made in accordance with the grant agreement.

The grant offer will specify the approved grant amount. We will not pay more than the approved grant amount. If you incur extra costs, You must meet them.

Grant funding may be subject to indexation.

All amounts referred to in these grant opportunity guidelines are exclusive of GST, unless expressly stated otherwise.

Any grant awarded will be subject to sufficient funds being available for the project, the provisions of the ARC Act and the continued satisfactory progress of the project.

Peer Review Obligations

Unless notified otherwise by the ARC, CIs will be obligated to assess up to 20 new applications for ARC funding per annum for the project activity period (with the required number being at the ARC’s discretion) in accordance with instructions provided by the ARC.

Failure of obligated assessors to honour this commitment may result in the ARC taking action in accordance with the grant agreement, including: suspending some or all of the Project (including expenditure of the grant funds) until obligations are met; requiring that the non-compliance or inability be remedied; and placing conditions on the consideration of any future grant applications.

Announcement of Grants

11.1. The outcome of all applications will be published in RMS.

11.2. The ARC will mint a Grant digital object identifier (DOI) using information provided in the grant application and the At Award compliance form.

11.3. We will publicise and report offers and grants awarded, including the following information about the project:

Your name and any other parties involved in or associated with the project;

named participants and their organisations;

Description

Research Hubs engage Australia's best researchers to develop collaborative solutions to the Industrial Transformation Priorities. The focus is on the creation of industry and academic partnerships working together on research and development projects to create innovative and transformative solutions for industry.

Objectives

The Research Hubs scheme objectives are to:

support collaborative research projects between universities and organisations outside the Australian higher education sector that involve cutting-edge research on new technologies; and

leverage national and international investment in targeted industry sectors, including from industry and other research end-users. The intended outcomes of the Research Hubs scheme are:

growth, productivity and competitiveness within the Industrial Transformation Priorities; and

This includes authority across all stages of the data lifecycle, from creation and collection to access, analysis, interpretation, management, dissemination and reuse. |

| in-kind contributions | A contribution of goods, services, materials and/or time to the project from an individual, business or organisation. Values should be calculated based on the most likely actual cost, for example, current market, preferred provider or internal provider rates/valuations/rentals/charges (that is in the financial year of the date of the application) of the costs of labour, workspaces, equipment and databases. The calculations covering time and costs should be documented by the Administering Organisation. The ARC may require these calculations to be audited. |
| Instructions to Applicants | a set of instructions prepared by the ARC to assist applicants in completing the application form. |

| Partner Investigator | a named participant who satisfies the eligibility criteria for a Partner Investigator (PI) under these grant opportunity guidelines. |

| Postdoctoral Research Associate or PDRA | a postdoctoral research associate funded by the Commonwealth through the Administering Organisation, who will be employed on the project. |
| Postgraduate Researcher or PGR | a postgraduate research student funded by the Commonwealth through the Administering Organisation, who will undertake a Higher Degree by Research through the project. |
| Preprint or comparable resource | A preprint or comparable resource is a scholarly output that is uploaded by the authors to a recognised publicly accessible archive, repository, or pre-print service (such as, but not limited to, arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, ChemRxiv, Peer J Preprints, Zenodo, GitHub, PsyArXiv and publicly available university or government repositories etc.). This will include a range of materials that have been subjected to varying degrees of peer review from none to light and full review. Ideally, a preprint or comparable resource should have a unique identifier or a DOI (digital object identifier). Any citation of a preprint or comparable resource should be explicitly identified as such and listed in the references with a DOI, URL or equivalent, version number and/or date of access, as applicable. |
| project | an application approved to receive grant funds, and in respect of which both parties have accepted a grant offer under an ARC grant agreement.<br>For clarity:<br>references to ‘Project’ include the entire Project, and any part of, or an activity within, the Project; and<br>Fellowships and Awards are types of Projects. |
| project activity period | the period during which a project is receiving grant funding according to the original grant Offer, or has any carryover funds approved by the ARC, or an approved variation to the project’s end date. During this period, the project is known as an active project. |
| project end date | the expected date that the project activity is completed and by which all grant funding will be spent. |

| research | the creation of new knowledge and/or the use of existing knowledge in a new and creative way so as to generate new concepts, methodologies, inventions and understandings. This could include synthesis and analysis of previous research to the extent that it is new and creative. |
| Research Associate | postdoctoral research associate funded by the Commonwealth through the Administering Organisation, who will be employed on the Project. |
| Research Hubs | refers to the Industrial Transformation Research Hubs (ITRH) under the Industrial Transformation Research Program. |
| Research Hub Manager | a person with relevant skills and experience who is able to management and facilitate the day-to-day operation of the Research Hub. |
| research infrastructure | the assets, facilities, services, and coordinated access to major national and/or international research facilities or consortia which directly support research in higher education organisations and more broadly, and which maintain the capacity of researchers to undertake excellent research and deliver innovative outcomes. |

| Research Office | a business unit within an Eligible Organisation that is responsible for contact with the ARC regarding applications and projects. |
| Research Opportunity and Performance Evidence (ROPE) | an ARC policy framework used to consider and assess the quality and research excellence of a named participant within the context of the participant’s career and life experiences. One key element is that the assessment process takes into account the quality rather than simply the volume or size of the research contribution. |
| research output | includes all products (including Preprints or comparable resources) of an ARC-funded research Project that meet the definition of Research. |
| Selection Advisory Committee (SAC) | a group of experts from academia and industry appointed to assist the ARC to assess applications and to provide a recommendation for funding to the Minister. A SAC may be drawn from the ARC College of Experts. |
| selection process | the method used to select applications to which funding is awarded. This process may involve comparative assessment of applications or the assessment of applications against the eligibility criteria and/or the assessment criteria. |
| Special Condition | a condition included in the grant offer by the ARC that is identified as being a special condition, or is otherwise reasonably apparent that it is intended to be a special condition. |
| strategic basic research | experimental and theoretical work undertaken to acquire new knowledge directed into specified broad areas in the expectation of practical discoveries. | value with money | in this document refers to ‘value with relevant money’ which is a judgement based on the application representing an efficient, effective, economical and ethical use of public resources determined from a variety of considerations, including merit of the proposal, risk, cost and expected contribution to outcome achievement. |
