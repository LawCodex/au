In this instrument:

***Act*** means the *Carbon Credits (Carbon Farming Initiative) Act 2011*.

***baseline scenario***, in relation to an offsets project, means the scenario in which the project is not carried out.

***biomass*** means vegetation-derived organic matter, and includes living and non-living matter.

***carbon protection area***—see subsection 7(2).

***carbon stocks*** means the amount of carbon (expressed in units of mass) stored in included carbon pools that are relevant to calculating the net abatement amount.

***carbon stock change*** means the change in carbon stocks (expressed in units of mass) over a specified period of time.

***CFI Mapping Guidelines*** means the latest publicly released version of the document entitled “Carbon Farming Initiative (CFI) Mapping Guidelines”.

Note: The CFI Mapping Guidelines could in 2026 be viewed on the Department’s website (https://www.dcceew.gov.au).

***clearing*** means to kill, destroy or remove trees.

***clearing to facilitate timber harvesting*** includes clearing trees for roads to access the timber harvesting area, for snig tracks to remove cut timber or for landings to collect logs for haulage.

***commercial forestry use*** means the use of a public native forest for the purposes of obtaining wood for sale.

***disturbance*** means degradation or disturbance.

***exclusion area***—see subsection 11(2).

***EPBC Act forestry-related decision*** means a decision under the *Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999* (or under a law or procedure of a State that is accredited under that Act) that authorises any of the following (or that does not approve of any of the following):

timber harvesting;

clearing of native forest;

forestry operations (as defined in section 42A of that Act).

Note: Forestry operations are defined in that section as the following, when done for commercial purposes: the planting of trees; their management before harvesting; the harvesting of timber products and related land clearing; land preparation and regeneration and transport operations.

***forest carbon pool*** means a carbon pool mentioned in item 1 to 3 of column 2 of the table in subsection 24(1).

***forest debris*** means above-ground or below-ground dead vegetation-derived organic matter.

***forest management*** includes the following:

prescribed burns;

emergency back-burning or mechanical clearing for firebreaks to prevent the spread of a wildfire.

***forestry region*** means:

the area covered by a Regional Forest Agreement in accordance with the *Regional Forest Agreements Act 2002* on 1 July 2024; or

in the case of public native forests that were not covered by any such Regional Forest Agreement on that date—an area of those forests that is not less than 1.5 million hectares and that was, as at 1 July 2024, a separate forestry management area designated by the government of the relevant State.

Note: The area covered by a Regional Forest Agreement is treated as a single region for the purposes of this instrument even if it comprises a number of regions or subregions under the management arrangements of the relevant State.

***FullCAM*** means the latest publicly released version of the Full Carbon Accounting Model used to model forest carbon stocks associated with land use and management for Australia’s National Greenhouse Gas Inventory, and includes related databases and spatial inputs used by FullCAM for its calculations.

Note: FullCAM could in 2026 be viewed on the Department’s website (https://www.dcceew.gov.au).

***harvest scenario***—see paragraph 39(9)(b).

***included carbon pool***—see subsection 24(1).

***independent qualified assessor***: a person is an ***independent qualified assessor*** if the Regulator is satisfied of all of the following at the time of the person’s proposed or actual performance of an action mentioned in this instrument:

the person is not employed by the project proponent or, if the project proponent is an authority of a State, by that State (disregarding any employment that relates to performing the action, or to performing a similar action under this instrument or another methodology determination);

the person has the requisite expertise and experience to perform the action;

the person was not significantly involved in applying for the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project, conducting the project, or developing this instrument.

***INFM Modelling Guidelines*** means the latest publicly released version of the INFM Modelling Guidelines developed for the purposes of this instrument.

Note: The INFM Modelling Guidelines could in 2026 be viewed on the Department’s website (https://www.dcceew.gov.au).

***IPCC first order decay model*** means the model for estimating the amount of degradable organic carbon in, and methane emissions from, solid waste disposal sites described in Chapter 3 of the 2019 Refinement to the 2006 IPCC Guidelines for National Greenhouse Gas Inventories, published by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change.

Note: The IPCC first order decay model could in 2026 be viewed on the IPCC’s website (https://www.ipcc.ch).

***major disturbance event*** means a disturbance that is likely to reduce carbon stocks by more than 15% across more than 20% of the net harvestable area, where the net harvestable area is determined as at the date the application was made for the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project.

***major vegetation group*** means a major vegetation group as defined in the National Vegetation Information System.

***M-layer*** means the biophysical data set, known as the Site Potential (M) spatial layer, that is embedded in FullCAM.

Note: FullCAM could in 2026 be viewed on the Department’s website (https://www.dcceew.gov.au).

***modified sustainable yield***—see subsection 28(1).

***National Vegetation Information System*** means the latest publicly released version of the National Vegetation Information System.

Note: The National Vegetation Information System could in 2026 be viewed on the Department’s website (https://www.dcceew.gov.au).

***native forest*** means an area of land:

that is at least 0.2 of a hectare; and

of which every 0.2 of a hectare contains trees that are within their natural range and that: have attained, or have the potential to attain, a crown cover of at least 20% of that area of land; and have reached, or have the potential to reach, a height of at least 2 metres; and

no part of which is a plantation.

***net abatement amount***, for an eligible offsets project, means the carbon dioxide equivalent net abatement amount for the project in the reporting period for the purposes of paragraph 106(1)(c) of the Act.

***net harvestable area*** means the portion of the public native forests in the project area that is available for timber harvesting after accounting for areas that are excluded from harvesting due to regulatory and operational constraints (including, for example, restrictions on harvesting due to the slope of the land, environmental factors or safety).

***net harvested area*** means so much of the net harvestable area that has been subjected to actual harvesting over a specified period (other than an area cleared to facilitate timber harvesting). The net harvested area excludes any area covered by the crowns of canopy trees that were not harvested during the specified period.

***no-harvest scenario***—see paragraph 39(9)(b).

***plantation*** means an area of land that:

is planted with trees, whether by direct seeding or by using seedling stock, for the purpose of timber harvesting; and

is not managed to provide a reasonable degree of consistency between the composition and structure of the vegetation on the land and the native vegetation that was on the land before the later of: 1788; or when it was first subject, after 1788, to use for the purposes of obtaining wood for sale or to mechanical clearing.

***prescribed burn*** means the controlled application of fire in a selected area (including post-harvest) for hazard reduction, cultural conservation practices or other natural resource management objectives.

***prior period***—see subsection 27(1).

***prior period forest estate model***—see subsection 27(5).

***private native forest*** means a native forest (other than a public native forest) that may be used for the purposes of obtaining timber for sale.

***project map***—see subsection 14(1).

***project scenario***, in relation to an offsets project, means the scenario in which the project is carried out.

***public native forest*** means a native forest in Australia that is Crown land.

***pulplog*** means a log that is mainly used to produce woodchips or wood pulp.

***road*** includes track.

***sawlog*** includes a log that is used to produce sawn timber, veneers, poles, piles or girders, but does not include a pulplog or a residue (for example, firewood).

***State*** includes a Territory of the Commonwealth.

***sustainable yield***, for the public native forests in the project area, means an estimate (based on continued commercial forestry use of those forests) of the long-term wood yield from those forests that could be maintained in perpetuity in accordance with given forest management strategies and sustainable use objectives.

***timber harvesting*** means the carrying out of harvesting operations to obtain logs for timber products.

***tree*** means a perennial plant that has primary supporting structures consisting of secondary xylem.

A reference in subsection 27(1), 28(4), 39(6) or 40(5) to a period of years prior to the end of the last financial year before the making of an application for the declaration of a project as an eligible offsets project is, if there has been an interim suspension of timber harvesting in the carbon protection areas for the project of the kind referred to in subsection 10(2) during that period, to be construed as a reference to that period of years prior to the end of the last financial year during which timber harvesting was not so suspended.

### Part 2—Offsets projects to which instrument applies

###### 6 Instrument applies to projects to stop timber harvesting in public native forests

For the purposes of paragraph 106(1)(a) of the Act, this instrument applies to an offsets project that:

as a result of a decision of the government of a State to stop harvesting in public native forests: removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere by sequestering additional carbon in trees and forest debris in those forests; and avoids the emission of greenhouse gases associated with post-harvest (slash) burns in those forests; and

can reasonably be expected to result in eligible carbon abatement.

###### 7 Stopping timber harvesting

An offsets project to stop timber harvesting may relate to stopping timber harvesting across the whole of the project area or only in one or more parts of the project area.

An area in which timber harvesting is stopped for the purposes of an offsets project to which this instrument applies is a ***carbon protection area***.

A carbon protection area cannot be removed from an eligible offsets project or reduced in size, but additional carbon protection areas can be included in the project or an existing carbon protection area can be increased in size.

Forest management activities may be undertaken in the public native forests that constitute the project area for an offsets project, but not any such activities that result in the removal of wood from a carbon protection area within the project area.

If timber harvesting is stopped in only a part of the project area, the whole area remains the project area for the purposes of this instrument.

Note: Section 11 provides that the minimum project area must include the commercial public native forests in at least one whole forestry region. Even though an offsets project may relate to stopping timber harvesting in a part only of the project area, the net abatement amount for a project is calculated based on reductions in emissions of greenhouse gases, and increased removals of carbon dioxide, across the whole project area. Section 43 provides that carbon abatement is not eligible for Australian carbon credit units unless it meets the hurdle requirement for a minimum reduction in the volume of wood extracted from the project area.

### Part 3—Project requirements

#### Division 3.1—General

###### 8 Operation of this Part

For the purposes of paragraph 106(1)(b) of the Act, this Part sets out requirements that must be met for a project to which this instrument applies to be an eligible offsets project.

Note: Section 27 of the Act provides that the Regulator may, on application, declare an offsets project to be an eligible offsets project if it meets the requirements of the applicable methodology determination and other requirements.

#### Division 3.2—Eligibility requirements

###### 9 Designation of public native forests for commercial forestry use

The project area for the eligible offsets project must consist only of areas of land that, at the time the project is declared as an eligible offsets project:

are public native forests designated for commercial forestry use; and

were designated for commercial forestry use for at least 10 years before the making of the application for that declaration.

For the purposes of this section, a public native forest is designated for commercial forestry use if it is available for commercial forestry use in accordance with the applicable law relating to the use of the forest.

It does not matter that the public native forests included in the project area are multiple-use forests not exclusively used for commercial forestry.

A carbon protection area included in an eligible offsets project may, after the project becomes an eligible offsets project, be included in a national park or other conservation reserve in order to provide for the effective management of the area, to secure the objectives of the project or for other conservation purposes.

A public native forest is available for commercial forestry use even though:

an authority is required to be issued by a government agency for the carrying out of a particular timber harvesting operation; or

surveys, plans or other preliminary steps are required before timber harvesting can commence; or

particular areas or trees are required to be excluded from timber harvesting because of the size of the trees, the slope of the land, the capacity of the trees to provide, or be likely to provide, habitat for particular fauna or flora, or for any other reason.

###### 10 No previous law or decision to stop timber harvesting in carbon protection area

An area of land cannot be included as a carbon protection area for an eligible offsets project if, at any time between 1 January 2000 and the date the project is declared as an eligible offsets project: a law of the Commonwealth or the relevant State stopped timber harvesting in the area (even if the law has since been repealed or amended to remove that prohibition); or the government of the relevant State decided to stop timber harvesting in the area (even if the decision has since been changed to remove that prohibition or the decision was scheduled to take effect after the date this instrument commences).

Subsection (1) does not apply if the stopping of timber harvesting in the area of land: was an interim measure to enable an assessment of the suitability of all or any part of the area as a carbon protection area in an eligible offsets project or as a national park or other conservation reserve; or was an interim measure taken on condition that an instrument be made that would allow the declaration of an eligible offsets project for stopping timber harvesting in the area.

Note: An example of the exclusion mentioned in paragraph (b) is the temporary moratorium that was announced by the New South Wales government on 7 September 2025 in connection with the proposal to establish a koala national park in the North Coast region of New South Wales.

Paragraph (1)(b) does not apply to a decision of the government of a relevant State to stop timber harvesting in the area if the decision was made in connection with an application for the declaration of an eligible offsets project that includes the area as a proposed carbon protection area and on condition that the project be declared as an eligible offsets project.

A law of the Commonwealth or of a State that requires an approval before timber harvesting is undertaken is not a law stopping timber harvesting for the purposes of this section.

Note: For example, the *Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999* is not a law that stops timber harvesting because an EPBC Act forestry-related decision is required in accordance with that Act to authorise timber harvesting.

The requirements of this section are, for the purposes of subparagraphs 27(4A)(a)(ii) and (b)(ii) of the Act, declared to be requirements made in lieu of the newness and regulatory additionality requirements.

###### 11 Minimum size of project area

The project area for an eligible offsets project must consist of all public native forests designated for commercial forestry use (as provided by section 9) in at least one whole forestry region.

However, the project area for an eligible offsets project may exclude a public native forest designated for commercial forestry use in a forestry region (an ***exclusion area***) if: a person (other than the project proponent, the State in which the project area is located, or a government agency or authority of that State) holds an eligible interest (within the meaning of the Act) in that exclusion area; and the eligible interest was created prior to 30 June 2024.

The combined area of public native forests in a forestry region that are excluded from the project area in accordance with subsection (2) must not be more than 5% of the public native forests designated for commercial forestry use in that forestry region.

The project area must not include any area: that is prohibited by this instrument from being included in the project area; or that is included in the project area for another eligible offsets project covered by this instrument.

For the purposes of subsection 77A(2) of the Act, a division of the overall project must not: result in the creation of a part of the project area that does not comprise at least all the public native forests designated for commercial forestry use in one whole forestry region (other than an exclusion area); or take effect during a reporting period for the project.

###### 12 Minimum reduction in volume of wood extracted from project area

The stopping of timber harvesting for the purposes of an eligible offsets project must be likely to achieve, during each 12 months of the crediting period for the project, a reduction in the volume of wood extracted from the project area that meets the hurdle requirement of subsection 43(1) for eligible carbon abatement.

Note: The hurdle requirement for a 12-month period requires a reduction in the volume of wood extracted from the project area during that 12-month period to be at least 20% less than the level in the baseline scenario for the same 12-month period.

###### 13 Published sustainable yield for public native forests

The public native forests in the project area must have a sustainable yield that was published by a relevant State government agency during the period from 1 July 2014 to 30 June 2024.

The published sustainable yield must provide an estimate for each financial year in the period of 15 years from the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project.

If more than one relevant sustainable yield was published by the relevant State government agency during the period from 1 July 2014 to 30 June 2024, the most recent estimate is to be used for the purposes of this instrument.

Note: The published sustainable yield may include the yield from exclusion areas. The published yield is relevant to the calculation of the unmodified sustainable yield in accordance with section 28.

###### 14 Project area mapping

The project proponent must prepare, in accordance with this section, a geospatial map of the project area in a digital format (the ***project map***).

The project map must be provided to the Regulator when an application is made for the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project.

The project map may be prepared as part of a geospatial map identifying the project area that is required by the legislative rules to accompany an application for the declaration of the project.

The project map must identify the boundaries of the following: the project area; the carbon protection areas; any exclusion areas in relation to the project; the forestry region or regions that constitute the project area.

The project map must identify: the spatial distribution of each major vegetation group across the project area; and the age class of the native forests in the project area by major vegetation groups (set out separately for carbon protection areas and other areas).

The project map may be revised (and the revised map provided to the Regulator) at any time before the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project.

The project map must be revised (and the revised map provided to the Regulator): when an application is made for the variation of the project area in accordance with section 29 of the Act; and when any additional carbon protection area is included in the project or an existing carbon protection area is increased in size; and when any correction is required to the information referred to in subsection (5).

Note: Subsection 7(3) precludes the removal of a carbon protection area from an eligible offsets project or a reduction in the size of a carbon protection area included in the project.

The project map must be prepared in accordance with the CFI Mapping Guidelines.

###### 15 Approval of government of State

The project proponent for a project, other than the State or the government of the State in which the public native forests that constitute the project area for the project are located, must have obtained the approval of that government to those forests being included in the project.

The approval must accompany the application for the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project.

###### 16 Permanence period for project of 100 years

For a project to be declared eligible, it must be a 100-year permanence period project.

###### 17 Information collection and reporting about private native forestry

There must be a mandatory requirement in the State in which the project area for an eligible offsets project is located:

for the collection of information about the volume of logs produced from relevant private native forests in the State, as referred to in section 40; and

for the reporting of that information to the government agency responsible for the regulation of private native forestry in the State.

###### 18 Cancellation of Australian carbon credit units if timber harvesting increases

A project proponent that is a State or the government of a State must request the Regulator to cancel the number of Australian carbon credit units worked out in accordance with this section if a relevant increase in timber harvesting occurs during the period (the ***relevant period***) that starts at the end of the crediting period for the project and ends at the end of its permanence period.

A ***relevant increase in timber harvesting*** is:

timber harvesting, or clearing to facilitate timber harvesting, that occurs during any 2-year period of the relevant period in an exclusion area in relation to the project; or

timber harvesting during any 2-year period of the relevant period that results in any of the following: the volume of wood extracted from the project area exceeding the harvest level in the baseline scenario for the project; the volume of wood extracted from the public native forests in the State in which the eligible offsets project is located (other than such forests in the project area for the project in question, or for any other eligible offsets project covered by this instrument and declared as eligible before the start of the relevant 2-year period) exceeding the direct leakage baseline harvest level in accordance with section 39; the volume of wood extracted from private native forests in the State in which the project is located exceeding the private native forests leakage baseline harvest level in accordance with section 40.

The exceedances mentioned in subsection (2) must be worked out as follows: for the purposes of subparagraph (2)(b)(i)—by calculating, for that 2-year period, the exceedance volume for the project area in accordance with Equation 9; for the purposes of subparagraph (2)(b)(ii)—by calculating, for that 2-year period, the exceedance volume for the applicable public native forests in accordance with Equation 9; for the purposes of subparagraph (2)(b)(iii)—by calculating, for that 2-year period, the exceedance volume for the applicable private native forests in accordance with Equation 12.

The number of Australian carbon credit units that must be requested to be cancelled must be equivalent to the emissions (in tonnes CO2-e) worked out as follows (rounded to the nearest whole number): for the purposes of paragraph (2)(a)—by calculating the exclusion area leakage deduction in accordance with Equation 8; for the purposes of subparagraphs (2)(b)(i) and (ii)—by calculating the direct leakage deduction in accordance with Equation 11; for the purposes of subparagraph (2)(b)(iii)—by calculating the private native forests leakage deduction in accordance with Equation 13.

In working out the exceedances mentioned in subsection (2) and the number of Australian carbon credit units to be cancelled in accordance with subsection (4): treat the references in sections 38, 39 and 40 to reporting period i as references to the applicable 2-year period; and for the purposes of paragraph (3)(a)—treat the references in section 39: to the relevant public native forests as references to the project area; and to the direct leakage baseline harvest level as references to the harvest level in the baseline scenario; and

make any other necessary substitutions to the terms used in sections 38, 39 and 40.

The cancellation request must be made:

in accordance with section 64B of the *Australian National Registry of Emissions Units Act 2011*; and

within 120 days of: for the purposes of paragraph (2)(a)—the occurrence of a relevant increase in timber harvesting; or otherwise—the end of the 2-year period in which the relevant increase in timber harvesting occurred.

Before making the cancellation request, the project proponent:

must ensure that it is the registered holder of the number of Australian carbon credit units that must be cancelled; and

if it does not hold that number of Australian carbon credit units—must acquire them.

This section does not apply to a relevant increase in timber harvesting in relation to which:

the Regulator has given the project proponent a notification in accordance with subsection 90(2) of the Act; or

a project proponent for another eligible offsets project covered by this instrument has made a cancellation request in accordance with this section.

Note: A notification in accordance with subsection 90(2) of the Act requires the project proponent to relinquish a specified number of Australian carbon credit units.

#### Division 3.3—Management plan for project area

###### 19 Preparation of management plan

The project proponent must prepare a management plan for the project area.

The management plan must include the following information:

an estimate of the harvest level in the baseline scenario for the project area for each 12 months of the project’s crediting period (in cubic metres (m3) of logs produced and hectares harvested), calculated by reference to Division 5.4;

estimates of the direct leakage baseline harvest level and the private native forests leakage baseline harvest level for the project area for each 12 months of the project’s crediting period (in cubic metres (m3) of logs produced), calculated by reference to Division 5.7;

an estimate of the volume of wood to be extracted from the project area during each 12 months of the project’s crediting period (being a volume that meets the project eligibility requirement of section 12);

information about how the carbon protection areas will be managed and the extent of the reduction in the volume of wood extracted from the project area as a result of the creation of those carbon protection areas.

The management plan must be provided to the Regulator when an application is made for the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project.

###### 20 Amendment and publication of management plan

The project proponent: must update the management plan prepared in accordance with section 19 if a change to the way the project is carried out is likely to result in changes to any of the values mentioned in that section; and may otherwise amend the management plan.

An update to or amendment of the plan is an ***amendment*** of the plan for the purposes of this section (and the ***amended plan*** has a corresponding meaning).

The project proponent must, within 30 days after any amendment of the management plan, notify the Regulator and give the amended plan to the Regulator.

The management plan may be amended at any time before or after the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project.

The amended management plan must include the information required by this Division to be included in the management plan.

An amended management plan cannot increase the volume of wood proposed to be extracted from the project area during each 12 months of the project’s crediting period.

The initial version of the management plan (and the project map) given to the Regulator must be published on the project proponent’s website within 30 days after the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project. Any amended plan (or amended project map) must be published on the proponent’s website within 30 days after it is given to the Regulator.

###### 21 Compliance with management plan

The requirements of this Part for the declaration of a project as an eligible offsets project include the requirement that the public native forests in the project area be managed in accordance with the management plan prepared in accordance with this Division.

Note: Because of this section, the Regulator will be authorised under the legislative rules to unilaterally revoke the declaration of an eligible offsets project if the project proponent does not comply with the management plan.

### Part 4—Crediting period for projects

###### 22 Crediting period for projects of 15 years

For the purposes of paragraph 69(2)(b) of the Act, the crediting period for an eligible offsets project to which this instrument applies is 15 years.

###### 25 Summary of method for calculating net abatement amount

The net abatement amount for a project for a reporting period is calculated in accordance with Equation 1 as the difference between the carbon stock change in the project scenario and the carbon stock change in the baseline scenario, plus the emissions from post-harvest (slash) burns in the baseline scenario, minus the leakage deduction, plus the aggregate negative abatement amount in the aggregate negative abatement account (if any) for all projects covered by this instrument and carried out in the same State.

Note: Carbon abatement is not eligible for Australian carbon credit units unless it meets the hurdle requirement in section 43 for a minimum reduction in the volume of wood extracted from the project area.

The following is a summary of the steps required to calculate the net abatement amount:

**Step 1:** Representative FullCAM model plots must be developed based on the timber harvesting and clearing to facilitate timber harvesting (for example, for access roads, snig tracks and log landings) that occurred in the 5-year period prior to the end of the financial year prior to the making of the application for the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project (the prior period). The model plots must be used to develop a prior period forest estate model that provides estimates of the total net harvested area (in hectares) in, and logs produced (in cubic metres (m3)) from, the project area that are within ±5% of the actual values for the prior period.

**Step 2:** The baseline harvest level, (in cubic metres (m3)) of wood harvested, must then be calculated. The baseline harvest level, in volume of wood harvested, must be converted to a net area subject to harvesting in the baseline scenario (in hectares) using the prior period forest estate model from Step 1. The baseline harvest level and the net harvested area in the baseline scenario are used to model carbon stock changes and emissions in the baseline scenario and are intended to reflect a conservative estimate of the level of harvesting in the project area in the baseline scenario (ie, in the absence of the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project). The area cleared to facilitate timber harvesting in the baseline scenario must also be calculated using the ratio between the cleared area and logs produced (in cubic metres (m3)) in the prior period and the baseline harvest level (in cubic metres (m3)).

**Step 3:** Carbon stock changes in the forest carbon pools (live above- and below-ground biomass and forest debris), and CH4 and N2O emissions associated with post-harvest (slash) burns, in the project and baseline scenarios, are calculated using FullCAM in accordance with the INFM Modelling Guidelines. The modelling must be undertaken using the representative FullCAM model plots derived in accordance with Step 1, supplemented with additional model plots as necessary that reflect clearing events that are not directly related to timber harvesting. Generally, the same representative FullCAM model plots are required to be used in the baseline and project scenarios, with timber harvesting (including post-harvest (slash) burns) and clearing to facilitate timber harvesting included (in the baseline scenario) and excluded (in the project scenario) as relevant to reflect the nature of the project activities. The main exception to this relates to clearing that is not directly related to timber harvesting, which is modelled in the project scenario but excluded from the baseline scenario.

**Step 4:** Carbon stock changes in harvested wood products that are in service are then calculated using FullCAM. In the baseline scenario, the volume of wood modelled as harvested must equal the baseline harvest levels (in cubic metres (m3)) of logs produced, for the reporting period, which includes all log types except residues (for example, firewood). In the project scenario, the log inputs must be the actual logs harvested over the reporting period, excluding pulplogs and residues. The inclusion of pulplogs in the baseline scenario, and their exclusion from the project scenario, promotes conservatism and avoids creating ineligible abatement.

**Step 5:** Carbon stock changes in harvested wood products in landfills must then be calculated using the IPCC first order decay model and the model parameters specified in section 35. The inputs to the first order decay model are derived from the modelling of harvested wood products in service in Step 4, based on the assumption that a proportion of the products that are no longer in service are deposited in landfills. The modelling must be undertaken in accordance with the INFM Modelling Guidelines.

**Step 6:** The leakage deduction for the reporting period is calculated as the exclusion area leakage deduction for the reporting period, plus the direct leakage deduction for the reporting period, plus the private native forests leakage deduction for the reporting period, plus the indirect leakage deduction for the reporting period.

**Step 7:** If the net abatement amount for a reporting period is negative, there are no Australian carbon credit units for the project for the reporting period, and the negative abatement amount is added to the aggregate negative abatement account and then applied to the next project to report under this instrument in the State in which the project is located.

###### 28 Calculating baseline harvest levels, in volume of wood harvested

(1) The harvest level in the baseline scenario (in cubic metres (m3) of logs produced) for the reporting period is the latest modified sustainable yield for the period calculated as follows (the ***modified sustainable yield***):

(i) include sawlogs (high and low quality), pulplogs, veneer logs (including peelers), poles, piles and girders (where they are, or would otherwise be, produced from the forests in the project area); and

###### 41 Indirect leakage deduction (ILDi)

(1) The indirect leakage deduction (in tonnes CO2-e) for a reporting period is the indirect leakage deduction percentage for the reporting period multiplied by the net abatement amount for the reporting period after the application of the other leakage deductions but before the application of the aggregate negative abatement amount (if any).

(2) The indirect leakage deduction percentage is the percentage:

determined by an independent qualified assessor in accordance with this section; and

set out in an indirect leakage assessment report prepared by that assessor and included in the offsets report for the reporting period.

(3) The indirect leakage deduction percentage:

must be expressed as a percentage of the net abatement amount for the reporting period after the application of the other leakage deductions but before the application of the aggregate negative abatement amount (if any); and

must be between 0% and 40%.

(4) The indirect leakage deduction percentage must constitute a conservative estimate of the likely net change in emissions and removals outside the project area because of the reduction, relative to baseline harvest levels, in log production in the project area, except:

any change in emissions and removals referrable to timber harvesting that has been accounted for in another leakage deduction; and

any emissions associated with the harvesting and haulage of substitute logs or with the production of substitute non-wood products.

For this purpose, ***conservative*** means the estimate is more likely to overestimate than underestimate the net increase in emissions and removals.

(5) The emissions and removals taken into account in calculating the indirect leakage deduction percentage are limited to those caused by the substitution in relevant markets of the logs that would have been produced from the project area but for the project (and the wood products that would have been derived from those logs) with the logs, wood products and non-wood products referred to in subsection (6), including emissions and removals related to changes in investment in wood production that result from reductions in log production from the project area.

(6) The substituted logs, wood products and non-wood products are as follows:

logs, and wood products derived from logs, harvested from plantations in Australia;

logs, and wood products derived from logs, harvested from native forests in Australia (other than native forests in the State in which the eligible offsets project is located);

logs, and wood products derived from logs, harvested from plantations or native forests outside of Australia;

non-wood products produced in Australia;

non-wood products produced outside Australia.

(7) The independent qualified assessor must, when preparing the indirect leakage assessment report, take the following matters into consideration:

any change in emissions and removals caused by the substitution of logs, wood products and non-wood products referred to in subsection (6) relative to what is likely to have occurred in the absence of the eligible offsets project;

any increase in log production in public or private native forests in the State in which the project is located relative to the direct leakage baseline harvest level and the private native forests leakage baseline harvest level, respectively;

changes in the supply of wood and non-wood products in relevant markets, and consequential changes to emissions and removals, that are attributable to other eligible offsets projects under this instrument;

changes in the supply of wood and non-wood products in relevant markets, and consequential changes to emissions and removals, that are attributable to the reductions in log production from native forests in other States and that have occurred independently of projects under this instrument;

the potential for reductions in log production from the project area to prompt increased investment in wood production, including from the expansion in the area of plantations and increased wood production in existing plantations and other intensively managed forests;

the impact of distance and haulage costs on the ability of timber mills that previously sourced logs from the project area to source logs from elsewhere, and of government policies to affect haulage costs through subsidies to mills or transporters;

regulatory schemes and policies in or outside Australia that restrict or otherwise limit timber harvesting in native forests, including in accordance with forestry or environmental legislation;

regulatory schemes and policies in or outside Australia that restrict or otherwise limit increases in emissions arising from the substitution of alternative wood and non-wood products (for example, the Safeguard Mechanism under the *National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting Act 2007* and other carbon pricing schemes).

(8) The indirect leakage deduction percentage must be reduced as follows if any plantation is established after the declaration of the eligible offsets project on previously cleared land used for agriculture by or with the support of the State in which the project is located (unless the plantation is the subject of an eligible offsets project covered by another methodology determination):

the reduction is calculated as the increase in the average long-term sequestration in live and dead above and below ground biomass included in carbon pools on land in which the plantation is established;

the average long-term sequestration associated with the plantation must be modelled in FullCAM, using a 100-year scenario in which the plantation is harvested and re-established over the modelling period;

the average long-term sequestration must be allocated in equal portions over 15 years;

the sequestration must be split between projects covered by this instrument in the same State in which the eligible offsets project is located based on the reduction in relevant log production in the project areas relative to the applicable baseline harvest level, in accordance with the INFM Modelling Guidelines.

(9) For the purposes of subsection (8), the support of the relevant State means the provision of government grants or concessional loans, where the value of the resulting subsidy constitutes more than 10% of the present value of the plantation establishment costs, including land costs.

(10) The indirect leakage assessment report must include the following:

a review of peer-reviewed literature on leakage associated with the stopping of timber harvesting that deals with changes in emissions and removals outside a project area because of a reduction in log production caused by a project;

an analysis of the markets supplied with logs from the project area in the 10 years before the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project;

an analysis of the reduction in log production caused by the project (relative to the baseline scenario under this instrument) after taking into account any increase in log production in public or private native forests in the State in which the project is located;

an analysis of the markets that were likely to have been supplied with the logs that have not been produced because of the project;

an analysis of the volume and nature of the substitute products in relevant markets that are likely to have replaced the logs and wood products that would have been supplied but for the project;

an analysis of whether, and the extent to which, timber mills that source or previously sourced logs from the project area have substituted logs from other States, and the nature of the forests from which the substitute logs were sourced;

an analysis of the changes in emissions and removals that are likely to arise from the substitution of products in relevant markets, including those that arise from induced increases in investment in wood production;

details of how each of the above matters have been considered in determining the indirect leakage deduction percentage;

a statement of the magnitude and nature of the uncertainties associated with determining the indirect leakage deduction percentage;

details of the method used to determine the indirect leakage deduction percentage;

the indirect leakage deduction percentage determined for the reporting period;

all data relied on in preparing the indirect leakage assessment report.

(11) In this section, a reference to emissions and removals is a reference to emissions of greenhouse gases and removal of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere by the sequestration of carbon.

if the aggregate negative abatement amount in the account at the end of the reporting period is zero, then ANAAi is zero;

if the aggregate negative abatement amount in the account at the end of the reporting period is less than zero, then ANAAi is equal to the amount in the account.

(4) If the reporting periods for 2 or more projects in the same State end on the same day, the aggregate negative abatement amount must be applied sequentially to the projects, based on the assumption that they report one after the other.

(5) Where a negative abatement amount from the account is applied in the calculation of the net abatement amount for a project, the amount must be deducted from the account.

#### Division 5.9—Hurdle for eligible carbon abatement

###### 43 Minimum reduction in volume of wood extracted from levels in baseline scenario

(1) The net abatement amount in relation to a reporting period is zero if the volume of wood (in cubic metres (m3)) extracted from the project area during each 12 months of the reporting period is not at least 20% less than the levels in the baseline scenario for each such 12-month period.

Note: If the reporting period is greater than 12 months, the hurdle for eligible carbon abatement applies to each 12-month period sequentially, measured for any such 12-month period against the baseline scenario for that 12-month period.

(2) If the reporting period:

is less than 12 months—subsection (1) applies to that shorter period as if the reference to 12 months were a reference to the reporting period; or

is more than 12 months but is not a multiple of 12 months—subsection (1) applies (in addition to applying to each such 12-month period) to the balance of the reporting period as if the reference to 12 months were a reference to the balance of the reporting period.

(3) The net abatement amount in relation to a reporting period is also zero if the volume of wood (in cubic metres (m3)) extracted from the project area during the period from the declaration of the project as an eligible offsets project until the end of the reporting period is not at least 20% less than the levels in the baseline scenario for that period.

(4) This section does not apply if the net abatement amount calculated in accordance with this Part is (but for this section) a negative amount.

#### Division 5.10—Factors and parameters from external sources

###### 44 Factors and parameters from external sources

(1) If a calculation in this instrument includes a factor or parameter that is defined or calculated by reference to another instrument or writing, the factor or parameter to be used for a reporting period is the factor or parameter referred to in, or calculated by reference to, the instrument or writing as in force at the end of the reporting period.

(2) Subsection (1) does not apply if:

this instrument specifies otherwise; or

it is not possible to define or calculate the factor or parameter by reference to the instrument or writing as in force at the end of the reporting period.

(3) If paragraph (2)(b) applies, the proponent must:

ascertain the factor or parameter by reference to an earlier version of the instrument or writing; or

make a conservative estimate of the factor or parameter by reference to the following, as applicable:

any applicable measurement or estimation approaches or requirements in the *National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting (Measurement) Determination 2008*;

any relevant historical data for the project, or other project data that the proponent considers relevant;

any other matters that the proponent considers relevant.

#### Division 5.11—Transparency in abatement calculations

###### 45 Publication of data and methods

(1) The project proponent must publish all necessary information to enable third parties to replicate the calculation of the net abatement amount for each reporting period, including (without limitation) information to replicate:

the representative FullCAM model plots developed in accordance with subsections 27(1) and (3) and the prior period forest estate model; and

the calculations used to derive the modified and unmodified sustainable yields in accordance with section 28; and

the calculation of the hurdle requirement in accordance with section 43.

(2) The information required to be published includes all relevant methods, data, representative FullCAM model plots and forest estate models.

(3) The information is to be published as soon as practicable, and no later than 30 days, after the Regulator has informed the project proponent that the offsets report for the reporting period satisfies the applicable requirements for the offset report.

(4) The information is to be published on the website of the project proponent.

### Part 6—Reporting, record‑keeping, monitoring and notification requirements

Note: The reporting, record‑keeping, monitoring and notification requirements in this Part supplement the general requirements relating to those matters set out in the legislative rules.

#### Division 6.1—Reporting requirements

###### 46 Operation of this Division

For the purposes of paragraphs 106(3)(a) and 76(4)(e) of the Act, this Division sets out reporting requirements for an offsets project to which this instrument applies that is an eligible offsets project.

###### 47 Information required in offsets reports

(1) An offsets report for a reporting period must include:

a copy of the project map for the project; and

the current management plan for the project prepared in accordance with Division 3.3; and

any other forest management plans that are required to be prepared in relation to timber harvesting or forest management activities in the project area as at the end of the reporting period; and

in the case of any carbon protection area—details of the measures taken to promote the effective management of that area (including in relation to the conservation of biodiversity and to the engagement of Aboriginal people in management actions in that area); and

the verification in accordance with subsection 28(7) by the independent qualified assessor of the initial and revised sustainable yields and associated unmodified sustainable yields; and

the indirect leakage assessment report prepared in accordance with section 41 for the reporting period; and

details of the aggregate negative abatement account maintained in accordance with section 42, including details of all additions and deductions from the account in the reporting period.

(2) If, in modelling a management action or disturbance event in FullCAM, the project proponent specified a portion of the project area affected by a FullCAM modelled event, the offsets report must describe how the portion was estimated.

(3) If, in the circumstances described in paragraph 44(2)(b), a factor or parameter is ascertained or estimated in accordance with subsection 44(3), the offsets report about the project for the reporting period must include the following information about the factor or parameter:

if an earlier version of the instrument or writing mentioned in section 44 was used—the version that was used, and the start and end dates of each use;

the reasons why it was not possible to define or calculate the factor or parameter by reference to the instrument or writing as in force at the end of the reporting period.

###### 48 Timing of offsets reports

For the purposes of paragraph 76(4)(e) of the Act, an offsets report for a reporting period must be given to the Regulator within 48 months after the end of the reporting period.

Note: The extended time to submit offsets reports is needed to ensure data on log production from public and private native forests are available. This data is required for the calculation of the direct leakage deduction in accordance with section 39 and private native forests leakage deduction in accordance with section 40.

#### Division 6.2—Record‑keeping requirements

###### 49 Operation of this Division

For the purposes of paragraph 106(3)(c) of the Act, this Division sets out record‑keeping requirements for an offsets project to which this instrument applies that is an eligible offsets project.

###### 50 Records relating to abatement calculations

The project proponent must make and keep records of all information required to be published in accordance with section 45.

###### 51 Records relating to timber harvesting and clearing to facilitate timber harvesting

If timber harvesting was undertaken in the project area during a reporting period, the project proponent must make and keep records that evidence the timber harvesting and areas cleared to facilitate timber harvesting, including the extent of harvesting and clearing and the harvesting practices that were used.

###### 52 Records relating to monitoring harvesting and clearing events

The project proponent must make and keep records that result from the monitoring of harvesting and clearing events in accordance with section 58 and evidence the monitored actions that were undertaken in the project area.

###### 53 Records relating to major disturbance events

If a major disturbance event occurs in the project area during a reporting period, the project proponent must make and keep a record that evidences the event, including the extent and impact of the event.

###### 54 Mapping and imagery of harvested and cleared areas

The records under this Division of harvested and cleared areas must include:

maps of the areas harvested and cleared that are prepared in accordance with the CFI Mapping Guidelines; and

date-stamped and geo-referenced remotely sensed imagery of the harvested and cleared areas.

###### 55 Records relating to aggregate negative abatement account

The project proponent must make and keep records of the aggregate negative abatement account that is required to be established in accordance with section 42, including all additions and deductions from the account.

###### 56 Records to be kept for at least 7 years

The project proponent must retain:

a record required to be kept in accordance with this Division; or

a copy of the record;

for at least 7 years after the record is made.

#### Division 6.3—Monitoring requirements

###### 57 Operation of this Division

For the purposes of paragraph 106(3)(d) of the Act, this Division sets out monitoring requirements for an eligible offsets project covered by this instrument.

###### 58 Monitoring of harvesting, clearing and other activities

The project proponent must monitor the following:

a relevant increase in timber harvesting for the purposes of section 18;

other timber harvesting and clearing events (including post-harvest (slash) burns) in the project area that are required to be modelled in accordance with this instrument in the project scenario;

forest management activities in the project area and, in particular, any such activities in a carbon protection area that may result in the removal of wood and the possible relinquishment of Australian carbon credit units;

whether any timber harvesting or clearing (other than as mentioned in paragraph (a)) occurs in an exclusion area.

#### Division 6.4—Notification requirements

###### 59 Operation of this Division

For the purposes of paragraph 106(3)(b) of the Act, this Division sets out notification requirements for an eligible offsets project covered by this instrument.

###### 60 Matters to be notified to Regulator

The project proponent must notify the Regulator, in writing, of each of the following:

a failure to manage the public native forests in the project area in accordance with the current management plan for the project prepared in accordance with Division 3.3;

the making of an EPBC Act forestry-related decision relating to the project area;

timber harvesting in a carbon protection area or clearing in a carbon protection area that is unrelated to the management of the area;

timber harvesting or clearing, in any part of an exclusion area, of which the project proponent becomes aware;

timber harvesting that exceeds the baseline harvesting level in a financial year;

timber harvesting in which the volume of timber extracted from public native forests covered by section 39 is likely to exceed the direct leakage baseline harvest level in a reporting period;

timber harvesting in which the volume of timber extracted from native forests covered by section 39 is likely to exceed the private native forests leakage baseline harvest level in a reporting period;

a relevant increase in timber harvesting for the purposes of section 18 that has occurred in any 2-year period that is, or is part of, a reporting period for the project.

Note: Sections 81 and 82 of the Act require the proponent to notify the Regulator of a specified natural disturbance or conduct that causes a reversal of the removal of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.

###### 61 When matters must be notified

(1) A matter that is required to be notified in accordance with this Division must be notified as soon as practicable, and no later than 90 days after the matter occurs.

(2) However, the matter mentioned in paragraph 60(h) must be notified within 30 days of the end of the reporting period mentioned in that paragraph.
