###### 3 Authority

This Standard is made under section 514YD of the *Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999*.

###### 4 Definitions

Note: A number of expressions used in this Standard are defined in the Act, including:

(a) action

(b) approved conservation advice

(c) bioregional plan

(d) bioregional guidance plan

(e) designated environmental instrument

(f) impact

(g) management or authorisation framework

(h) NOPSEMA management or authorisation framework

(i) protection statement

(j) recovery plan

(k) ruling

(l) threat abatement plan

In this Standard:

***Act*** means the *Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999* (Cth).

***conservation planning document*** means any of a recovery plan, a threat abatement plan, a protection statement, an approved conservation advice, a designated environmental instrument, a bioregional guidance plan, a bioregional plan, a ruling, or a relevant State or Territory conservation document (however described).

***metadata*** means data or information that describes and adds meaning to other data to support its interpretation.

***protected matter*** means a matter protected by a provision of Part 3 of the EPBC Act.

***provenance*** means the information about the history of data, including lineage, the inputs, entities, and activities by which it was produced.

***suitably qualified expert*** means a person who has appropriate professional qualifications or specialist knowledge, training, skills or experience relevant to the subject matter.

Note: An example of a suitably qualified expert could be an Indigenous traditional owner or group in relation to Traditional Ecological Knowledge.

###### 5 Objective

The objective of this Standard is to ensure that decisions made under the Act are based on appropriate data and information and to extend the availability of credible data and information assets for use in such decisions.

###### 6 Outcomes

The following outcomes of the Standard are intended to promote the objective in section 5:

(a) data and information used in decisions made under the Act is transparently described and representative;

(b) data and information used in decisions made under the Act is comparable and reusable;

(c) data and information used in decisions made under the Act is ethically collected, managed and used.

###### 7 Principles

(1) For subsection 514YD(4) of the Act, the principles by which the objective and outcomes in sections 5 and 6 of this Standard are to be achieved are the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 of this Standard.

(2) Having regard to the objective and outcomes in sections 5 and 6 of this Standard, a decision-maker may be satisfied that:

(a) a decision to approve the taking of an action, taking into account any conditions to be attached to the approval, or to vary such an approval is consistent with the Standard if the data and information provided in, or in relation to, the referral or assessment of the action, is consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 of this Standard;

(b) a decision to approve the taking of an action or class of actions in accordance with an endorsed policy, plan or program, taking into account any conditions to be attached to the approval, or to vary such an approval is consistent with the Standard if:

(i) the data and information on which the report on the impacts to which the assessment relates is based; and

(ii) the data and information on which the endorsed policy, plan or program is based,

is consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 of this Standard;

(c) a management or authorisation framework, or an approval of an action or class of actions in accordance with a management or authorisation framework, is consistent with the Standard if the framework requires the data and information provided in relation to the assessment of the action or class of actions to either:

(i) be consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 of this Standard; or

(ii) meet requirements that are equivalent in effect to the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 of this Standard; or

(iii) meet requirements that are higher in effect than is provided by the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 of this Standard;

(d) a NOPSEMA management or authorisation framework, or an approval of an action in accordance with a NOPSEMA management or authorisation framework, is not inconsistent with the Standard if the framework requires the data and information provided in relation to the assessment of the action to either:

(i) be not inconsistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 of this Standard; or

(ii) meet requirements that are equivalent in effect to the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 of this Standard;

(e) a specified manner of assessment will be consistent with this Standard if the manner of assessment requires the data and information provided in relation to the assessment of the action to be assessed in a way that will allow the Minister to determine whether the data and information is consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 of this Standard;

(f) making or varying a bioregional plan or a bioregional guidance plan is consistent with the Standard if the data and information on which the plan is based is consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 of this Standard.

###### 8 Principle 1 – Representative data and information

(1) Data and information used in, or for the purposes of, making a decision under the Act or an accredited management or authorisation framework must adequately and with suitable accuracy, reflect and describe the context of the relevant protected matters.

(2) Subsection (1) will be satisfied if, either:

(a) The data and information meets all of the following:

(i) the data and information was collected by a method that is robust and, with suitable accuracy, accounts for the context of the relevant protected matter; and

(ii) the data and information was created, modelled or analysed using appropriate scientific or other suitable processes to ensure it represents, with suitable accuracy, the context required for the decision; and

Note: Other suitable processes may include academic and cultural processes and knowledge systems.

(iii) the data and information includes, or is accompanied by, appropriate explanations or mitigation strategies to manage identified uncertainties and gaps; and

(iv) data and information is from or based on authoritative sources relevant to the protected matter (for example published datasets, peer-reviewed information or a suitably qualified expert) or, where no authoritative source for particular data and information is available or accessible, an explanation is provided as to why the selected data and information is accurate and representative; or

(b) If paragraph (a) is not satisfied:

(i) for a bioregional plan or a bioregional guidance plan - there is other evidence available that demonstrates the data and information is representative and suitable for use; or

(ii) for all other decisions – the person providing the data and information has provided other evidence that demonstrates the data and information is representative and suitable for use.

###### 9 Principle 2 – Transparent data and information

(1) Data and information used in, or for the purposes of, making a decision under the Act or an accredited management or authorisation framework must be reliable and sufficiently described to enable informed use.

(2) Subsection (1) will be satisfied if, either:

(a) The data and information meets both of the following:

(i) the data and information includes, or is accompanied by, adequate metadata; and

Note: What is adequate metadata for data and information will depend on the particular context and may include providing, for example, matters such as an identifier, title, description, point of contact, the creator, author or source, the custodian, the rights holder, any relevant licence, any access and use constraints, any sensitive data and information types, an uncertainty statement, description of artificial intelligence use, temporal extent, spatial extent, update frequency, date created or accessed.

(ii) the data and information includes, or is accompanied by, appropriate contextual information, including in relation to:

(A) whether any quality assurance and quality control processes were applied to the data and information and if so, what those processes were and a description of the resulting quality of the data and information;

(B) a description of any technical or scientific methods that were applied to the data and information or used in its creation and an explanation of any deviations from these methods;

(C) the provenance of the data and information;

(D) version and change control; or

(b) If paragraph (a) is not satisfied:

(i) for a bioregional plan or a bioregional guidance plan – there is other evidence available that demonstrates the data and information is reliable, transparent and traceable; or

(ii) for all other decisions – the person providing the data and information has provided other evidence that demonstrates the data and information is reliable, transparent and traceable.

###### 10 Principle 3 – Comparable data and information

(1) Data and information provided for use in, or for the purposes of, making a decision under the Act or an accredited management or authorisation framework must be internally consistent in the practices, formats, structures and measurements applied to enable ease of comparison.

(2) Subsection (1) will be satisfied if, either:

(a) The data and information meets all of the following:

(i) the data and information is structured in a way that is appropriate for the proposal; and

Note: What is appropriate for the proposal will depend on the context and may include using formats, schemas, data models and naming conventions. It may also include presenting the data and information in machine readable electronic format.

(ii) the data and information is presented (for example using standardised measurement systems and established reference systems) in an appropriate way for the protection of the protected matter and is used consistently; and

(iii) data and information uses appropriate and consistent taxonomic, ecological and other relevant classifications; and

(iv) data and information is consistent with references and definitions made in conservation planning documents; or

Note: For the purposes of paragraphs (2)(a)(ii) and (2)(a)(iii), appropriateness should be assessed with consideration of the context and cultural considerations.

(b) If paragraph (a) is not satisfied:

(i) for a bioregional plan or a bioregional guidance plan – there is other evidence that demonstrates the data and information is comparable and is accompanied by metadata or contextual information that documents and describes any inconsistency in the way the data and information has been prepared and provided;

(ii) for all other decisions – the person providing the data and information has provided other evidence that demonstrates the data and information is comparable and is accompanied by metadata or contextual information that documents and describes any inconsistency in the way the data and information has been prepared and provided.

###### 11 Principle 4 – Reusable data and information

(1) Data and information used in, or for the purposes of, making a decision under the Act or an accredited management or authorisation framework must be collected, managed and provided in a way that promotes reuse with minimal additional effort, where practicable.

(2) Subsection (1) will be satisfied if, either:

(a) The data and information meets both of the following:

(i) the data and information is provided in a format that promotes reuse with minimal additional effort; and

(ii) the data and information is accompanied by appropriate information to determine suitability for reuse and to manage reuse, including (where relevant) information about methods and limitations, sensitive data or information categories, attribution, retention and disposal obligations, security controls, licensing and rights and consent arrangements; or

Note: In determining the suitability of data and information for reuse, relevant considerations may include (but are not limited to) applicable legal obligations, cultural governance arrangements, consent arrangements, national interest protections or confidentiality requirements.

(b) If paragraph (a) is not satisfied:

(i) for a bioregional plan or a bioregional guidance plan – there is other evidence that demonstrates the data and information is prepared and provided in a way that supports broader reuse; or

(ii) for all other decisions – the person providing the data and information has provided other evidence that demonstrates the data and information is prepared and provided in a way that supports broader reuse.

###### 12 Principle 5 – Ethical data and information

(1) Data and information used in, or for the purposes of, making a decision under the Act, or an accredited management or authorisation framework, must be collected, managed and used responsibly to minimise harm, protect rights and privacy, and, where possible, incorporate cultural values.

(2) Subsection (1) will be satisfied if, either:

(a) The data and information meets both of the following:

(i) the person providing the data and information has made all reasonable efforts to ensure the data and information has been collected, managed and provided consistently with any requirements of relevant Commonwealth, State or Territory laws; and

(ii) the person providing the data and information has made all reasonable efforts to ensure the data and information has been collected, managed and provided consistently with any relevant ethical data principles; or

Note: For example, the person providing the data has:

sought to identify the appropriate custodian of any culturally sensitive data and information;

acted in a way that is respectful of Indigenous rights and cultural governance, including (where appropriate) establishing Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property agreements;

had regard to opportunities for communities to derive collective benefit from data and information concerning them;

had regard to any relevant rights, interests, consent, permissions or cultural protocols, including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge holders’ cultural authority for the data and information;

provided relevant information about the source, custodianship, permitted uses and any conditions applying to the data and information.

(b) If paragraph (a) is not satisfied:

(i) for a bioregional plan or a bioregional guidance plan – there is other evidence that demonstrates the data and information was treated ethically and managed responsibly; or

(ii) for all other decisions – the person providing the data and information has provided other evidence that demonstrates the data and information was treated ethically and managed responsibly.
