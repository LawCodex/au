## Schedule 1—Description of quality assurance activity

**1 Name of activity**

Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Psychiatrists (RANZCP) – Peer Review Groups (PRGs).

**2 Description of activity**

This declaration applies to the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Psychiatrists (RANZCP) Peer Review Groups (PRGs). PRGs comprise Fellows, Affiliates, Associates of the RANZCP, as well as other medical practitioners, psychologists, and mental health nurses. Participants meet in small, self-selected peer groups to review and assess their professional practice and to receive confidential feedback to support improvement. Activities may be adapted to accommodate different clinical contexts and patient populations. Groups may operate within or across employer boundaries and are often organised by specialty including, child and adolescent psychiatry, forensic psychiatry, and leadership and management. The purpose of PRGs is to improve the quality of medical practice, health services, and patient outcomes.

The objectives of PRGs are to:

promote evidence-based practice by enabling psychiatrists to undertake structured quality improvement activities across clinical care, service systems, and procedures.

support continuous professional development through opportunities for self-review and constructive feedback to enhance patient outcomes.

foster a safe and open environment for psychiatrists to discuss work-related challenges and share solutions.

encourage peer collaboration by facilitating advice, knowledge exchange, and joint efforts to improve practice and consumer outcomes.

The activity is delivered by the RANZCP, which is the organisation responsible for training, educating, and representing psychiatrists in Australia and New Zealand. It collates de-identified information from PRG surveys to identify trends, recurring themes, and the impact on clinical practice. RANZCP publicly reports aggregated findings and recommendations at RANZCP meetings to inform changes to psychiatric practice and assess effectiveness.
