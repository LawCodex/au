###### 4 Definitions

Note: A number of expressions used in this instrument are defined in the Act, including the following:

(a) Classification level

(b) Classification type

(c) Service group

(d) Tier amount

In this instrument:

***Act*** means the *Aged Care Act 2024*.

***Age-sex group*** means a category of persons of a specific age (in one-year increments) and sex.

***Anticipated tier value*** for the service groups assistive technology and home modifications: subsection 15(2).

***approvals data*** means data produced by the Department for the previous financial year that specifies the number of older persons approved for residential permanent care and residential respite care by age, and sex.

***average AT place cost*** is the number worked out as follows:

(a) calculate the sum of all tier amounts under sections 212-5 and 212-10 of the Rules applicable to individuals who have an approval in effect for the service group assistive technology under section 65 of the Act;

(b) divide the number at paragraph (a) by the number of individuals to whom that amount relates.

***average HM place cost*** is the number worked out as follows:

(a) calculate the total of all tier amounts under section 221-5 of the Rules applicable to individuals who have an approval in effect for the service group home modifications under section 65 of the Act;

(b) divide the number at paragraph (a) by the number of individuals to whom that amount relates.

***average SAH place cost***: see subsection 8(2).

***committed expenditure*** for a particular service group, classification type or classification level, means the amount of funding calculated by the Department to be required for all individuals who have an approval in effect under section 65 of the Act and have been allocated a place under section 92 of the Act for that service group, classification type or classification level, for the financial year, including subsidies, fee reductions and supplements related to each individual.

***financial year*** means the 2026-2027 financial year commencing on 1 July 2026 and ending on 30 June 2027.

***forecast expenditure*** for a period, means the Department’s estimated expenditure for the period based on committed expenditure, consideration of the wait list and forecast new approvals.

***general population data*** means the population data in the Annual Population Statement produced by Treasury for Australians aged 65 and over and sorted by age and sex.

***previous financial year*** means the 2025-2026 financial year commencing on 1 July 2025 and ending on 30 June 2026.

***Rules*** means the *Aged Care Rules 2025*.

***special appropriation*** means the amount of funding agreed by the Government of the Commonwealth to be allocated for the financial year to fund places in a service group, classification type or classification level as authorised by a provision within the Act.

###### 5 Simplified outline of this instrument

This instrument is made under subsection 91(1) of the Act and provides a method for the

financial year 2026-2027 to make a point in time calculation of:

how many places remain available to be allocated to individuals for the service groups home support, residential care, assistive technology and home modification;

how many of those places for each service group are available for allocation to either the ongoing or short-term classification types; and

how many of those places for each classification type are available for allocation to a particular classification level.

For the service group home support, this instrument also provides a method for calculating

how many of the places are available for allocation as interim places or full places.

For some classification types and classification levels, the places available will be up to the

number of places available for the service group until the relevant budget is fully utilised.

This means that there is no cap on how many places are available for that classification type

or classification level, other than the number of places available for their respective service

group.

## Part 2—Home support

###### 6 Method for working out the number of places available for the service group home support

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(a) of the Act, the method for working out the number of places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for the service group home support is to calculate the sum of the available places for the following classification levels and classification types in the following order:

(a) the classification level SAH restorative care pathway for the classification type short-term for the service group home support as calculated under paragraph 10(1)(d);

(b) the classification level SAH end-of-life pathway for the classification type short-term for the service group home support as calculated under paragraph 9(1)(b); and

(c) the classification type ongoing for the service group home support as calculated under paragraph 8(1)(c).

###### 7 Method for working out the number of places available for the classification type short-term for the service group home support

For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(b) of the Act, the method for working out the number of places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for the classification type short-term for the service group home support is that the number of places available is the number of places available for the service group as worked out by section 6.

###### 8 Method for working out the number of places available for the classification type ongoing for the service group home support

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(b) of the Act, the method for working out the number of places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for the classification type ongoing for the service group home support is the following:

(a) subtract the following amounts from the special appropriation for the service group home support:

(i) the committed expenditure for service group home support, classification type ongoing;

(ii) the SAH restorative care pathway special appropriation; and

(iii) the committed expenditure for the SAH end-of-life pathway

(iv) the committed expenditure for the SAH restorative care pathway insofar as it relates to supplements for individuals who meet the criteria under subsections 196-15(1), 196-20(1) or 196-25(1) of the Rules.

(b) multiply the average SAH place cost by the remaining days in the financial year.

(c) divide the number worked out at (a) by the number worked out at (b).

(2) The ***average SAH place cost*** is the amount calculated by:

(a) calculating the sum of the following amounts that apply to individuals who have had a full place for the classification type ongoing in the service group home support in effect during the financial year:

(i) the base individual amounts under subsection 194-5(2) of the Rules;

(ii) any primary person-centred supplements under section 196-10 of the Rules;

(iii) the provider-based subsidy under section 204-5 of the Rules;

(iv) the provider-based supplement under 205-15 of the Rules;

(b) calculating the sum of all primary person-centred supplements that apply to individuals who have been allocated a place for the classification level restorative care pathway in the classification type short-term in the service group home support;

(c) sum the numbers in paragraphs (a) and (b);

(d) divide the amount worked out in paragraph (c) by the number of full places in effect for the classification type ongoing for the service group home support .

(3) Despite subsection (2), if paragraph 13(1)(a) applies, the average SAH place cost is the amount calculated in accordance with subsection 8(2) but with the amounts at subparagraphs 8(2)(a)(i) and 8(2)(a)(iii) multiplied by 60%.

###### 9 Method for working out the number of places available for the classification level SAH end-of-life pathway for the classification type short-term for the service group home support

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(c) of the Act, the method for working out the number of places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for the classification level SAH end-of-life pathway for the classification type short-term for the service group home support is to identify the lesser of:

(a) the number of individuals who have an access approval in effect for the classification level SAH end-of-life pathway for the classification type short-term for the service group home support but not yet been allocated a place; or

(b) the number calculated by subtracting the committed expenditure for the service group home support from the special appropriation for home support and dividing that number by the SAH end-of life funding value.

(2) The ***SAH end of life funding value*** is the number of days for initial credit prescribed by paragraph 195-1(a) of the Rules multiplied by the base individual amount for the SAH end-of-life pathway prescribed by item 2 of subsection 194-10(2) of the Rules.

###### 10 Method for working out the number of places available for the classification level SAH restorative care pathway for the classification type short-term for the service group home support

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(c) of the Act, the method for working out the number of places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for the classification level SAH restorative care pathway for the classification type short-term for the service group home support is the following:

(a) calculate the committed expenditure for the classification level SAH restorative care pathway for the classification type short-term for the service group home support minus any amounts relating to supplements for individuals who meet the criteria under subsections 196-15(1), 196-20(1) or 196-25(1) of the Rules;

(b) divide the number at paragraph (a) by the restorative care funding value;

(c) subtract the number at paragraph (b) from the quarterly limit;

(d) if the number worked out at paragraph (c) is:

(i) less than 1, then there are no places available; and

(ii) more than or equal to 1, the number of places available for allocation for the classification level SAH restorative care pathway for the classification type short-term for the service group home support is that number.

(2) The ***restorative care funding value*** is the maximum period of effect prescribed by section 80-55 of the rules multiplied by the base individual amount for the classification level SAH restorative care pathway for the classification type short-term for the service group home support prescribed by item 1 of subsection 194-10(2) of the Rules.

(3) The **quarters** and their **quarterly limits** are

| Quarter | Quarterly limit |
|---|---|
| 1 July 2026 – 31 October 2026 | 5,000 |
| 1 November 2026 – 31 December 2026 | 10,000 |
| 1 January 2027 – 31 March 2027 | 15,000 |
| 1 April 2027 – 30 June 2027 | 20,000 |

###### 11 Method for working out the number of places available for the transitional classification levels for the classification type short-term for the service group home support

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(c) of the Act, the method for working out the number of places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for the following classification levels for the classification type short-term for the service group home support is that the number of places available is zero:

(a) STRC class;

(b) HSST NATSIFACP;

(c) HSST MPSP.

###### 12 Method for working out the number of places available for all classification levels for the classification type ongoing for the service group home support

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(c) of the Act, the method for working out the number of places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for all classification levels for the classification type ongoing for the service group home support is the following:

(a) for the classification levels SAH Classes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 and classification levels HCP class 1, 2, 3 and 4, for the classification type ongoing for the service group home support, the number of places for each classification level is up to the number of places available for the classification type ongoing for the service group home support as worked out by section 8, allocated until such point as the special appropriation for the classification type is fully utilised; and

(b) for the following classification levels for the classification type ongoing for the service group home support, the number of places is zero:

(i) CHSP class;

(vi) CHSP transitional;

(vii) HSO NATSIFACP; and

(viii) HSO MPSP;

###### 13 Method for working out the number of interim places available for the service group home support

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(d) of the Act, the method for working out the number of interim places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for all classification levels for the classification types ongoing and short-term for the service group home support in this part is that:

(a) if the forecast expenditure exceeds the special appropriation for the classification type ongoing for the service group home support, 100 % of places available for allocation for classification levels SAH Classes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 and classification levels HCP class 1, 2, 3, and 4, of the classification type ongoing for the service group home support are available to be interim places; and

(b) 0 % of places available for allocation for the classification levels SAH restorative care pathway and SAH end-of-life pathway for the classification type short term for the service group home support are available to be interim places.

###### 14 Method for working out the number of full places available for the service group home support

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(d) of the Act, the method for working out the number of full places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for all classification levels for the classification types ongoing and short-term for the service group home support in this part is that:

(a) if the forecast expenditure does not exceed the special appropriation for the classification type ongoing for the service group home support, 100 % of places available for allocation for the classification levels SAH Classes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 and classification levels HCP class 1, 2, 3, and 4, for classification type ongoing for the service group home support are available to be full places.

(b) 100% of places available for allocation for the classification levels SAH restorative care pathway and SAH end-of-life pathway for the classification type short-term, are available to be full places.

## Part 3—Assistive Technology

###### 15 Method for working out the number of places available for the service group assistive technology

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(a) of the Act, the method for working out the number of places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for service group assistive technology is the following:

(a) calculate the ***anticipated tier value*** in accordance with subsection (2);

(b) subtract the amount at (a) from the special appropriation for the service groups home modifications and assistive technology;

(c) divide the number at paragraph (b) by the remaining number of months in the financial year, including the current month;

*Example: If it is March, the remaining number of months in the financial year is four*

(d) calculate the ***demand for assistive technology*** by:

(i) summing the number of individuals allocated a place during this financial year for the service group assistive technology with the number of individuals allocated a place for the service group home modifications;

(ii) dividing the number of individuals allocated a place during this financial year for the service group assistive technology by number the number at subparagraph (i);

(e) calculate the ***demand for home modifications*** by subtracting the number worked out by the demand for assistive technology from 1;

(f) multiply the demand for assistive technology by the average AT place cost;

(g) multiply the demand for home modification by the average HM place cost;

(h) add the numbers worked out at paragraphs (f) and (g) to calculate a weighted average place cost;

(i) divide the number worked out at paragraph (c) by the number worked out at paragraph (h);

(j) multiple the number at paragraph (i) by the demand for assistive technology.

## Part 5—Residential care

###### 22 Method for working out the number of places available for the service group residential care

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 91(1)(a) of the Act, the method for working out the number of places available to be allocated to individuals at a time in the financial year for the service group residential care is the following:

(a) calculate the percentage approval rate for each age-sex group for the ages 65 to 100 for the previous financial year by:

(i) dividing the number of people approved in the age-sex group according to the approvals data by the number of people in the same age-sex group according to the general population data for the previous financial year; and

(ii) multiplying that number by 100;

*Example: If 250 females aged 80 were approved and there are 10,000 females aged 80 in the population, the approval rate is 2.5% (250 divided by 10,000 multiplied by 100) for the age-sex group 80 year old females.*

(b) calculate the projected number of places for each age-sex group for the ages 65 to 100 for the financial year by multiplying the percentage approval rate calculated at paragraph (a) by the general population data for the financial year for the same age-sex group;

*Example: If the approval rate is 2.5% for the age-sex group 80 year old females and there are 12,000 females aged 80 in the population this financial year, the projected number of places for the age-sex group 80 year old females is 300 (2.5% multiplied by 12,000).*

(c) sum the projected number of places for each age-sex group;

(d) subtract the number of places that have already been allocated this financial year.

(2) For the purposes of paragraph (a):

(a) all males aged 100 or over will form part of the age-sex group for 100 year old males; and

(b) all females aged 100 or over will form part of the age-sex group for 100 year old females.

## Part 6—Miscellaneous

###### 26 Calculations to be rounded down

Where a method of calculating the number of places available for allocation for a service group, classification type or classification level produces a number that is not a whole number, it must be rounded down to the nearest whole number.
