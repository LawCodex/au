###### 3 Authority

This Standard is made under section 514YD of the *Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999*.

###### 4 Definitions

Note: A number of expressions used in this Standard are defined in the Act, including:

(a) action

(b) advanced restoration action

(c) approved conservation advice

(d) bioregional guidance plan

(e) bioregional plan

(f) bioregional plan restoration contribution

(g) bioregional restoration action

(h) bioregional restoration measure

(i) designated environmental instrument

(j) impact

(k) impacted protected matter

(l) management or authorisation framework

(m) protection statement

(n) recovery plan

(o) residual significant impact

(p) restoration contribution charge

(q) Restoration Contributions Holder

(r) ruling

(s) threat abatement plan

In this Standard:

***Act*** means the *Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999* (Cth).

***advanced offset activity*** means:

(a) an offset activity that is registered as an advanced restoration action under regulations made for the purposes of section 170CAA of the Act; or

(b) an offset activity that is being, or has been carried out by a person before the approval of the taking of the action or class of actions, or the making of the bioregional plan (whether or not the offset activity is registered as an advanced offset (however described) under a Commonwealth, State or Territory law).

***affected protected matter*** means a protected matter that will or may be damaged by a residual significant impact of an action or class of actions.

***approved State or Territory offset*** means an activity required or authorised to be carried out pursuant to a law of a State or Territory to compensate for damage that will or may be caused by impacts to an affected protected matter as a result of an action or class of actions.

***conservation planning document*** means any of a recovery plan, a threat abatement plan, a protection statement, an approved conservation advice, a designated environmental instrument, a bioregional guidance plan, a bioregional plan, a ruling, or a relevant State or Territory conservation document (however described).

***direct offset*** means a compensatory mechanism that is used to directly protect, conserve, restore or recover a protected matter.

***durable*** means capable of withstanding pressure to last a long time.

***indirect offset*** means a compensatory mechanism that is used to indirectly protect, conserve, restore or recover a protected matter and may include scientific research and education on existing, new or emerging threats to the protected matter (or a financial contribution towards such research or education).

***like-for-like result*** means a result which protects, conserves, restores or recovers the same kind of environmental feature, attribute or habitat of an affected protected matter that will or may be damaged.

***offset activity*** means the doing of any thing or the taking of any measure (including a restoration action, advanced offset activity, or a bioregional restoration action), other than the payment of a restoration contribution charge or a bioregional plan restoration contribution, to compensate for the damage that may or will be caused by a residual significant impact of an action or class of actions on a protected matter.

Note: For classes of actions, an offset activity will be used to compensate for the damage that may be caused by a residual significant impact that the class of actions as a whole will have or is likely to have on an affected protected matter. The Act does not require that residual significant impacts of individual actions in the class of actions are identified and compensated for.

***protected matter*** means a matter protected by a provision of Part 3 of the Act.

Note: The matters protected by a provision of Part 3 of the Act are set out in section 34 of the Act.

***relevant impact site baseline for the affected protected matter*** means the likely condition of the protected matter at the sites where the residual significant impact will or is likely to occur that is:

(a) evidence-based; and

(b) determined at the time of the decision to approve the taking of the action or class of actions, vary the conditions of the approval or the making or varying of the bioregional plan (as relevant).

Note: Where a residual significant impact is of a class of actions as a whole, determining the relevant impact site baseline for the affected protected matter at the time of the making or varying of the approval or bioregional plan (as relevant) may involve the use of evidence-based assumptions.

***relevant offset site baseline for the affected protected matter*** means the likely condition of the protected matter at the site of the offset activity that:

(a) is evidence-based; and

(b) is determined:

(i) where the offset activity is an advanced offset activity – at the time the offset activity commenced;

(ii) where the offset activity is being delivered out of a State or Territory offset fund (however described) – at an appropriate time;

(iii) for all other offset activities – either:

(I) at the time of the decision to approve the taking of the action or class of actions, vary the conditions of the approval, or the making or varying of the bioregional plan (as relevant); or

(II) prior to the offset activity commencing and in accordance with an appropriate process for determining the baseline.

Note: Where an offset activity is used to compensate for the damage that may or will be caused by a residual significant impact of a class of actions as a whole, determining the relevant offset site baseline for the affected protected matter at the time of the making or varying of the approval or bioregional plan (as relevant) may involve the use of evidence-based assumptions.

***required net gain*** means the net gain for the affected protected matter within the meaning of section 527K of the Act and is measured relative to the relevant impact site baseline for the affected protected matter.

***suitably qualified expert*** means a person who has appropriate professional qualifications or specialist knowledge, training, skills or experience relevant to the subject matter of the offset activity.

Note: An example of a suitably qualified expert could be an indigenous traditional owner or group in relation to offset activities relating to indigenous heritage.

###### 5 Objective

The objective of this Standard is to ensure that offset activities (where permitted) compensate for the residual significant impacts of an action or class of actions on an affected protected matter to deliver the required net gain in a way that contributes to the protection, conservation, restoration and recovery of that protected matter.

Note: The required net gain for a protected matter is the net gain for that protected matter in section 527K of the Act (see section 4). It sets the amount of gain that is required to compensate for the damage that may or will be caused by a residual significant impact of an action or class of actions to that protected matter.

###### 6 Outcomes

The following outcomes of this Standard are intended to promote the objective in section 5:

(a) offset activities compensate for the damage that will or may be caused by residual significant impacts of actions or classes of actions on affected protected matters to protect, conserve, restore or recover that protected matter;

(b) offset activities provide a measurable improvement to the affected protected matter (compared to the relevant offset site baseline for the affected protected matter) that is equal to or greater than the required net gain for the protected matter; and

(c) offset activities provide a high level of confidence that the long-term survival and continuation of the affected protected matter will be promoted and enhanced.

###### 7 Principles

(1) For subsection 514YD(4) of the Act, the principles by which the outcomes and objectives in section 5 and section 6 of this Standard are to be achieved are the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 of this Standard.

(2) Where the decision relates to approving the taking of an action or class of actions under Part 9 or 10 of the Act, the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 of this Standard only apply in relation to an offset activity that will be used to compensate for the damage that will or may be caused by a residual significant impact of an action or class of actions on an affected protected matter where the affected protected matter is a matter protected by a provision of Part 3 that is a controlling provision for the action or an action in the class of actions.

(3) Having regard to the objectives and outcomes in sections 5 and 6 of this Standard, a decision-maker may be satisfied that:

(a) a decision to approve the taking of an action or class of actions, or to vary such an approval, taking into account any conditions to be attached to the approval, is consistent with this Standard if the damage that will or may be caused by any residual significant impacts of the action or class of actions on an affected protected matter will be compensated by:

(i) an offset activity that is consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 of this Standard; or

(ii) the payment of a restoration contribution charge;

(b) a management or authorisation framework, or an approval of an action or class of actions in accordance with a management or authorisation framework, is consistent with this Standard if the framework requires the damage that may or will be caused by any residual significant impacts of the action or class of actions on an affected protected matter to be compensated by an offset activity that:

(i) is consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 of this Standard; or

(ii) meets requirements that are equivalent in effect to the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 of this Standard; or

(iii) meets requirements that provide a higher level of protection to the affected protected matter than is provided by the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 of this Standard.

(c) a specified manner of assessment will be consistent with this Standard if the manner of assessment requires an action or class of actions to be assessed in a way that will allow the Minister to determine whether the damage that will or may be caused by any residual significant impacts of the action or class of actions on an affected protected matter will be compensated by an offset activity that is consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 of this Standard;

(d) the making or varying of a bioregional plan is consistent with this Standard if the bioregional plan (or the bioregional plan as varied) requires the damage that may or will be caused by any residual significant impacts of a priority class of actions to be taken under the bioregional plan on an impacted protected matter to be compensated by a bioregional restoration measure that meets any of the following:

(i) the bioregional restoration measure is:

(A) a bioregional restoration action that is required under the bioregional plan to be delivered by a person other than the Restoration Contributions Holder; and

(B) consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 of this Standard; or

(ii) the bioregional restoration measure is a bioregional plan restoration contribution; or

(iii) the bioregional restoration measure is a bioregional plan restoration action that is required, under the bioregional plan, to be delivered by the Restoration Contributions Holder.

Note: A bioregional restoration measure may be a bioregional restoration action or a bioregional plan restoration contribution (see sections 528 and 177AH of the Act).

(4) For the purposes of subsection 7(3), where an offset activity involves the payment of money into a State or Territory offset fund (however described), the offset activity is consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 of this Standard if the State or Territory framework that establishes the offset fund requires the money to be spent on an offset activity that is consistent with the principles in sections 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 of this Standard.

###### 8 Principle 1—Feasibility

(1) An offset activity must be feasible.

(2) An offset activity will be feasible if there is a high level of confidence that the offset activity:

(a) will contribute to the protection, conservation, restoration or recovery of the affected protected matter; and

(b) will, on its own or in combination with other offset activities or the payment of a restoration contribution charge or a bioregional plan restoration contribution, result in the required net gain for the affected protected matter; and

(c) will achieve the intended result in a timeframe that is relevant to the protected matter, having regard to the damage to the protected matter from the residual significant impact.

(3) The required high level of confidence referred to in subsection (2) must be based on appropriate evidence.

(4) Appropriate evidence may include:

(a) evidence from a suitably qualified expert; or

(b) existing peer reviewed science; or

(c) information or data that is provided to the public by the Head of Environment Information Australia; or

(d) information or data obtained from the management of another offset activity relating to the affected protected matter by the person or another person; or

(e) other evidence that demonstrates the matters in subsection (2).

###### 9 Principle 2—Security

(1) An offset activity must be securely protected.

Note: An offset site that has an existing legal protection mechanism in place is not prevented from being used for additional offset activities.

(2) An offset activity will be securely protected if there is an appropriate protection mechanism in place, or the process for obtaining such a mechanism is demonstrably underway, that provides a high level of confidence that:

(a) the required measurable improvement will be delivered; and

(b) where the offset activity is a direct offset, the result of the offset activity will be maintained for the applicable maintenance period.

Note: See section 11 of this Standard for the required measurable improvement.

(3) The required high level of confidence referred to in subsection (2) must be based on appropriate evidence.

(4) Appropriate evidence may include:

(a) evidence from a suitably qualified expert; or

(b) existing peer reviewed science; or

(c) information or data that is provided to the public by the Head of Environment Information Australia; or

(d) information or data obtained from the management of another offset activity relating to the affected protected matter by the person or another person; or

(e) other evidence that demonstrates the matters in subsection 9(2).

(5) For the purposes of paragraph (2)(b), the ***applicable maintenance period*** is the period after the result of the offset activity has been delivered and during which the result of the offset activity must be maintained and specifically:

(a) where damage to the affected protected matter is short term or temporary – the earlier of:

(i) 20 years; and

(ii) until the result of the offset activity becomes durable;

(b) where the damage to the affected protected matter is long term or permanent – the earlier of:

(i) the period for which the approval has effect; or

(ii) until the result of the offset activity becomes durable.

(6) Despite paragraph (5)(b), where:

(a) the offset activity is a bioregional restoration action that is required under the bioregional plan to be delivered by a person other than the Restoration Contributions Holder; and

(b) the damage to the affected protected matter is long-term or permanent,

the ***applicable maintenance period*** is the period that the offset activity must be maintained as set out in the bioregional plan.

(7) The result of an offset activity becomes durable at the point at which maintenance activities are no longer needed to maintain that result.

Note: Durable results will not be achieved for all offset activities, and where this is the case, continuation of maintenance activities will be required for the duration of the approval or the period set out in the bioregional plan (as relevant).

(8) The criteria for when the result of the offset activity becomes durable must be provided, at an appropriate time, in:

(a) a management plan; or

(b) where an approval under section 146B of the Act requires the offset activity to be delivered – either a management plan or the endorsed policy, plan or program.

Note: A bioregional plan may set out when management plans are to be provided.

(9) For offset activities that are direct offsets – mechanisms that provide legal protections should be used when available and feasible.

Note 1: An example of an offset activity that is a direct offset is the protection of land.

Note 2: Examples of mechanisms that provide legal protections (where appropriate) include: covenants on land where the offset activity will occur, purchasing land, the use of State or Territory-based offset schemes, the use of statutory-based nature repair markets, public protected areas, indigenous land use agreements and native title determinations.

(10) For offset activities that are direct offsets – an alternative protection mechanism may be used if all of the following apply:

(a) a mechanism that provides legal protections is not available or feasible; and

(b) there is a low risk that other activities will prevent or compromise the offset activity being delivered or maintained for the applicable maintenance period; and

(c) the alternative protection mechanism identifies protection, conservation, restoration or recovery of the affected protected matter as a management objective.

Note: Examples of alternative protection mechanisms may include voluntary property registration in formal government programs, stewardship agreements, long term land management contracts, and Other Effective area-based Conservation Measures.

(11) The matters in subsection (10) must be based on appropriate evidence.

(12) Appropriate evidence may include:

(a) evidence from a suitably qualified expert; or

(b) existing peer reviewed science; or

(c) information or data that is provided to the public by the Head of Environment Information Australia; or

(d) information or data obtained from the management of another offset activity relating to the affected protected matter by the person or another person; or

(e) other evidence that demonstrates the matters in subsection (10).

(13) For offset activities that are indirect offsets – the appropriate protection mechanism may relate to security of the funding or reliable expertise for the offset activity.

Note: An example of an indirect offset includes research or specialist work.

###### 10 Principle 3—Tangible

(1) An offset activity must provide a tangible benefit to the affected protected matter.

(2) An offset activity will provide a tangible benefit to the affected protected matter if there is a high level of confidence that the offset activity both:

(a) where the result of the offset activity is:

(i) quantifiable – provides a benefit to the affected protected matter that is quantifiable; or

(ii) qualitative – provides a benefit to the affected protected matter that is qualitative; and

(b) contributes to the protection, conservation, restoration or recovery of the affected protected matter.

(3) The required high level of confidence referred to in subsection (2) must be based on appropriate evidence.

(4) Appropriate evidence may include:

(a) evidence from a suitably qualified expert; or

(b) existing peer reviewed science; or

(c) information or data that is provided to the public by the Head of Environment Information Australia; or

(d) information or data obtained from the management of another offset activity relating to the affected protected matter by the person or another person; or

(e) other evidence that demonstrates the matters in subsection (2).

(5) An offset activity must be a direct offset unless a relevant conservation planning document identifies an indirect offset as a higher priority measure for the affected protected matter.

###### 11 Principle 4—Measurable improvements

(1) There must be a high level of confidence that an offset activity will deliver a measurable improvement to the affected protected matter relative to the relevant offset site baseline for the affected protected matter.

Note 1: Measurable improvement is what is expected to occur at the offset site from delivery of the offset activity.

Note 2: A residual significant impact of an action or class of actions may be compensated by one or more offset activities such that the total measurable improvement across all offset activities meets the required net gain.

(2) The required high level of confidence referred to in subsection (1) must be based on appropriate evidence.

(3) Appropriate evidence may include:

(a) evidence from a suitably qualified expert; or

(b) existing peer reviewed science; or

(c) information or data that is provided to the public by the Head of Environment Information Australia; or

(d) information or data obtained from the management of another offset activity relating to the affected protected matter by the person or another person; or

(e) other evidence that demonstrates the matter in subsection (1).

###### 12 Principle 5—Additionality

(1) An offset activity must be likely to deliver an additional benefit to the affected protected matter that would not have otherwise occurred.

(2) An offset activity that is required to be undertaken by another law, or would otherwise have occurred in the absence of the action being taken, does not deliver an additional benefit.

(3) Despite subsection (2), an offset activity that is an approved State or Territory offset that applies in relation to the same action or class of actions and the same affected protected matter may meet the requirement in subsection (1).

(4) Despite subsection (2), an offset activity that is an advanced offset activity is not precluded from delivering an additional benefit.

(5) Despite subsection (2), an offset activity that is located on a site that was previously, or is currently, being managed for an environmental benefit is not precluded from delivering an additional benefit.

(6) The matters in subsection (1) must be based on appropriate evidence.

(7) Appropriate evidence may include:

(a) evidence from a suitably qualified expert; or

(b) existing peer reviewed science; or

(c) information or data that is provided to the public by the Head of Environment Information Australia; or

(d) information or data obtained from the management of another offset activity relating to the affected protected matter by the person or another person; or

(e) other evidence that demonstrates the matters in subsection (1).

###### 13 Principle 6—Like-for-like

(1) An offset activity must compensate for the damage that will or may be caused by a residual significant impact of an action or class of actions on the affected protected matter by delivering a like-for-like result for that protected matter.

(2) Despite subsection (1), an offset activity that will be used to compensate for damage to the ecological character of a declared Ramsar wetland is not required to be directed at the particular component of the ecological character that has been, is being or is likely to be damaged.

(3) Despite subsection (1), an offset activity that does not deliver a like-for-like result will be permitted if the decision-maker is satisfied that both:

(a) the offset activity will deliver a greater benefit for the affected protected matter than if a like-for-like result was delivered; and

(b) the offset activity is consistent with the priorities for protection, conservation, restoration or recovery of the affected protected matter set out in a conservation planning document.

(4) The matter in paragraph (3)(a) must be demonstrated by appropriate evidence.

(5) Appropriate evidence may include:

(a) evidence from a suitably qualified expert; or

(b) existing peer reviewed science; or

(c) information or data that is provided to the public by the Head of Environment Information Australia; or

(d) information or data obtained from the management of another offset activity relating to the affected protected matter by the person or another person; or

(e) other evidence that demonstrates the matter in paragraph (3)(a).

###### 14 Principle 7—Relevant area

(1) An offset activity must be located in an area that is relevant to the affected protected matter.

(2) An offset activity will be located in an area that is relevant to the affected protected matter if:

(a) the offset activity is located near to the area in which the residual significant impact on the affected protected matter has or will occur; or

(b) where it is not reasonably practicable for paragraph (2)(a) to be satisfied, or where the offset activity is a bioregional restoration action:

(i) the offset activity is located in an area that is relevant to the protected matter; and

(ii) the locating of the offset activity in the area is likely to result in an equivalent or greater benefit for the affected protected matter.

(3) The matters in paragraph (2)(b) must be demonstrated by appropriate evidence.

(4) Appropriate evidence may include:

(a) evidence from a suitably qualified expert; or

(b) existing peer reviewed science; or

(c) information or data that is provided to the public by the Head of Environment Information Australia; or

(d) information or data obtained from the management of another offset activity relating to the affected protected matter by the person or another person; or

(e) other evidence that demonstrates the matters in paragraph (2)(b).

###### 15 Principle 8—Offset commenced prior to impact

(1) An offset activity that will be used to compensate for the damage that will or may be caused by a residual significant impact of an action or class of actions on the affected protected matter must be secured and registered, and management of the offset activity must have commenced, before the relevant residual significant impact occurs.

Note: Where an action or class of actions is to be delivered in distinct stages over an extended time period, an offset activity may be identified for each stage of the development, and those offset activities must commence prior to the impact(s) occurring in relation to the relevant stage.

(2) Despite subsection (1), the relevant residual significant impact may occur if the process of obtaining security for the offset activity is demonstrably underway and it is reasonable in the circumstances for the impact to occur.

(3) The requirements in subsections (1) and (2) must be demonstrated by appropriate evidence.

(4) Appropriate evidence may include:

(a) a plan and timeframe for securing, managing and registering the offset activity; or

(b) other evidence that demonstrates that the matters in subsections (1) and (2) will be satisfied.

(5) Despite subsection (1), where the offset activity involves the payment of money into a State or Territory offset fund (however described), only the payment must be made before the relevant residual significant impact occurs.

Note: This means the offset activity on which the money paid into the fund will be ultimately spent is not required to be identified, secured or registered before the relevant residual significant impact occurs.

(6) Despite subsection (1), where an approval under section 146B of the Act requires the offset activity to be delivered, the offset activity must be secured and delivered in accordance with any timeframes set out in:

(a) the approval; and

(b) the endorsed policy, plan or program to which the approval relates.

(7) Despite subsection (1), where the offset activity is a bioregional restoration action that is required under the bioregional plan to be delivered by a person other than the Restoration Contributions Holder, the offset activity must be secured and registered, and management of the offset activity must have commenced, in accordance with any timeframes set out in the relevant bioregional plan.

(8) For the purposes of subsection (1), an offset activity is ***registered*** if the offset is included in a register of offsets established by a Commonwealth, State or Territory government.
