# AD/ENG/5 Amdt 10 – Turbine Engine Continuing Airworthiness Requirements

AIRWORTHINESS DIRECTIVE

On the commencement date specified below, and for the reasons set out in the background section, the CASA delegate whose signature appears below repeals Airworthiness Directive (AD) AD/ENG/5 Amdt 9 and issues the following AD under subregulation 39.001 (1) of CASR and subsection 33 (3) of the *Acts Interpretation Act 1901*. The AD requires that the action set out in the requirement section (being action that the delegate considers necessary to correct an unsafe condition) be taken in relation to the aircraft or aeronautical product mentioned in the applicability section: (a) in the circumstances mentioned in the requirement section; and (b) in accordance with the instructions set out in the requirement section; and (c) at the time mentioned in the compliance section.

**REQUIREMENTS**

The PT6A engine maintenance program must be carried out in accordance with approved maintenance data and detailed in an approved system of maintenance. The system of maintenance must include, but need not be limited to, the following requirements:

1\. PT6A engines not previously maintained in accordance with a maintenance program reflecting the intent of the maintenance requirements detailed in this Appendix and with engine hours in service greater than 75% of the Pratt and Whitney Canada published Time Between Overhaul (***TBO***) period at the time of introduction, must be subject to a pro-rata TBO period based on the Pratt and Whitney Canada published TBO period.

2\. The engine must be on an Engine Condition Trend Monitoring (***ECTM***) program, including data collection, analysis and follow up actions. This program must be in accordance with the WebECTM® system or CAMP Systems International (CAMP), as required under revision 10 of Pratt and Whitney Canada SIL No. GEN-055 (PWC SIL No. GEN-055 R10 dated 13 November 2018), as existing at the commencement of this instrument, and include requirements for qualification and training for personnel conducting ECTM.

3\. Hot section inspections must be carried out at intervals not to exceed:

(a) 1250 hours time in service; or

(b) 1760 hours time in service subject to a boroscope inspection of the engine hot section for evidence of cracks, distortion, burning and coating loss at intervals not to exceed 610 hours time in service.

4\. All fuel nozzles must be cleaned and flow checked at intervals not to exceed 460 hours time in service.

5\. The engine oil filter element must be inspected for contamination at intervals not to exceed 220 hours time in service.

6\. The reduction gearbox chip detector must be inspected for contamination, or continuity, at intervals not to exceed 220 hours time in service.

7\. Engines with an annual utilisation of less than 500 hours per annum, an engine oil change must be carried out at intervals not to exceed 12 calendar months time in service.

8\. An inspection of the 1st stage compressor for evidence of Foreign Object damage (FOD), corrosion and contamination must be carried out at intervals not to exceed 220 hours time in service.

9\. A compressor power recovery wash must be carried out in response to trend monitoring parameter deviations, or at intervals not to exceed 220 hours time in service, or 3 calendar months, whichever occurs first.

10\. All life limited components must be replaced at engine manufacturer’s published life limits per AD/ENG/7.

11\. Critical components, as identified in the Pratt and Whitney Service Bulletin that applies to the engine model, must be inspected at intervals specified by the engine manufacturer.

12\. TBO of accessories must be per the recommendations of Pratt and Whitney Canada.

13\. The maintenance program must be reviewed and revised as required at periods not to exceed 2 years taking into consideration all major defects and defects that affect engine durability. Outcome of the review must be reported to CASA via CASA’s online Defect Reporting System (DRS) Portal or via Form 404 referring to AD/ENG/5.

**REQUIREMENTS**

The PT6A engine on-condition maintenance program must be carried out in accordance with approved maintenance data and detailed in an approved system of maintenance. The system of maintenance must include, but need not be limited to, the following requirements:

1\. The engine must be on an Engine Condition Trend Monitoring (***ECTM***) program, including data collection, analysis and follow up actions, qualification and training of personnel. This program must be in accordance with the WebECTM® system or CAMP Systems International (CAMP), as required under revision 10 of Pratt and Whitney Canada SIL No. GEN-055 (PWC SIL No. GEN-055 R10 dated 13 November 2018), and include requirements for qualification and training for personnel conducting ECTM.

2\. Hot section inspections must be carried out at intervals not to exceed 1510 hours time in service.

3\. All fuel nozzles must be cleaned and flow checked at intervals not to exceed 460 hours time in service.

4\. The engine oil filter element must be inspected for contamination at intervals not to exceed 220 hours time in service.

5\. The reduction gearbox chip detector must be inspected for contamination, or continuity, at intervals not to exceed 220 hours time in service.

6\. Engines with an annual utilisation of less than 500 hours per annum, an engine oil change must be carried out at intervals not to exceed 12 calendar months time in service.

7\. Engines not incorporating an engine air intake positive barrier air filter, an inspection of the 1st stage compressor for evidence of Foreign Object Damage (FOD), corrosion and contamination must be carried out at intervals not to exceed 220 hours time in service.

8\. All life limited components must be replaced at the engine manufacturer’s published life limits per AD/ENG/7.

9\. Critical components, as identified in the latest revision of the Pratt and Whitney Service Bulletin that applies to the engine model, must be inspected at intervals specified by Pratt and Whitney Canada.

10\. Engine work-scope during engine shop visits must have regard to the recommendations of Pratt and Whitney Canada, including minimum modification standards for engine durability; and

11\. TBO of accessories must have regard to the recommendations of Pratt and Whitney Canada.

12\. The on-condition program must be reviewed and revised as required at periods not exceeding 2 years taking into consideration all major defects and defects that affect engine durability. Outcome of the review must be reported to CASA via CASA’s online Defect Reporting System (DRS) Portal or via Form 404 referring to AD/ENG/5.
