## Schedule 2— Description of ecological community

**Wetlands and inner floodplains of the Macquarie Marshes**

The ecological community consists of the assemblage of native flora, fauna and other organisms associated with and dependent upon the lower Wambuul/Macquarie River, its anabranches, effluent streams and tributaries and associated floodplains. It is associated with a large, wetland ecosystem complex with interconnected habitats, including permanent/intermittent lowland streams and freshwater lakes, seasonal floodplain wetlands, and associated woodlands, forests, shrublands, marshlands and other vegetation types.

The area in nature for the *Wetlands and inner floodplains of the Macquarie Marshes* is within the northern Murray-Darling Basin and extends from: south-east of Brewarrina and includes occurrences near the Ginghet/Marra Creek confluence and Castlereagh/Wambuul/Macquarie River confluence; west of Quambone; and north of Warren and includes occurrences upstream of the Marebone Weir near the Junction Creek and Wambuul/Macquarie River confluence.

The ecological community occurs where water regularly overflows (breaks out of) the main Wambuul/Macquarie River channel, including Bulgeraga Creek, Gum Cowal Creek, Monkeygar Creek and Terrigal Creek, and develops as a network of smaller channels and braided swamps. The ecological community also occurs in areas further upstream and downstream from the floodplain that have similar biophysical characteristics, for instance the Ginghet Creek and Marthaguy Creek.

The vegetation throughout the *Wetlands and inner floodplains of the Macquarie Marshes* varies, both along river channels and across the floodplain, depending on the habitat type. The wetland vegetation assemblages that comprise the ecological community include:

*Eucalyptus camaldulensis* (river red gum) forest and woodland.

Shrubland wetland dominated by the native shrubs *Acacia stenophylla* (river cooba) or *Duma florulenta* (lignum).

Seasonal to intermittent marshland wetland dominated by native grasses, sedges and herbs. Key species include *Paspalum distichum* (water couch), *Phragmites australis* (reedbeds), *Typha* spp. (cumbungi), *Bolboschoenus* spp. (clubrushes) and *Eleocharis* spp. (spikerushes).

More permanent water bodies (lakes, billabongs, lagoons and channels) which may contain aquatic vegetation (macrophytes) such as *Vallisneria australis* (ribbon weed) and *Azolla* spp. (floating ferns).

Due to variable hydrological regimes and inundation patterns between years, some ecotypes vary from wet to dry and the vegetation cover ranges from dense to sparse. During dry periods, vegetation typical of drier sites, such as dryland grasses and chenopods, may encroach into wetland sites. In natural systems this is a temporary cycle in which wetland vegetation will replace dryland species soon after inundation.

The ecological community includes a variety of aquatic and terrestrial fauna species. This includes several functionally important species, and species that are listed individually as threatened at a national level.
