###### 6 Recognized-Value Standard of Measurement of the Density of Water

The magnitude of the density of water *d*t at a temperature *t* and a mean pressure *p*, provided *t* lies within the range 0 °C to 40 °C and *p* lies within the range 2 × 104 Pa to 106 Pa, is a Recognized-Value Standard of Measurement determined as follows: When *p* is 101325 Pa and *t* is one of the temperatures listed in Schedule 1 the magnitude of the density in kg·m-3 is as stated in Schedule 1, which is derived from the following formula:

[image: Start formula d subscript t equals 999.972 minus start fraction open bracket t minus 3.98419 close bracket superscript 2 over 506.60312 end fraction times start fraction open bracket t plus 286.4601 close bracket over open bracket t plus 67.7601 close bracket end fraction end formula]

where:

*dt* is the density in kg m−3

*t* is the temperature in ℃.

When *p* is 101325 Pa and *t* is between two adjacent values of temperature listed in Schedule 1 then the magnitude of the density in kg·m-3 shall be determined from Schedule 1 by linear interpolation.

When *p* differs from 101325 Pa the magnitude of the density in kg·m-3 as stated in Schedule 1 or derived in accordance with the above linear interpolation shall be algebraically increased by an amount equal to:

[image: Start formula open bracket 5.0619 minus 0.0309 times t plus 0.0003614 times t superscript 2 close bracket times 10 superscript minus 7 times open bracket p minus 101325 close bracket end formula]

If the value of *t* used in Schedule 1 and the above equations does not differ from the true mean temperature of the water by more than 0.1 °C, if the value of *p* used in the equation does not differ from the true mean pressure within the water by more than 1000 Pa, and if impurities in the water do not exceed 1 part in 105 by mass, the chance is not more than one in one hundred that the density so ascertained differs from the true value of density by more than 0.05 kg·m-3.
