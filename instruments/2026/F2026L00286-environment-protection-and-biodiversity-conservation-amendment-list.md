## Schedule 2— Description of ecological community

**White Ash forests of south-east Australia**

The ecological community consists of the native flora, fauna, and other organisms associated with, and dependent upon, tall open forests dominated by *Eucalyptus fraxinoides* (white ash), with small areas dominated by *Eucalyptus paliformis* (Wadbilliga ash).

The White Ash forests are scattered on escarpments and coastal ranges from the upper Clyde River (southeast of Nerriga township) and the Gourock Range (east of Queanbeyan) in southeast New South Wales (NSW) to the Howe Range in far East Gippsland, Victoria. The ecological community typically occurs on moderate to steep slopes at elevations mostly within the range of 430 to 1240 m. The ecological community occurs most commonly on fine-grained sedimentary and metasedimentary silicastic substrates.

The ecological community is usually dominated by *Eucalyptus fraxinoides* (white ash), typically with a tall (to approximately 40 m) open tree canopy of eucalypts, a sparse to patchy shrub midstorey and an open ground layer. Small areas of the ecological community may be dominated by the restricted species *Eucalyptus paliformis* (Jilliga/Wadbilliga ash), where it is known from a few populations in the Wadbilliga area north-east of Cooma. *Eucalyptus paliformis* is a shorter species, growing to approximately 12 m.

*Eucalyptus fraxinoides* (white ash) is typically the dominant tree species of the community and often occurs in pure stands*.* In mixed-species stands, the most commonly co-occurring species is *E. sieberi* (silvertop ash), while *E. radiata* (narrow-leaved peppermint), *E. fastigata* (brown barrel), *E. obliqua* (messmate) and *E. cypellocarpa* (mountain grey gum, monkey gum) may also be present.

The midstorey varies in density and height with fire history and site conditions but rarely exceeds 2-3 m tall. The understorey includes a sparse to patchy shrub layer (midstorey), often including *Styphelia affinis* (beard heath), *Platysace lanceolata* (shrubby platysace), *Persoonia silvatica* (geebung) and *Polyscias sambucifolia* (elderberry panax).

The ground layer tends to be sparse and species-poor, commonly including *Pteridium esculentum* (austral bracken) with scattered clumps of graminoids, *Lomandra longifolia* (spiny-headed mat-rush), *Poa meionectes* (broad-leaved snowgrass) and *Hierochloe rariflora* (cane holy-grass), with forbs *Dianella tasmanica* (Tasman flax-lily), *Gonocarpus teucrioides* (germander raspwort), *Stylidium graminifolium* (grass triggerplant), *Viola hederacea* (ivy-leaved violet), and twining *Billardiera scandens* (hairy apple berry).

The ecological community includes a variety of fauna species, including several species that are listed as threatened at a national or state level.
