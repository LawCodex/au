###### 7 Meaning of fire season, early dry season and late dry season

In this instrument, the ***fire seasons*** are the early dry season and the late dry season.

Note: Each year in northern Australia, there is also a wet season, which occurs approximately from November to April. For the purposes of this instrument, the definitions of the early and late dry seasons overlap with the wet season, as fire generally does not occur during the wet season.

In this instrument:

***early dry season***, for a particular area of land and for a particular calendar year, means the period of the calendar year that is not the late dry season for that area.

***late dry season***, for a particular area of land and for a particular calendar year, means the period that:

begins on the date of that year indicated for that area in the Savanna Fire Management LDS Start Date spatial data layer; and

ends on the date for that year (which may be in that year or in the following year) indicated for that area of land in the Savanna Fire Management LDS End Date spatial data layer.

***Savanna Fire Management LDS End Date spatial data layer***, in relation to a calendar year, means the document of that name, as published on the Department’s website and in force on 30 June in the previous calendar year.

***Savanna Fire Management LDS Start Date spatial data layer***, in relation to a calendar year, means the document of that name, as published on the Department’s website and in force on 30 June in the previous calendar year.

###### 8 Meaning of baseline period

For the purposes of this instrument, the ***baseline period*** for a particular area of land that is not an area referred to in subsections (2), (3) or (4) is the period of 20 calendar years that ends immediately before:

(a) for an area of land that is part of a project area that was identified in the section 27 declaration when the project was declared an eligible offsets project—the calendar year in which the crediting period for the project begins; and

(b) for an area of land that is part of a project area that was identified in the section 27 declaration at a later date—the calendar year in which the relevant variation of the section 27 declaration took effect.

(2) The ***baseline period*** for an area of land in a subdivided project area is the same as the baseline period of the area of land in the original project area.

(3) The ***baseline period*** for an area of land in a transferring project area is the same as the baseline period for that area under the former determination for that area.

(4) If capacity building fire management has been conducted within an area of land and that area is not a transferring project area, the ***baseline period*** for the project area that contains that area of land is the period of 20 calendar years that ends immediately before:

(a) if the time period between the start date and end date of the capacity building fire management is 6 calendar years or fewer—the calendar year in which the capacity building fire management began, or

(b) if the time period between the start date and end date of the capacity building fire management is more than 6 calendar years— the calendar year that is 6 years before the end date of the capacity building fire management.

###### 9 Meaning of original project area and subdivided project area

For the purposes of this instrument, if:

a project area (the ***original project area***) is divided into 2 or more smaller project areas; and

the area made up of the areas of land covered by those smaller project areas, when taken together, is identical to the area of land covered by the original project area;

each smaller project area is a ***subdivided project area***.

Note: To effect the subdivision, the project proponent must make a section 29 application in respect of the project area in accordance with the CFI Rules.

### Part 2—Savanna sequestration projects

###### 12 Savanna sequestration projects

For the purposes of paragraph 106(1)(a) of the Act, this instrument applies to an offsets project that:

involves undertaking savanna fire management with both of the following objectives: removing carbon dioxide from the atmosphere by sequestering more carbon (in living biomass or dead organic matter) than was sequestered during the baseline period; avoiding the emission of methane and nitrous oxide from the burning of savannas, compared to the emissions during the baseline period; and

does not result in an increase in: greenhouse gas emissions from any other source; or the rate of decomposition of organic carbon; and

is carried out on a savanna that is in either or both of the following:

(i) the high rainfall zone;

(ii) the low rainfall zone; and

can reasonably be expected to result in eligible carbon abatement.

A project covered by subsection (1) is a ***savanna sequestration project***.

This instrument does not apply to an offsets project if that offsets project has been or is an eligible offsets project under a savanna emissions avoidance determination.

Note: The *Carbon Credits (Carbon Farming Initiative—Savanna Fire Management— Emissions Avoidance) Methodology Determination 2026* covers offsets projects that involve undertaking savanna fire management with the sole objective of avoiding the emission of methane and nitrous oxide from the burning of savannas, compared to the emissions during the baseline period.

### Part 3—Project requirements

#### Division 1—General

###### 13 Operation of this Part

For the purposes of paragraph 106(1)(b) of the Act, this Part sets out requirements that must be met for a savanna sequestration project to be an eligible offsets project.

Division 6 specifies:

for the purposes of subparagraph 27(4A)(a)(ii) of the Act, requirements in lieu of the newness requirement for savanna sequestration projects; and

for the purposes of subparagraph 27(4A)(b)(ii) of the Act, a requirement in lieu of the regulatory additionality requirement for savanna sequestration projects.

For the purposes of paragraph 69(2)(b) of the Act, Division 7 specifies crediting periods for certain projects.

#### Division 2—Project area

###### 14 Requirement to be in high or low rainfall zone

A project area for a savanna sequestration project must not include an area of land that is in neither the high rainfall zone nor the low rainfall zone.

###### 15 Requirement to maintain vegetation fuel types

A project area for a savanna sequestration project must include an area of land that contains a vegetation fuel type at the relevant date.

After the relevant date, a project area must continue to include an area of land that contains a vegetation fuel type.

If, at any time after the relevant date, there is a change that results in a significant portion of land in the project area becoming classified as ineligible according to the Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods, that significant portion of land may be removed from the project in accordance with the CFI Rules.

Note: To effect the removal, the project proponent must make a section 29 application in respect of the project area in accordance with the CFI Rules. To remove part of a project area from the project, the project area must first be subdivided so that the area to be removed now constitutes the whole of a (smaller) project area.

In this section:

***relevant date*** means the date of the section 22 application, section 29 application or section 128 application in relation to the project in which the project area is first identified.

***significant portion of land*** means a contiguous area of land in the project area that is larger than 5% of the total area of the project area or larger than 50 hectares.

###### 16 Requirement to manage relevant weed species

A project area for a savanna sequestration project must be clear of all relevant weed species on the relevant date.

If, after the relevant date, the project proponent becomes aware that the project area includes an area of land that contains a relevant weed species (a ***weed-affected area***), the weed-affected area must be treated appropriately during the period that starts no later than 18 months after the area becomes a weed-affected area and ends when the relevant weed species has been eradicated from that area.

If a weed-affected area is removed from the project in accordance with the CFI Rules, that area is taken to have not included a weed-affected area before its removal for that purpose.

Note: The weed-affected area may be removed from the project in accordance with the CFI Rules. To effect the removal, the project proponent must make a section 29 application in respect of the project area in accordance with the CFI Rules. To remove part of a project area from the project, the project area must first be subdivided so that the area to be removed now constitutes the whole of a (smaller) project area.

For the purposes of subsection (1), a project area is taken to be clear of all relevant weed species at the relevant date if there is evidence that demonstrates all known weed-affected areas in that project area were treated appropriately within the 12-month period preceding that date.

A weed-affected area is ***treated appropriately*** if:

the relevant weed species in that area is treated with a herbicide to kill the entirety of its aboveground biomass, or is subjected to mechanical removal to destroy the entirety of its aboveground biomass; and

that treatment or removal is carried out in accordance with any relevant law of the Commonwealth, a State or a Territory; and

that treatment occurs at least once every 12 months (unless such treatment is not possible due to circumstances beyond the project proponent’s reasonable control).

A relevant weed species is taken to have been ***eradicated from*** a weed-affected area when it has not been detected in that area for at least 24 months or, if another period of time is specified in the Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods for a particular relevant weed species, for at least that period of time.

In this section:

***relevant date*** means the date of the section 22 application, section 29 application or section 128 application in relation to the project in which the project area is first identified.

###### 17 Project area not to contain previously removed areas

A project area for a savanna sequestration project must not be, or include, an area of land that was previously part of the project, or previously part of another project covered by this instrument or a savanna fire management determination, but was removed in accordance with subsection 15(3) or as referred to in section 16(3) (or an equivalent provision of the savanna fire management determination).

#### Division 3—Project activity

###### 18 Requirement to undertake savanna fire management

(1) The project proponent must undertake savanna fire management by undertaking planned burning in each project area each calendar year.

(2) The planned burning must be undertaken each year in such a way that the combination of:

(a) the pattern of planned and unplanned burning (if any) in all previous calendar years since the project commenced; and

(b) the planned burning undertaken in that calendar year (if any); and

(c) the planned burning that is proposed to be undertaken for the remainder of the permanence obligation period for the project;

in the project area together demonstrate a program of burning that:

(d) meets the objectives referred to in paragraph 12(1)(a); and

(e) could reasonably be expected to ensure that the amount of carbon sequestered in savanna in the project area does not decrease significantly over the permanence obligation period for the project.

(3) A failure to undertake planned burning in a particular project area in a particular calendar year is not, of itself, a contravention of subsection (1) if the failure resulted from circumstances beyond the project proponent’s reasonable control.

(4) If a project area is divided into 2 or more subdivided project areas on a particular date, for the purposes of this section, planned burning that was undertaken in any part of the original project area prior to that date may be treated as having been undertaken in any of the subdivided project areas.

Note: In monitoring whether a project continues to meet the requirements of section 18, the Regulator will consider a range of information, including the project management plan and other information included in an offsets report under subsection 38(2) and the information in any SavCAM reports relating to the project.

#### Division 4—Project management plan

###### 19 Requirement to prepare project management plan

For each savanna sequestration project, the project proponent must, each calendar year, prepare a project management plan or plans that cover all project areas for that project.

A ***project management plan*** is a document that:

(a) describes the planned burning that is intended to be undertaken in a project area for that year; and

(b) demonstrates that:

the likely impact of PM2.5 particulate matter on air quality, as a result of that burning, in areas where people live has been considered; and

if there are air quality management requirements specified in the Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods, the consideration referred to in subparagraph (i) is in a manner consistent with those requirements; and

(c) includes a plan for monitoring for the presence of each relevant weed species in each project area that:

demonstrates the level of risk of relevant weed species occurring in each project area has been considered; and

describes how that risk has been determined; and

describes a monitoring approach (including the methods and frequency of monitoring); and

is consistent with any weed monitoring requirements specified in the Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods.

A project management plan that relates to a particular project area must be prepared before commencing planned burning in that project area in the year to which the plan relates.

Note: The Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods may contain recommended guidelines relating to aspects of project management.

###### 20 Updating or revising project management plan

A project management plan may be revised or updated throughout the calendar year to which it relates.

The part of the project management plan described in paragraph 19(2)(c) must be reviewed by the project proponent at least once every 5 years and revised accordingly.

Any revision or update of the project management plan must include the date of the revision or update.

#### Division 5—Vegetation fuel type map

###### 21 Requirement to create and validate vegetation fuel type map

For each savanna sequestration project, the project proponent must create and validate a vegetation fuel type map for each project area (whether the project area was identified in the section 22 application or section 128 application, or was subsequently added to the project in accordance with a section 29 application):

(a) in accordance with the Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods; and

(b) before the project proponent submits the first offsets report to which Division 1 of Part 5 of this instrument applies.

###### 22 Revising vegetation fuel type map

If the project proponent becomes aware that the vegetation fuel type map: classifies a mapping unit as a vegetation fuel type when, in accordance with the Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods, it should be classified as ineligible; or classifies a mapping unit as one particular vegetation fuel type when, in accordance with the Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods, it should be classified as another vegetation fuel type;

the project proponent must:

(c) notify the Regulator in writing of the matter in paragraph (a) or (b) within 90 days of becoming aware of it; and

(d) revise the vegetation fuel type map accordingly.

If the project proponent becomes aware that the vegetation fuel type map classifies a mapping unit as ineligible when, in accordance with the Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods, it should be classified as a vegetation fuel type, the project proponent:

may revise the vegetation fuel type map accordingly; and

if the vegetation fuel type map is revised in accordance with paragraph (a) **—** notify the Regulator in writing of the date and nature of the revision within 90 days of the revision being made.

A vegetation fuel type map revised in accordance with this section must be validated in accordance with the Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods.

Note: The Technical Guidance Document for the 2026 Savanna Fire Management Methods may require validation of the entire vegetation fuel type map even if the area of land represented by the mapping unit or units that are being revised is only part of the area covered by that vegetation fuel type map.

A revision and validation in accordance with this section must be completed: in the case of a revision required by subsection (1)**—**before submitting an offsets report for the reporting period in which the project proponent becomes aware of the matter referred to in that subsection; or in any other case**—**before submitting an offsets report for the first reporting period for which the revised map is used.

###### 23 Vegetation fuel type map for subdivided project area

This section applies if: a vegetation fuel type map for a particular project area has been created and validated, or revised and validated, as the case requires, in accordance with this instrument; and the project area is divided into 2 or more subdivided project areas in accordance with the CFI Rules.

The vegetation fuel type map for the original project area is taken: to be a single map that relates to each subdivided project area; and to have been created and validated, or revised and validated, as the case requires, in accordance with this instrument.

However, if the subdivision of the project area impacts the accuracy of the vegetation fuel type map, the vegetation fuel type map must be revised, if necessary, and re-validated within 2 years of the subdivision.

#### Division 6—Newness and additionality

###### 24 Requirement in lieu of newness requirement

For the purposes of subparagraph 27(4A)(a)(ii) of the Act, the requirement in lieu of the newness requirement for a savanna sequestration project is the requirement in subsection (2).

Each project area for the project: must be a transferring project area; or must not have had any project covered by a savanna fire management determination or by this instrument undertaken in any part of the project area; or if the project area has had a project covered by a savanna fire management determination or by this instrument undertaken in any part of the project area**—**must not have had any Australian carbon credit units issued in relation to any part of the project area while the project was covered by any such methodology determination.

*Modification of operation of some provisions of this instrument if paragraph (2)(c) is met*

Note: For a project area that meets the requirement of paragraph (2)(c), some provisions of this instrument will operate in a modified manner to account for the project that has been previously undertaken. Subsection (3) sets out these modifications.

For a project area that meets the requirement of paragraph (2)(c): for the purposes of subsection 15(3), disregard the section 22 application or the section 29 application in which the project area referred to in paragraph (2)(c) was first identified; and for the purposes of subsection 27(1), disregard the project referred to in paragraph (2)(c); and for the purposes of the following: paragraph 3(1)(a) of Schedule 1; paragraph 3(1)(a) of Schedule 2;

disregard any values that might have been calculated in relation to the project area referred to in paragraph (2)(c).

###### 25 Requirement in lieu of regulatory additionality requirement

For the purposes of subparagraph 27(4A)(b)(ii) of the Act, the requirement in lieu of the regulatory additionality requirement for a savanna sequestration project is the requirement in subsection (2).

A project area for the project must not include an area of land if fire management for the primary purpose of either or both of the following is required to be carried out by or under a law of the Commonwealth, a State or a Territory on that land: reducing emissions from fire; sequestering carbon in living biomass or dead organic matter.

#### Division 7—Projects that include transferring project areas

###### 26 Simplified outline of this Division

This Division is relevant to a savanna sequestration project if it contains a project area that was previously part of either a project covered by a savanna fire management determination or another project covered by this instrument. Such a project area is a transferring project area.

There are several ways of moving a transferring project area from its original project to another project that is covered by this instrument.

**Moving project areas between savanna sequestration projects**

A project area may be moved from one savanna sequestration project to another savanna sequestration project in accordance with the requirements for voluntary variation of section 27 declarations in the CFI Rules. The project’s crediting period would be adjusted in accordance with this determination and the requirements in the CFI Rules relating to restructured eligible offsets projects.

**Moving project areas from emissions avoidance project to sequestration project**

If the original project is an emissions avoidance project covered by a savanna fire management determination, there are several options.

● One option is to apply to move all of the original project’s project areas onto a project covered by this instrument, in accordance with the CFI Rules (see the sections dealing with linked applications in respect of savanna emissions avoidance projects and savanna sequestration projects). This option would involve seeking revocation of the original project’s section 27 declaration.

● Another option is to apply to move only some of the original project’s project areas onto a project covered by this instrument, in accordance with the CFI Rules (see the sections dealing with linked applications in respect of savanna emissions avoidance projects and savanna sequestration projects). This option would involve seeking a variation of the original project’s section 27 declaration to subdivide the project into two subdivided project areas, one covering all area of the project that is to remain, and one covering all area of the project that is to be moved. An application could then be made to add the entire subdivided project area to the sequestration project (and seeking to remove that entire subdivided project area from the original project) in accordance with the CFI Rules.

In either case, a new savanna sequestration project would need to be declared as an eligible offsets project. The new project would have a new crediting period and would be a restarting transferring project.

**Projects that contain transferring project areas**

Some provisions of this instrument apply special rules to projects that contain transferring project areas. These provisions are principally set out in this Division.

###### 27 Meaning of transferring project area and related definitions

A project area for a savanna sequestration project is a ***transferring project area*** if, immediately before it was a project area for a project covered by this instrument, it was a project area for either of the following (the ***former project***): a project covered by a savanna emissions avoidance determination; a project covered by the *Carbon Credits (Carbon Farming Initiative—Savanna Fire Management—Sequestration and Emissions Avoidance) Methodology Determination 2018* or a different project covered by this instrument.

Note 1: Subsection (1) is modified in some cases if the requirement in lieu of the newness requirement set out in paragraph 24(2)(c) was met. See paragraph 24(3)(b).

The methodology determination that applied to the former project is the ***former determination*** for the transferring project area.

Note 2: The former determination could be this instrument.

A savanna sequestration project is a ***restarting transferring project*** if:

when the project was first declared eligible, one or more of its project areas was a transferring project area; and

the former determination for each transferring project area was a savanna emissions avoidance determination; and

before declaring the project an eligible offsets project, the Regulator either revoked the section 27 declaration in relation to the former project of each transferring project area, or varied that declaration to remove each transferring project area.

### Part 4—Net abatement amount

#### Division 1—Preliminary

###### 32 Operation of this Part

For the purposes of paragraph 106(1)(c) of the Act, this Part specifies the method for working out the net abatement amount for a reporting period for a savanna sequestration project that is an eligible offsets project.

###### 33 Simplified outline of this Part

This instrument accounts for carbon abatement from undertaking savanna fire management. Carbon abatement is achieved through the avoidance of emitting greenhouse gases into the atmosphere and removing carbon dioxide from the atmosphere by sequestering carbon in living biomass and dead organic matter in savannas.

A project covered by this instrument is a sequestration offsets project, and as such is subject to the obligations under the Act that relate to the permanence period.

To ascertain the net abatement amount in relation to a reporting period for a savanna sequestration project, the project proponent must calculate the adjusted contributions from emissions avoidance and from sequestration of carbon each calendar year that ends during the reporting period and add these together. These calculations must be performed using SavCAM.

If the project has 2 or more project areas, adjusted contributions to the net abatement amount must be calculated separately for each project area and added together.

When calculating the contribution to the net abatement amount from emissions avoidance, SavCAM calculations account for emissions for all fuel classes (that is, grass fuel, fine fuel, coarse fuel, and heavy fuel and the fire impacted proportion of living biomass). In contrast, when calculating the contribution to the net abatement amount from sequestration, SavCAM calculations account for living biomass and dead organic matter that consists of coarse fuel and heavy fuel.

###### 34 Use of SavCAM

If a calculation is undertaken using SavCAM for the purposes of this instrument, SavCAM must be used in accordance with any guidance material on the SavCAM website.

## Schedule 1—Emissions avoidance—calculation of adjusted contribution to net abatement amount for a calendar year

#### Division 1—Preliminary

###### 1 Simplified outline of this Schedule

First, the contribution to the net abatement amount for the project area from emissions avoidance in the project area for the calendar year, *AEA*, is calculated. This is calculated as the difference between the mean annual emissions from savanna burning over the baseline period, and the emissions from savanna burning during that year.

Then, from *AEA*, the adjusted contribution to the net abatement amount for the calendar year, *AEA,adj*, is calculated. The adjustment manages the risk of the project containing some years in which emissions are higher than the mean annual baseline emissions, and involves apportioning the amount *AEA* between the adjusted contribution *AEA,adj* and an ‘uncertainty buffer’. Generally, the uncertainty buffer starts at zero (unless the project area is a transferring project area). The uncertainty buffer is capped at a maximum, *BU,Cap*, which is equal to 5 per cent of the mean annual baseline emissions. The uncertainty buffer is subtracted from in years in which *AEA* is less than zero. For years in which *AEA* is greater than zero, the uncertainty buffer is generally added to, subject to its reaching the maximum, *BU,Cap*.

The value of the uncertainty buffer cap, *BU,Cap*, and values relating to the baseline period, are recalculated each year, and will potentially change from year to year. Changes to these values might arise, for example, if documents that are referred to as in force from time to time are modified, if the vegetation fuel type map is revised, or if project areas are subdivided.

The following provides an explanation of how this is calculated.

**Contribution from Sequestration (ASeq)**

For the first calendar year, ASeq is equal to half of the difference between the carbon stock at the end of the calendar year and the mean carbon stock during the baseline period, plus the abatement released from the sequestration bank (R).

For each subsequent years, ASeq equals half of the change in carbon stock (carbon stock at the end of the current calendar year minus carbon stock at the end of the previous calendar year), plus the amount of abatement released from the sequestration bank (R).

**Adjusted Contribution (ASeq,adj)**

The adjusted contribution to the net abatement amount, ASeq,adj, is calculated by decreasing ASeq by any negative abatement that was carried over from the previous calendar year. The amount calculated is further decreased by the sequestration buffer. The sequestration buffer is an amount that is applied in lieu of the risk of reversal buffer number and the permanence period discount number that ordinarily apply when the unit entitlement for a sequestration offsets project is calculated under section 16 of the Act. For projects covered by this instrument, these numbers are equal to zero as a result of the CFI Rules. (The contribution to the net abatement amount from emissions avoidance is not adjusted.)

Changes to some values will arise if documents that are referred to as in force from time to time are modified, if the vegetation fuel type map is revised, or if project areas are subdivided.

**Contribution to the sequestration bank**

For the first calendar year, half of the difference between the carbon stock at the end of the calendar year and the mean carbon stock during the baseline period is deposited into the sequestration bank.

For subsequent years, the sequestration bank is calculated as the previous year’s sequestration bank plus half of the change in carbon stock (the difference between the current carbon stock and the previous carbon stock) minus the amount of abatement released in the previous year.

**Calculation of abatement released from the sequestration bank**

Abatement will be released from the sequestration bank for a calendar year as the total sequestration bank divided by the remaining years in the crediting period (crediting period length minus elapsed years).

**Adjustments to calculations**

The calculations are adjusted for projects spanning rainfall zones, lack of fire activity, projects that have been subdivided, have substantial areas of ineligible vegetation, have transferring project areas, or have transferred from other methods.
