### Part 1—Suspension from duty while under investigation

###### 87 Charging a person with a service offence and summons or order to appear

(1) If the Director of Military Prosecutions believes, on reasonable grounds, that a person has committed a service offence, the Director of Military Prosecutions may:

(a) charge the person with the service offence; and

(b) either:

(i) request the Registrar to refer the charge to a Defence Force magistrate for trial; or

(ii) request the Registrar to convene a restricted court or a general court martial to try the charge.

(2) If an authorised charging member or the Director of Military Prosecutions believes, on reasonable grounds, that a person has committed a service offence, the authorised charging member or the Director of Military Prosecutions may:

(a) if the person is a defence member:

(i) charge the defence member with the service offence; and

(ii) cause a copy of the charge to be given to the defence member; and

(iii) order the defence member to appear before a commanding officer at a specified time and place to be dealt with in accordance with section 110; or

(b) whether or not the person is a defence member—cause to be prepared a summons directed to the person specifying the service offence that the person is alleged to have committed and requiring the person to appear before a commanding officer at a time and place specified in the summons to be dealt with in accordance with section 110; or

(c) only in the case of the Director of Military Prosecutions:

(i) if the person is a defence member—charge the defence member with the service offence, cause a copy of the charge to be given to the defence member and order the defence member to appear before a superior summary authority at a specified time and place to be dealt with in accordance with section 109; or

(ii) whether or not the person is a defence member—cause to be prepared a summons directed to the person specifying the service offence that the person is alleged to have committed and requiring the person to appear before a superior summary authority at a time and place specified in the summons to be dealt with in accordance with section 109.

(3) To avoid doubt, the Director of Military Prosecutions:

(a) may exercise any or all of the powers referred to subsections (1) and (2); and

(b) may exercise the power referred to in subsection (1) in addition to, or instead of, the powers referred to in subsection (2).

(4) A summons under subsection (2) must be served on the person to whom it is directed in a manner specified in the regulations.

(5) Where a summons relating to a service offence is served on a person in accordance with subsection (2), the person is taken, for the purposes of this Act, to have been charged with the offence.

Summons or order to appear before service tribunal

(6) A superior officer may order an accused person, being a defence member, to appear before a service tribunal for any purpose relating to the charge against the accused person.

(7) The appropriate authority for a service tribunal may summon an accused person (whether or not the accused person is a defence member) to appear before the service tribunal for any purpose relating to a charge against the accused person.

(8) A summons under subsection (7) must be served on the accused person in a manner specified in the Court Martial and Defence Force Magistrate Rules or the Summary Authority Rules (as the case may be).

Delegation

(9) The Director of Military Prosecutions may delegate any or all of the Director’s functions or powers under this section to a person referred to in paragraph 188GB(b).

###### 87A Charge sheets

(1) A charge against an accused person must be entered in a charge sheet that is signed and dated by the Director of Military Prosecutions.

#### Division 3—Courts martial

##### Subdivision A—Convening of court martial

###### 114 Composition of court martial

(1) A court martial may be a general court martial or a restricted court martial.

(2) A general court martial is to consist of:

(a) the judge advocate; and

(b) if and when required, a court martial panel consisting of:

(i) a President; and

(ii) not less than 4 other members.

(3) A restricted court martial is to consist of:

(a) the judge advocate; and

(b) if and when required, a court martial panel consisting of:

(i) a President; and

(ii) not less than 2 other members.

###### 115 Jurisdiction of court martial

Subject to section 63 (consent for proceedings), a court martial has jurisdiction to try any charge of a service offence (other than a custodial offence).

###### 116 Convening a court martial

(1) If the Registrar is requested or directed under this Act to convene a court martial to try a charge, the Registrar must make an order:

(a) convening a court martial; and

(b) appointing the judge advocate of the court martial.

(2) The Registrar may vary or revoke an order under subsection (1):

(a) at any time before the judge advocate commences proceedings in relation to the charge; and

(b) at any other time as required or permitted by this Act.

(3) If a court martial panel is required, the Registrar must make an order:

(a) appointing the members of the court martial panel; and

(b) appointing sufficient alternative members for the court martial panel; and

(c) fixing, or providing for the fixing of, the time and place for the assembling of the court martial panel.

(4) The Registrar may vary or revoke an order under subsection (3):

(a) at any time before the assembly of the court martial panel; and

(b) as required from time to time to ensure that the court martial panel is properly constituted as required under section 114 for the purposes of making a finding about guilt under section 134; and

(c) at any other time, as required or permitted by this Act.

(5) The Registrar must, as soon as practicable after making an order under this section, give a copy of the order to the accused person.

(6) To the extent that the exigencies of service permit, if an order under this section is varied or revoked, the Registrar must notify the accused person accordingly.

###### 117 Appointment of judge advocate

(1) Subject to section 129B, the Registrar may appoint, by order under subsection 116(1), a person to be the judge advocate of a court martial if the person is a member of the judge advocates’ panel.

(2) However, the Registrar must not appoint the person if the Registrar believes the person to be:

(a) biased or likely to be biased; or

(b) likely to be thought, on reasonable grounds, to be biased.

(3) An appointment of a judge advocate is on a part‑time basis.

Remuneration

(4) The judge advocate of a court martial is to be paid the remuneration that is determined by the Remuneration Tribunal. If no determination of that remuneration by the Tribunal is in operation, the judge advocate is to be paid the remuneration that is determined under Part IIIA of the *Defence Act 1903*.

(5) The judge advocate of a court martial is to be paid the allowances that are determined under Part IIIA of the *Defence Act 1903*.

(6) This section has effect subject to the *Remuneration Tribunal Act 1973*.

###### 119 Appointment of members of court martial panel and alternative members

(1) The Registrar may appoint, by order under subsection 116(3), a person as a member of a court martial panel or an alternative member if:

(a) the person:

(i) is an officer or a Warrant Officer Class 1 (or equivalent); and

(ii) has been a defence member for a period or periods totalling at least 3 years; and

(b) in a case where the accused person, or any of the accused members, to be tried by the court martial is a member of the Defence Force—the person holds a rank that is not lower than the rank held by the accused person or by any of the accused persons.

(2) An officer is eligible to be appointed as the President of a court martial panel if the officer holds a rank that is not lower than:

(a) for a general court martial—the naval rank of captain or the rank of colonel or group captain; or

(b) for a restricted court martial—the rank of commander, lieutenant‑colonel or wing commander.

(3) However, the Registrar must not appoint a person as a member, an alternative member, or the President, of a court martial panel if the Registrar believes the person to be:

(a) biased or likely to be biased; or

(b) likely to be thought, on reasonable grounds, to be biased.

(4) The requirements set out in paragraph (1)(b) and subsection (2) apply only if, and to the extent that, the exigencies of service permit.

(5) The Chief of the Defence Force, for the purposes of a court martial, must make available a defence member who is appointed to be a member, an alternative member, or the President, of the court martial panel.

##### Subdivision B—Substitution etc. of judge advocate and members of court martial panel

###### 120 Substitution by Registrar—ineligible, bias or belief of bias

(1) A person who is the judge advocate of a court martial, or a member of a court martial panel, must notify the Registrar as soon as practicable if the person believes that the person:

(a) is biased, or is likely to be biased; or

(b) is likely to be thought, on reasonable grounds, to be biased.

Objection to judge advocate before proceedings commence

(2) At any time before a person appointed as the judge advocate of a court martial convened for a charge commences proceedings in relation to the charge, the accused person may lodge with the Registrar an objection to the judge advocate on the ground that the judge advocate:

(a) is ineligible to be appointed; or

(b) is, or is likely to be, biased; or

(c) is likely to be thought, on reasonable grounds, to be biased.

Objection to member or alternative member before swearing in

(3) At any time before a person appointed as a member of a court martial panel, or an alternative member, is sworn or affirmed, the accused person may lodge with the Registrar an objection to the member on the ground that the member:

(a) is ineligible to be appointed; or

(b) is, or is likely to be, biased; or

(c) is likely to be thought, on reasonable grounds, to be biased.

Registrar’s powers

(4) If the Registrar is notified under subsection (1), or an objection is lodged with the Registrar under subsection (2) or (3), the Registrar may revoke the person’s appointment and appoint another person in the person’s place.

###### 121 Substitution by Registrar—other grounds

(1) The Registrar may revoke the appointment of a person as the judge advocate of a court martial convened for a charge and appoint another person in the person’s place:

(a) at any time before the judge advocate commences proceedings in relation to the charge; and

(a) at any time before the judge advocate commences proceedings in relation to the charge; and

(b) at any other time as required or permitted by this Act.

###### 122 Substitution of judge advocate following objection

###### 123 Substitution by judge advocate of members of court martial panel

(1) The judge advocate of a court martial must take action under subsection (2) if, before a member of the court martial panel is sworn or affirmed, the judge advocate:

(a) finds that the member is not, or is not likely to be, available; or

(b) upholds an objection entered under subsection 141(2) to the member; or

(c) finds that, for some other reason, the member should be excused from further attendance.

(2) The judge advocate must appoint an alternative member in the place of that member and:

(a) if the member concerned is the President and the next senior member is not more than one rank junior to the President—appoint that next senior member to be the President in the place of the member concerned; or

(b) if the member concerned is the President and the next senior member is more than one rank junior to the President—direct the Registrar to appoint a President in the place of the member concerned.

Dissolution before swearing in

(b) the judge advocate considers that by reason of the exigencies of service or for any other reason it is desirable that the court martial panel be dissolved.

Dissolution after swearing in

(2) The judge advocate of a court martial may direct the Registrar to dissolve the court martial panel if:

(a) the court martial panel has assembled and its members have been sworn or affirmed; and

(c) the judge advocate considers that in the interests of justice, the court martial panel should be dissolved.

Dissolution before finding about guilt

(3) The judge advocate of a court martial must direct the Registrar to dissolve the court martial panel if:

(a) the court martial has adjourned the hearing of proceedings for the purposes of the court martial panel making a finding about guilt under section 134; and

Registrar’s powers

(4) If the Registrar is directed under subsection (1), (2) or (3) to dissolve a court martial panel:

(a) the Registrar must dissolve the court martial panel; and

(b) the Director of Military Prosecutions may request the Registrar to appoint, by order under subsection 116(3), another court martial panel for the court martial.

##### Subdivision D—Discontinuance of proceedings before court martial

###### 125A Discontinuance of proceedings before court martial

Discontinuance before plea or on direction by judge advocate

(a) at a time before a plea in relation to the charge is recorded, it appears to the Registrar that, by reason of the exigencies of service, or for any other reason, it is desirable to dissolve the court martial; or

(b) at a time after a plea is recorded in relation to the charge:

(i) it appears to the judge advocate that it would not be in the interests of justice for the court martial to continue; and

(ii) the judge advocate directs the Registrar to dissolve the court martial.

Discontinuance where judge advocate unable to continue

(2) The Registrar must dissolve a court martial convened for a charge if the judge advocate is unable to continue to perform their functions in relation to the charge because of death, illness, the exigencies of service or other circumstances.

Registrar’s powers

(3) If, under subsection (1) or (2), the Registrar must dissolve a court martial convened for a charge:

(c) if the charge has not been dismissed or the accused person has not been acquitted or convicted—the Director of Military Prosecutions may request the Registrar to convene a general court martial or a restricted court martial to try the charge.

(4) If a court martial convened for a charge is dissolved after the conviction of the accused person and before action has been taken under Part IV in relation to the person, the Registrar may convene a general court martial or a restricted court martial to take action under Part IV in relation to the person.

###### 132 Procedural hearings

(1) The judge advocate of a court martial convened for a charge may:

(a) hold hearings in relation to the proceedings; and

(b) give directions or rulings in relation to the proceedings; and

(c) direct the parties to attend (in person or otherwise) before the judge advocate for the purposes of such hearings or making such directions or rulings; and

(e) hear applications and objections.

Note: For examples of applications and objections that may be made, see sections 103A and 141.

(2) The powers in subsection (1) may be exercised on the judge advocate’s own initiative or on application by a party to the proceedings.

###### 133 Pleas before judge advocate

(1) The judge advocate of a court martial convened for a charge may, during a procedural hearing, ask the accused person whether the person pleads guilty or not guilty to the charge.

Guilty plea

(2) If the accused person pleads guilty, and the judge advocate is satisfied that the accused person understands the effect of that plea, the judge advocate must:

(a) record a plea of guilty; and

(b) convict the accused person; and

(c) direct the Registrar not to assemble a court martial panel to try the charge, except where an order for trial without a court martial panel has already been made under subsection 103A(2)).

Not guilty plea

(3) The judge advocate must record a plea of not guilty if the accused person:

(a) pleads not guilty; or

(b) pleads guilty but the judge advocate is not satisfied that the accused person understands the effect of that plea; or

(c) refuses to plead; or

(d) does not plead intelligibly.

(4) The judge advocate must also direct the Registrar to assemble a court martial panel, except where an order for trial without a court martial panel has been made under subsection 103A(2)).

###### 134 Trial by court martial

(1) If a plea of not guilty in relation to a charge is recorded, the court martial convened for the charge must proceed to hear the evidence on the charge.

Ruling as to sufficient or insufficient evidence

(2) If after hearing the evidence on the charge adduced by the prosecution:

(a) the judge advocate rules that the evidence is insufficient to support the charge—the court martial must dismiss the charge; or

(b) the judge advocate rules that the evidence is sufficient to support the charge—the court martial must proceed with the trial.

Finding about guilt

(3) The court martial must acquit the accused person if:

(a) if an order has been made under subsection 103A(2) for trial without a court martial panel—the judge advocate finds the accused person not guilty; or

(b) otherwise—the court martial panel finds the accused person not guilty.

(4) The court martial must convict the accused person if:

(a) if an order has been made under subsection 103A(2) for trial without a court martial panel—the judge advocate finds the accused person guilty; or

(b) otherwise—the court martial panel finds the accused person guilty.

Withdrawal of not guilty plea during trial

(5) If, at any time before the judge advocate or court martial panel finds the accused person guilty or not guilty:

(a) the accused person withdraws their plea of not guilty; and

(b) the judge advocate is satisfied that the accused person understands the effect of that plea;

the court martial must convict the accused person.

No requirement to give a ruling under subsection (2)

(6) The judge advocate is not required to give a ruling of the kind referred to in paragraph (2)(a) or (b) unless:

(a) the accused person has submitted that the judge advocate should give a ruling of that kind; or

(b) the interests of justice require that the judge advocate should give a ruling of that kind.

(1) If, under this Subdivision, an accused person is convicted of a service offence by a court martial:

(a) the judge advocate of the court martial, or of a court martial convened under subsection 125A(4) or 129A(4) in relation to the conviction, must take action under Part IV in relation to the convicted person; and

(b) the judge advocate who takes action must give reasons for each punishment imposed and each order made under Part IV in relation to the convicted person.

(2) Before a judge advocate takes action under Part IV, the judge advocate must hear submissions relevant to the determination of what action should be taken.

###### 136 Determination of questions by court martial panel

(1) The President of a court martial panel is to preside in all matters to be determined by the court martial panel in proceedings before the court martial.

(2) Each question that is to be determined by the court martial panel is to be decided by a majority of the votes of the members of the panel.

(3) Subject to subsections (4) and (5), in the case of an equality of votes on any question referred to in subsection (2), the President has a casting vote.

(4) In the case of an equality of votes on the question whether an accused person is guilty or not guilty of a service offence, the court martial must find the accused person not guilty.

(5) In the case of an equality of votes on the question whether an accused person was not responsible for a service offence, because at the time of carrying out the conduct constituting the offence, the person was suffering from a mental impairment, the court martial must find the person not guilty because of mental impairment.

(6) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, the members of a court martial panel must sit without any other person present when determining the question whether an accused person:

(a) is guilty or not guilty of a service offence; or

(b) was not responsible for a service offence, because at the time of carrying out the conduct constituting the offence, the person was suffering from a mental impairment.

###### 136A Powers of judge advocate

(1) The judge advocate of a court martial may give any ruling, and exercise any discretion, that, in accordance with the law in force in the Jervis Bay Territory, would be given or exercised by a judge in a trial by jury.

(2) If in a trial by jury in the Jervis Bay Territory, a judge would, in accordance with the law in force in that Territory, sit in the absence of the jury to give a ruling or exercise a discretion, the judge advocate may sit alone to give the ruling or exercise the discretion.

(3) A ruling given by, or decision made by, the judge advocate in accordance with this Act is binding on the court martial.

(4) The powers conferred on the judge advocate by this section are in addition to any other powers conferred on the judge advocate by any other provision of this Act, the regulations or the Court Martial and Defence Force Magistrate Rules.

###### 136B Procedural hearings

(1) The Defence Force magistrate to whom a charge is referred may:

(1) The Defence Force magistrate to whom the charge is referred may, during a procedural hearing, ask the accused person whether the person pleads guilty or not guilty to the charge.

Guilty plea

(a) record a plea of guilty; and

(b) convict the accused person.

Not guilty plea

(a) pleads not guilty; or

(c) refuses to plead; or

(a) the accused person withdraws their plea of not guilty; and

the Defence Force magistrate must convict the accused person.

(6) The Defence Force magistrate is not required to give a ruling of the kind referred to in paragraph (2)(a) or (b) unless:

(a) the accused person has submitted that the Defence Force magistrate should give a ruling of that kind; or

(b) the interests of justice require that the Defence Force magistrate should give a ruling of that kind.

(a) the Defence Force magistrate, or the Defence Force Magistrate to whom the conviction is referred under subsection 129A(4), must take action under Part IV in relation to the convicted person; and

(2) Before a Defence Force magistrate takes action under Part IV, the Defence Force magistrate must hear submissions relevant to the determination of what action should be taken.

##### Subdivision C—Representation

###### 136F Representatives of parties before court martial or Defence Force magistrate

A person must not represent a party before a court martial or a Defence Force magistrate unless the person is:

(a) where the trial is held in Australia—a member of the Defence Force or a legal practitioner; or

(b) where the trial is held in a place outside Australia—a member of the Defence Force, a legal practitioner or a person qualified to practise before the courts of that place.

###### 136G Affording accused person representation

(1) To the extent that the exigencies of service permit, the Chief of the Defence Force must afford an accused person awaiting trial by a court martial or by a Defence Force magistrate the opportunity to be represented at the trial, and to be advised before the trial, by a legal officer.

(2) An accused person who is advised or represented in accordance with subsection (1) must be so advised or represented without expense to the accused person.

(3) Nothing in this section prevents the operation of any scheme of legal aid, advice or assistance under a law of the Commonwealth or of a State or Territory.

#### Division 2A—Powers in proceedings against person suffering a mental impairment

###### 137A Simplified outline of this Division

Where an accused person is suffering from a mental impairment in proceedings before a service tribunal that is a court martial or a Defence Force magistrate, the service tribunal may decide to:

(a) adjourn proceedings for up to 6 months where the judge advocate or Defence Force magistrate rules that continuing proceedings in the period is neither in the accused person’s interests nor in the interests of maintaining discipline in the Defence Force; or

(b) dismiss the charge where the judge advocate or Defence Force magistrate rules that trying or continuing to try the charge is not in the interests of maintaining discipline in the Defence Force.

A decision to dismiss the charge does not take effect until the decision is reviewed by a reviewing authority.

###### 137AA Application of this Division

This Division applies to a service tribunal that is a court martial or a Defence Force magistrate.

###### 137B Meaning of mental impairment

For the purposes of this Act, ***mental impairment*** has the same meaning as in the *Criminal Code*.

Note: Chapter 2 of the *Criminal Code* applies to service offences: see section 10 of this Act. For principles of responsibility for offences in relation to a person suffering from a mental impairment, see section 7.3 of the *Criminal Code*.

###### 137C Adjournment of proceedings

(1) At any stage of proceedings by a service tribunal in relation to a charge, the accused person may apply for an adjournment of the proceedings on the ground that the person is suffering from a mental impairment.

(2) An application for adjournment must:

(a) be accompanied by:

(i) a submission outlining the care, treatment or support the accused person needs due to suffering from a mental impairment; and

(ii) a report from a relevant medical practitioner outlining the nature, severity and expected duration of the mental impairment and the treatment plan; and

(b) comply with any other requirements prescribed by the regulations (if any).

(3) A ***relevant medical practitioner*** is:

(a) a medical practitioner who is a fellow of the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Psychiatrists; or

(b) a medical practitioner of a kind prescribed by the regulations.

(4) The service tribunal must order that the proceedings are adjourned for a specified period if:

(a) the judge advocate or Defence Force magistrate rules that continuing proceedings during that period is neither:

(i) in the accused person’s interests; nor

(ii) in the interests of maintaining discipline in the Defence Force; and

(b) the prosecution has had an opportunity to be heard on the decision to adjourn before the order is made.

(5) The service tribunal may not specify a period of adjournment that is longer than 6 months starting on the day the order takes effect.

(6) A service tribunal must not make an order under this section if the total period for which proceedings by any service tribunal in relation to the charge are suspended under this section is longer than 6 months.

(7) This section does not limit the powers of a service tribunal to adjourn a hearing under section 138.

###### 137D Action following adjournment

(1) If a service tribunal adjourns proceedings under section 137C, the accused person must, at the end of the adjournment period, provide the service tribunal with a report from a relevant medical practitioner outlining the nature, severity and expected duration of any mental impairment at that time.

(2) The report must be provided as soon as practicable after the end of the adjournment period.

(3) The service tribunal must decide whether to dismiss the charge under section 137E.

(4) If the service tribunal decides not to dismiss the charge, the service tribunal must continue the proceedings.

Note: Subject to subsection 137C(6), the service tribunal may make a further order under section 137C adjourning the proceedings.

###### 137E Dismissal of charge

(1) A service tribunal must dismiss a charge if, at any time before the accused person is found guilty or not guilty of the charge:

(a) the service tribunal is reasonably satisfied that the accused person is suffering from a mental impairment; and

(b) the judge advocate or Defence Force magistrate rules that trying or continuing to try the charge is not in the interests of maintaining discipline in the Defence Force; and

(c) the prosecution has had an opportunity to be heard.

(2) The following must be considered in deciding whether to form an opinion referred to in paragraph (1)(a) or make a ruling referred to in paragraph (1)(b):

(a) the nature, severity and expected duration of the mental impairment;

(b) any proposed treatment plan;

(c) any treatment that the accused person has had access to, the extent of the accused person’s participation in such treatment and the results of any such treatment;

(d) the risk of the accused person harming themselves or any part of the community;

(e) any prior application of this Division to the person in relation to any charge;

(f) the nature and seriousness of the charge and the circumstances in which the conduct is alleged to have occurred;

(g) the action that may potentially be taken under Part IV in relation to the charge;

(h) any material changes in the person’s circumstances since the conduct is alleged to have occurred;

(i) the person’s criminal record;

(j) any other relevant mitigating or aggravating circumstance referred to subsection 70(2).

(3) A judge advocate or a Defence Force magistrate is not required to make a ruling referred to in paragraph (1)(b) unless:

(a) the accused person has submitted that the judge advocate or Defence Force magistrate should do so; or

(b) the interests of justice require that the judge advocate or Defence Force magistrate should do so.

### Part IB—Summary contraventions

#### Division 1—Introduction

###### 9K Simplified outline of this Part

The summary contravention scheme applies in relation to defence members holding a rank of, or below, rear admiral, major‑general or air vice‑marshal, and certain other persons, who have contravened summary contravention provisions (see Schedule 2).

A person who is charged with a summary contravention will be given an opportunity to elect to instead be dealt with by a service tribunal for a service offence arising out of the contravention.

Summary contraventions are not service offences. However, if a person has been dealt with by a summary authority for a summary contravention, the person is not liable to be tried by a service tribunal for an offence arising out of the contravention and cannot be dealt with under the infringement scheme for a disciplinary infringement arising out of the contravention.

If the person chooses not to elect, the person may be dealt with by a summary authority. If the person chooses to elect, the person may be charged with a service offence arising out of the contravention and tried by a service tribunal.

A summary authority in dealing with a summary contravention may decide to:

(a) dismiss the charge, including where hearing the charge is not in the interests of maintaining discipline in the Defence Force; or

(b) find the person not guilty of the charge and dismiss the charge; or

(c) find the person guilty of the charge.

If the person is found guilty, a summary authority dealing with the charge will take action under Part IV (about punishments and orders) in relation to the person.

A decision to find the person guilty, or to dismiss the charge, including where hearing the charge is not in the interests of maintaining discipline in the Defence Force, does not take effect until the decision is reviewed under Part VIIIA by a reviewing authority.

###### 9KA Object of this Part

The object of this Part is to provide a means of dealing with service discipline matters that are not suitable to be dealt with through the disciplinary infringement scheme or are not serious enough to warrant being dealt with as a service offence that:

(a) is fair and efficient; and

(b) meets the disciplinary needs of the Defence Force.

#### Division 2—Key provisions

###### 9L Consequences of being dealt with by a summary authority

(1) A person who has been given an election notice under section 9P for a summary contravention must decide whether to elect to instead have a charge for a service offence arising out of the contravention tried by a service tribunal.

Note: For actions that may be taken by the Director of Military Prosecutions if the person elects, see subsections 103(4) and (5).

(2) If the person does not make an election under section 9P within the time specified in the election notice, the person may be dealt with by a summary authority for the contravention.

(3) The person may also be dealt with by a summary authority for the contravention if:

(a) the charge is referred to the summary authority by the Director of Military Prosecutions under this Act; or

(b) the person is not required under section 9PA to be given an election notice under section 9P in relation to the contravention.

Note: For action the Director of Military Prosecutions may take on referral of a charge, see section 9QA.

Consequences of being dealt with by a summary authority

(4) If a person has been dealt with by a summary authority for a summary contravention:

(a) the person is not liable to be tried by a service tribunal for a service offence arising out of the contravention; and

(b) the person may not be dealt with under the disciplinary infringement scheme in relation to a disciplinary infringement arising out of the contravention.

When a person has been dealt with by a summary authority

(5) A person has been dealt with by a summary authority for a summary contravention if:

(a) a summary authority has made a decision under Division 7:

(i) to dismiss the charge; or

(ii) to find the person guilty of the summary contravention; and

(b) for a decision to dismiss the charge under section 9RB (where hearing is not in the interests of maintaining discipline) or a decision to find the person guilty—that decision has been upheld on review.

Note: A person has not been dealt with by a summary authority for a summary contravention if there is a direction under this Act that the charge be not proceeded with.

#### Division 3—Summary contraventions

###### 9M When a summary contravention occurs

(1) A contravention of a summary contravention provision by a person is a ***summary contravention***.

(2) A provision in Schedule 2, or prescribed for the purposes of section 51 of Schedule 2, is a ***summary contravention provision***.

Note: For the punishments that may be imposed on a person for a summary contravention, see Subdivision C of Division 7.

(3) To avoid doubt, a summary contravention provision is not a service offence and Chapter 2 of the *Criminal Code* does not apply in relation to the contravention of a summary contravention provision.

Note: For the meaning of ***service offence***, see subsection 3(1).

###### 9MA Civil standard of proof for summary contravention

(1) A summary authority must find a person guilty of a summary contravention if the summary authority is satisfied that the contravention has been proved on the balance of probabilities.

(2) If, in proceedings for a summary contravention, a person wishes to rely on any exception, exemption, excuse, qualification or justification provided by this Act, then the person bears an evidential burden in relation to that matter.

###### 9MB No summary contravention if person has a reasonable excuse

(1) A person does not contravene a summary contravention provision if the person has a reasonable excuse.

Note: The person bears an evidential burden in relation to the matter in subsection (1): see subsection 9MA(2).

(2) The Summary Authority Rules may make provision for or in relation to determining when a person has a reasonable excuse.

###### 9MC No contravention if Act or omission in execution of law etc.

A person does not contravene a summary contravention provision by reason of an act or omission that:

(a) was in execution of the law; or

(b) was in obedience to:

(i) a lawful order; or

(ii) an unlawful order that the person did not know, and could not reasonably be expected to have known, was unlawful.

Note: The person bears an evidential burden in relation to the matter in this section: see subsection 9MA(2).

###### 9MD Determining recklessness and negligence

Subject to this Part, subsections 11(1), (2) and (3B) apply in relation to a summary contravention in the same way they apply to a commission of a service offence.

#### Division 4—Summons, arrest, custody and suspension from duty

###### 9N Application of Part V to summary contraventions

(1) Subject to this Part, Part V applies in relation to a summary contravention in the same way it applies to a commission of a service offence.

(2) Sections 87 (charging) and 87A (charge sheets) do not apply to a person charged with a summary contravention.

Note: For charging and summons of summary contraventions, see sections 9P and 9Q.

(3) A person may only be arrested under section 88, 89 or 90 in relation to a summary contravention provision if, at the time the person contravened the provision to which the contravention relates, the person was on active service.

(4) Paragraph 97(5)(a) applies as if that paragraph also referred to a direction by a summary authority that the charge not be proceeded with.

###### 9NA Charge sheets

(1) A charge against an accused person for a summary contravention must be entered in a charge sheet that is signed and dated by the authorised charging member who charged the person.

Note: The charge sheet and signature may be electronic: see section 10 of the *Electronic Transactions Act 1999*.

(2) A charge sheet for a summary contravention must be in accordance with any requirements prescribed by the Summary Authority Rules.

###### 9NB Admissibility of evidence collected under Part VI

Subject to section 9NC, any information, document or thing obtained under Part VI in relation to the investigation of a person for a service offence may be admissible in proceedings against the person for a summary contravention.

###### 9NC Admissibility of confessional evidence collected under Part VI

(1) Evidence of a confession made by a person in the presence of an investigating officer in relation to the investigation of the person for a service offence, is not admissible in proceedings against the person for a summary contravention unless the summary authority is satisfied that the confession was made voluntarily.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), a confession that is obtained from a person in consequence of:

(a) the use of physical violence, or a threat of physical violence, to any person; or

(b) the making of a promise, threat or other inducement (not being physical violence or a threat of physical violence) likely to cause the person to make a confession that is untrue;

is not made voluntarily.

#### Division 5—Charging and opportunity to elect

###### 9P Charging and opportunity to elect to be instead dealt with by a service tribunal

Charging of a summary contravention

(1) If an authorised charging member believes, on reasonable grounds, that a person has contravened a summary contravention provision, the authorised charging member may charge the person with the summary contravention.

Opportunity to elect must be given

(2) Subject to section 9PA, if the authorised charging member charges the person, the authorised charging member must, as soon as reasonably practicable:

(a) give a copy of the charge to the person; and

(b) give the person an election notice.

Note: Section 9PA provides for when the person does not have a further opportunity to elect.

(3) The election notice must:

(a) give particulars of the summary contravention to which the notice relates; and

(b) inform the member of the following:

(i) the person’s right to elect to be tried by a service tribunal for a service offence arising out of the contravention;

(ii) the period within which the person must decide whether to elect;

(iii) the punishments that may be imposed by a summary authority; and

(c) inform the person that:

(i) if the person does not elect, and the person is dealt with by a summary authority, the person is not liable to be tried by a service tribunal for an offence arising out of the contravention; and

(ii) if the person elects, the person may be charged with a service offence arising out of the contravention.

Note: There is no jurisdiction for a summary authority to deal with the charge if the person elects: see subsection 9QB(2). The Director of Military Prosecutions has discretion whether to charge the person with a service offence and as to which kind of service tribunal is to try the charge: see subsections 103(4) and (5).

(4) The specified period within which the person must decide to elect must be, starting at the time the person is given the election notice:

(a) 24 hours; or

(b) if the exigencies of service do not permit the person to make the decision within 24 hours—a period not exceeding 14 days.

Note 1: The person may make the decision at any time during the specified period.

Note 2: For example, a longer period may be specified under paragraph (b) where the exigencies of service affect the opportunity for the person to obtain legal advice within 24 hours.

(5) The person must be given an opportunity to obtain legal advice in relation to the election if a legal officer is reasonably available to give such advice.

(6) An election notice must be in accordance with a form approved by the Chief of the Defence Force.

Elections for multiple charges

(7) If the charge is entered in a charge sheet with other charges:

(a) the person must be given one election notice for all the charges on the charge sheet; and

(b) the person must make one decision whether to elect, or not elect, for all of the charges on the charge sheet.

Note: For charge sheets, see section 9NA.

(8) If the person purports to only elect for some charges on the charge sheet, the person is taken to have elected for all of the charges.

###### 9PA No further opportunity to elect

An authorised charging member is not required to give an accused person an election notice under section 9P in relation to a charge of a summary contravention if:

(a) the charge has been referred from a summary authority; or

(b) both of the following apply:

(i) the person has previously been charged with a summary contravention in respect of that conduct;

(ii) a summary authority or the Director of Military Prosecutions has directed under this Act that the charge not be proceeded with.

###### 9PB Election may be withdrawn before plea

(1) If a person elects under section 9P to be tried by a service tribunal for a service offence arising out of a summary contravention, the person may withdraw the election at any time before the person is asked to plead in relation to a charge of a service offence arising out of the summary contravention.

(2) If the charge is entered in a charge sheet with other charges, the person is taken to have withdrawn the election for all the charges.

#### Division 6—Summary authorities

###### 9Q Referral of charge to summary authority and order or summons to appear

(1) If an authorised charging member charges a person with having contravened a summary contravention provision, an authorised charging member must refer the charge to a summary authority if:

(a) the person has been given an election notice under section 9P and:

(i) the person does not elect to be tried by a service tribunal for an offence arising out of the contravention; or

(ii) the person does not make a decision whether or not to elect within the period specified in the election notice; or

(iii) the person withdraws the election in accordance with section 9PB; or

(b) the person is not required to be given an election notice under section 9P.

Order or summons to appear before summary authority

(2) If the charge has been referred to a summary authority under this Act, an authorised charging member may:

(a) if the person is a defence member—order the defence member to appear before the summary authority at a specified time and place for the purposes of being dealt with under this Part; or

(b) whether or not the person is a defence member—summon the person to appear before the summary authority at a specified time and place for the purposes of being dealt with under this Part.

(3) A summons under paragraph (2)(b) must be served on the person to whom it is directed in a manner specified in the Summary Authority Rules.

###### 9QA Courses open to Director of Military Prosecutions

(1) If a charge of a summary contravention has been referred to the Director of Military Prosecutions under this Act, the Director of Military Prosecutions may do one or more of the following:

(a) direct that the charge be not proceeded with;

(b) refer the charge back to the summary authority who referred the charge;

(c) refer the charge to another summary authority;

(d) charge the person under subsection 9P(1) with one or more other summary contraventions;

(e) refer one or more of the other charges back to the summary authority who referred the first charge or to another summary authority;

(f) take action under subsection (2).

Note: If the Director of Military Prosecutions refers a charge to a summary authority, an authorised charging member may order or summons the accused person to appear before the summary authority: see subsection 9Q(2).

(2) If a summary authority refers a charge of a summary contravention to the Director of Military Prosecutions, the Director of Military Prosecutions may:

(a) direct that the charge be not proceeded with; and

(b) charge the person with a service offence arising out of the contravention.

Note: The Director of Military Prosecutions has discretion as to which kind of service tribunal is to try the charge of the service offence: see subsection 103(6).

(3) Sections 9P and 9NA apply to a charge by the Director of Military Prosecutions under paragraph (1)(d) of this section in the same way those sections apply to a charge by an authorised charging member.

Note: For example, the Director of Military Prosecutions must have reasonable grounds to believe the person has contravened the provisions to which the other charges relate and, unless section 9PA applies, the person must be given an election notice in relation to the other charges.

###### 9QB Jurisdiction to deal with a summary contravention

(1) A summary authority has jurisdiction to deal with a summary contravention under this Part if:

(a) the accused person is not charged with a service offence arising out of the contravention and section 144 would not prevent the person from being tried for a service offence arising out of the contravention; and

(b) the accused person is not being dealt with, and has not been dealt with, under the disciplinary infringement scheme for a disciplinary infringement arising out of the contravention; and

(c) where the accused person is a defence member—the member holds a rank of, or below, rear admiral, major‑general or air vice‑marshal and:

(i) if the charge is dealt with by the summary authority while the member is on active service—is one or more ranks junior to the summary authority; or

(ii) in any other case—is 2 or more ranks junior to the summary authority.

Note: A summary authority may be disqualified from trying a charge under subsection 9QC(1).

(2) A summary authority does not have jurisdiction to deal with a summary contravention if:

(a) the person has elected to be tried by a service tribunal for a service offence arising out of the contravention; and

(b) that election has not been withdrawn under section 9PB.

###### 9QC Disqualification of summary authority from dealing with a summary contravention

(1) A summary authority must not deal with a summary contravention under this Part if the summary authority was involved in:

(a) the investigation of the contravention or of a service offence arising out of the contravention; or

(b) the issuing of a warrant for the arrest of the person in relation to the contravention or of a service offence arising out of the contravention; or

(c) charging the person with the contravention or with a service offence arising out of the contravention.

(2) If a summary authority is not permitted to deal with a summary contravention because of subsection (1), the summary authority must refer the charge, and may refer any other charge entered in the charge sheet, to another summary authority who:

(a) has jurisdiction to deal with the charge; and

(b) is not prevented from dealing with the charge because of subsection (1).

#### Division 7—Summary authority proceedings

##### Subdivision A—Hearing of charges

###### 9R Procedural hearings

(1) A summary authority dealing with a charge may:

(a) hold hearings in relation to the proceedings; and

(b) give directions or rulings in relation to the proceedings; and

(c) direct the parties to attend (in person or otherwise) before the summary authority for the purposes of such hearings or making such directions or rulings; and

(d) permit the accused person not to be present for such hearings, directions or rulings.

(2) The powers in subsection (1) may be exercised on the summary authority’s own initiative or on application by a party to the proceedings.

###### 9RA Hearings to determine whether to hear the charge

(1) At any time before hearing a charge, the summary authority dealing with the charge may hold hearings to determine whether to hear the charge.

(2) If the summary authority considers that it is desirable in the interests of justice or for any other reason for the charge to be dealt with by another summary authority, the summary authority may refer the charge, and any other charge entered in the charge sheet, to another summary authority.

(3) If the summary authority is of the opinion that there will be insufficient evidence to support a finding of guilt, the summary authority may direct that the charge be not proceeded with.

###### 9RB Dismissal of charge where hearing is not in the interests of maintaining discipline in the Defence Force

If the summary authority dealing with a charge is of the opinion that hearing the charge, or continuing to hear the charge, is not in the interests of maintaining discipline in the Defence Force, the authority must dismiss the charge.

Note: A decision to dismiss a charge does not take effect until it is upheld on review: see subsections 9L(5) and 9U(2).

###### 9RC Pleas

(1) A summary authority dealing with a charge may, during a procedural hearing, ask the accused person whether the person pleads guilty or not guilty to the charge.

Guilty plea

(2) If the accused person pleads guilty, and the summary authority is satisfied that the accused person understands the effect of that plea, the summary authority must:

(a) record a plea of guilty; and

(b) find the person guilty of the summary contravention.

Not guilty plea

(3) The summary authority must record a plea of not guilty if the accused person:

(a) pleads not guilty; or

(b) pleads guilty but the summary authority is not satisfied that the accused person understands the effect of that plea; or

(c) refuses to plead; or

(d) does not plead intelligibly.

###### 9RD Hearing of the charge

(1) If a plea of not guilty by an accused person is recorded, the summary authority dealing with the charge must proceed to hear the evidence on the charge.

Finding of sufficient or insufficient evidence

(2) If after hearing the evidence on the charge adduced by the prosecution:

(a) the summary authority finds that the evidence is insufficient to support the charge—the summary authority must dismiss the charge; or

(b) the summary authority finds that the evidence is sufficient to support the charge—the summary authority must continue with the hearing.

Finding about guilt

(3) If the summary authority finds the accused person is not guilty of the charge, the summary authority must dismiss the charge.

(4) If the summary authority finds the accused person is guilty of the charge, the summary authority must find the person guilty of the summary contravention.

Withdrawal of not guilty plea during hearing

(5) If at any time before the summary authority finds the accused person guilty or not guilty of the charge:

(a) the accused person withdraws their plea of not guilty; and

(b) the summary authority is satisfied that the accused person understands the effect of that plea;

the summary authority must find the person guilty.

No requirement to make a finding under subsection (2)

(6) The summary authority is not required to make a finding of the kind referred to in paragraph (2)(a) or (b) unless:

(a) the accused person has submitted that the summary authority should make a finding of that kind; or

(b) the interests of justice require that the summary authority should make a finding of that kind.

###### 9RE Time within which hearing of charge must commence

(1) The hearing of a charge by a summary authority:

(a) must be commenced:

(i) as soon as practicable within the period of 30 days beginning on the day the accused person is charged; or

(ii) if the exigencies of service or other circumstances do not permit the hearing to be commenced within this period—within a longer period (not exceeding 6 months) as allowed by a superior authority; and

(b) must be completed as soon as practicable.

(2) If the summary authority dealing with the charge does not commence the hearing within the period required under subsection (1), the summary authority must refer the charge, and may refer any other charge entered in the charge sheet, to the Director of Military Prosecutions.

Note: For when the charge is referred to the Director of Military Prosecutions, see section 9QA.

###### 9RF Determination of action under Part IV

(1) If, under this Division, an accused person is found guilty of a summary contravention:

(a) a summary authority dealing with the charge must take action under Part IV in relation to the guilty person; and

(b) the summary authority who takes action must give reasons for each punishment imposed and each order made under Part IV in relation to the guilty person.

(2) Before a summary authority takes action under Part IV, the summary authority must hear evidence relevant to the determination of what action should be taken.

###### 9RG Referrals to Director of Military Prosecutions

Referral where desirable in the interests of justice

(1) A summary authority dealing with a charge, may at any stage of the proceedings, refer the charge to the Director of Military Prosecutions if the summary authority considers that it is desirable in the interests of justice that the charge be so referred.

(2) Subsection (1) does not affect the operation of any other provision of this Act.

Referral where precluded from taking appropriate action under Part IV

(3) A summary authority dealing with a charge must refer the charge to the Director of Military Prosecutions if the summary authority is of the opinion:

##### Subdivision B—Procedure of summary authorities

###### 9S Natural justice rules etc. in proceedings before a summary authority

(1) In proceedings before a summary authority dealing with a charge, the summary authority must comply with:

(a) the rules of natural justice; and

(b) the Summary Authority Rules.

(2) Nothing in this section allows a person to be compelled to testify against themself, or to give particular evidence, in proceedings before a summary authority, if doing so might tend to incriminate the person or expose the person to a penalty.

(3) This section does not affect the law relating to legal professional privilege.

###### 9SA The Summary Authority Rules

(1) The Judge Advocate General may, by legislative instrument, make rules (the ***Summary Authority Rules***) prescribing matters:

(a) required or permitted by this Act to be prescribed by the Summary Authority Rules; or

(b) necessary or convenient to be prescribed for carrying out or giving effect to Part IB.

(2) Without limiting subsection (1), the Summary Authority Rules may provide for the practice and procedure to be followed by summary authorities in making decisions in relation to summary contraventions.

(3) Without limiting subsection (2), the Summary Authority Rules may make provision for or in relation to, the following:

(a) the attendance and compellability of witnesses;

(b) the giving of testimony and other evidence;

(c) the production of documents;

(d) representation of parties;

(e) the forms to be used in relation to proceedings;

(f) the service of any process of a summary authority;

(g) the manner and form of charges for summary contraventions;

(h) charge sheets for summary contraventions;

(i) amendments of charges and charge sheets;

(j) the grounds for, manner and form of making, applications and objections, in or in relation to proceedings;

(k) the recording of proceedings;

(l) the reopening of proceedings on request by a reviewing authority.

###### 9SB Accused person to be present at hearing

(1) A hearing of a charge before a summary authority must be held in the presence (including by video link or audio link where allowed by the summary authority) of the accused person.

(2) If:

(a) because of exceptional circumstances, the accused person is unable to attend the hearing of the charge; and

(b) the accused person pleads guilty to the charge in writing before the hearing;

either party to the proceedings may apply in writing to the summary authority for an order permitting the accused person not to be present at the hearing.

(3) The summary authority may make an order permitting the accused person not to be present at the hearing if the authority:

(a) is satisfied that the accused person understands the effect of the plea; and

(b) considers that exceptional circumstances exist.

(4) If the summary authority makes an order under subsection (3), the summary authority must proceed with the hearing of the charge by considering, without holding a hearing, the documents or other material provided to the summary authority in relation to the charge.

###### 9SC Objections

(1) At any time before the completion of a hearing of a charge by a summary authority, the accused person may enter an objection to the charge on any ground, including any of the following grounds:

(a) that, because of subsection 9C(2) or section 9L or 144, the person is not liable to be dealt with by a summary authority for the charge;

(b) that the charge was made in contravention of section 96 (time limitation);

(c) that the person has been charged for a civil court offence that is substantially the same as the summary contravention;

(d) that the charge does not disclose a summary contravention or is otherwise wrong in law;

(e) that the summary authority does not have jurisdiction (see section 9QB);

(f) that the summary authority is not permitted to deal with the charge because of subsection 9QC(1).

Note: The Summary Authority Rules may make provision in relation to applications and objections.

(2) At any time before an accused person is asked to plead by a summary authority, the accused person may enter an objection to the summary authority on the ground that the summary authority is ineligible.

(3) Nothing in subsection (2) is to be taken, by implication, to authorise proceedings by a summary authority who:

(a) is, or is likely to be, biased; or

(b) is likely to be thought, on reasonable grounds, to be biased.

(4) A summary authority is not to be regarded:

(a) as biased; or

(b) as likely to be biased;

in relation to the hearing of a charge against an accused person merely because the summary authority is a superior in the chain of command of the accused person.

(5) The circumstance that a summary authority is the commanding officer or a superior in the chain of command of an accused person is not, without more, a reasonable ground for thinking that, in relation to the hearing of a charge against the accused person, the summary authority is biased.

(6) Where a summary authority allows an objection under this section, the summary authority may refer the charge against the accused person, and any other charge entered in the charge sheet, to another summary authority.

##### Subdivision C—Consequences for contraventions

###### 9T Application of Part IV to summary contraventions

(1) Subject to this Subdivision, Part IV applies to a person found guilty of a summary contravention in the same way it applies to a convicted person in relation to a service offence.

(2) Section 68 applies to the guilty person as if:

(a) that section did not include paragraphs 68(1)(a) to (c); and

(b) paragraph 68(1)(d) were omitted and the following paragraph were substituted:

“(d) if the person was a defence member on active service when the person contravened the provision to which the finding relates—detention for not more than 42 days;”.

(3) Section 70 does not apply to the guilty person. However, in determining what action should be taken in relation to a guilty person, a summary authority is to take into account:

(a) the mitigating or aggravating circumstances set out in subsection 70(2); and

(b) the need to maintain discipline in the Defence Force.

(4) The amount that the guilty person may be ordered to pay by a summary authority under section 84 must not exceed:

(a) where the guilty person is a defence member—the amount of the person’s pay for 28 days; or

(b) in any other case—5 times the maximum fine that the summary authority could impose on the guilty person.

###### 9TA Scale of custodial punishments

(1) The only punishments that may be imposed by a summary authority on a person found guilty of a custodial contravention are, in decreasing order of severity, as follows:

(a) segregated confinement for a period not exceeding 10 days;

(b) confinement to cell for a period not exceeding 10 days;

(c) extra drill for a period not exceeding 6 days;

(d) restriction of custodial privileges for a period not exceeding 14 days.

(2) The Chief of the Defence Force may, by legislative instrument, make rules with respect to the consequences, in relation to a detainee, that are to flow from the imposition by a summary authority on that detainee of any custodial punishment.

(3) The officer in charge of a detention centre in which a detainee subject to a custodial punishment is undergoing a punishment of detention may moderate the consequences of the custodial punishment in relation to the detainee in such manner as the officer thinks appropriate having regard to:

(a) the particular circumstances of the case; and

(b) any directions, in writing, of the Chief of the Defence Force.

(1) A summary authority must not impose a punishment in respect of a finding of guilt for a summary contravention except in accordance with this Subdivision and Part IV.

Note: Where a summary authority would be precluded by this section from imposing a punishment that the summary authority considers warranted by the level of misconduct (for example, where the misconduct is of a more serious nature due to the seniority of the member), the summary authority may refer the charge to the Director of Military Prosecutions: see subsection 9RG(3). The misconduct may then instead be dealt with by a service tribunal which can impose more serious punishments: see subsection 9QA(2) and section 69A.

A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this section if:

(a) the person assaults another person; and

(b) that other person is a member of the Defence Force or of an allied force; and

(c) that other person is on guard duty.

###### 11 Summary contravention—obstructing a police member

(1) A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the person obstructs another person; and

(b) that other person:

(i) is a police member acting in the performance of the police member’s duty; or

(ii) is lawfully exercising authority under or on behalf of a service police officer.

(2) A defence member contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the member is called on to assist another person; and

(b) that other person:

(i) is a police member acting in the performance of the police member’s duty; or

(ii) is lawfully exercising authority under or on behalf of a service police officer; and

(c) the member refuses to assist that other person.

###### 12 Summary contravention—person on guard or on watch

A defence member contravenes this section if the member is on guard duty or on watch and the member:

(a) sleeps at the member’s post or on watch; or

(b) is not on duty at a post but sleeps when the member’s duty requires the member to be awake; or

(c) is intoxicated; or

(d) leaves their post before being regularly relieved or otherwise absents themself from a place where it is the member’s duty to be.

###### 13 Summary contravention—assault, insulting or provocative words etc.

A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this section if the person:

(a) assaults another person; or

(b) creates a disturbance or takes part in creating or continuing a disturbance; or

(c) within the view or hearing of another person, engages in conduct that is obscene; or

(d) uses insulting or provocative words to another person or otherwise harasses another person.

###### 14 Summary contravention—assaulting a subordinate

A defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) the member:

(i) assaults a person; or

(ii) ill‑treats a person with violence; or

(iii) otherwise ill‑treats a person; and

(b) the person is a member of the Defence Force who is of subordinate rank to the member.

#### Division 3—Contraventions relating to performance of duty

###### 15 Summary contravention—negligence in performance of a duty

A person who is a defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) the person is required, because of the person’s office or appointment, to perform a duty; and

(b) the person engages in conduct; and

(c) that conduct results in a failure to perform the duty to the required standard; and

(d) the person is negligent as to that result.

Note: For the meaning of ***required standard***, see subsection 3(1).

###### 16 Summary contravention—failure to perform duty or carry out activity

A defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) the member’s office or appointment, or the requirements of the Defence Force, require the member to perform a duty or carry out an activity; and

(b) the member fails to perform the duty or carry out the activity.

###### 17 Summary contravention—unauthorised discharge of weapon

A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this section if:

(a) the person engages in conduct; and

(b) the conduct causes, or contributes to, the discharge of a weapon; and

(c) the discharge of the weapon is not authorised.

###### 18 Summary contravention—negligent discharge of weapon

A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this section if:

(a) the person engages in conduct; and

(b) the conduct causes, or contributes to, the discharge of a weapon; and

(c) the person is negligent as to that result.

###### 19 Summary contravention—intoxicated while on duty etc.

A defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) the member is on duty, or reports or should report for duty; and

(b) the member is intoxicated.

###### 20 Summary contravention—rendering or falsely representing oneself as unfit for duty or service etc.

(1) A defence member contravenes this subsection if, with intent to make or keep themself unfit for duty or service, the member:

(a) injures themself or causes or permits themself to be injured; or

(b) by act or omission, causes themself to suffer from a sickness or disability or prolongs or aggravates a sickness or disability from which the member is suffering.

(2) A defence member contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the member represents themself to be suffering from a physical or mental condition; and

(b) the member makes the representation with intent to avoid duty or service; and

(c) the representation is false; and

(d) the member knows that the representation is false.

#### Division 4—Contraventions relating to ships, vehicles, aircraft and weapons

###### 21 Summary contravention—driving while intoxicated

(1) A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the person drives a service vehicle in any place, whether a public place or not; and

(2) A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the person drives a vehicle on service land; and

(b) the person is under the influence of intoxicating liquor or a drug to such an extent as to be incapable of having proper control of the vehicle.

###### 22 Summary contravention—dangerous driving

(1) A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the person drives a service vehicle in any place, whether a public place or not; and

(2) A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the person drives a vehicle on service land; and

(b) the person does so at a speed, or in a manner, dangerous to another person on that land.

###### 23 Summary contravention—driving a service vehicle for unauthorised purpose

A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this section if:

(a) the person:

(i) drives a service vehicle in any place, whether a public place or not; and

(ii) is not authorised to drive that vehicle; or

(b) the person uses a service vehicle for an unauthorised purpose.

###### 24 Summary contravention—driving without due care or attention etc.

(1) A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the person drives a service vehicle in any place, whether a public place or not; and

(b) the person does so without due care and attention or without reasonable consideration for another person in that place.

(2) A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the person drives a vehicle on service land; and

(b) the person does so without due care and attention or without reasonable consideration for another person on that land.

###### 25 Summary contravention—low flying

A defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) the member flies a service aircraft; and

(b) by or in accordance with a lawful general order, there is a minimum height at which the member is authorised to fly; and

(c) the height at which the member flies is less than that minimum height; and

(d) the member is reckless or negligent as to the matter in paragraph (c).

###### 26 Summary contravention—inaccurate certification in relation to ships, aircraft, vehicles etc.

A person who is a defence member or a defence civilian contravenes this section if:

(a) the person gives a certificate, makes or signs a document or makes an entry in a document; and

(b) the certificate, document or entry relates to any matter affecting the safety or efficiency of a service ship, service aircraft, service vehicle, service missile or service weapon; and

(c) the person does not take reasonable care to ensure the accuracy of the certificate, document or entry.

###### 50 Summary contravention—refusing to submit to medical examination etc.

(1) A person contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the person is in lawful custody in respect of a service offence; and

(b) an investigating officer has arranged for a medical practitioner to examine the person for the purpose of securing evidence of, or relating to, the service offence; and

(c) either:

(i) the person has given their consent in writing to the examination; or

(ii) an authorised officer has, under subsection 101Q(4), approved the examination; and

(d) the investigating officer has informed the person that refusal or failure by the person to submit to the examination will constitute an offence; and

(2) A person contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the person is in lawful custody in respect of a service offence; and

(b) an investigating officer has arranged for a medical practitioner to take a specimen from the person; and

(i) the person has given their consent in writing to the taking of the specimen; or

(ii) an authorised officer has, under subsection 101Q(4), approved the taking of the specimen; and

(e) the investigating officer has informed the person that refusal or failure by the person:

(i) to submit to the taking of the specimen; or

(ii) to do any act reasonably necessary to enable the specimen to be taken;

will constitute an offence; and

(f) after being so informed, the person:

(i) refuses or fails to submit to the taking of the specimen; or

(ii) refuses or fails to do an act reasonably necessary to enable the specimen to be taken.

(3) Nothing in this section renders a person guilty of a summary contravention for refusing to submit to a medical examination or the taking of a specimen to the extent that the person proves that the examination or the taking of the specimen, as the case may be, was not reasonably necessary for the purpose of obtaining evidence relating to the presence or absence of a prohibited drug in the blood or urine of a person.

###### 2A Simplified outline of this Act

This Act provides means of dealing with service discipline matters. There are 3 schemes graduated based on the severity of the discipline matter.

Minor service discipline matters are dealt with under the infringement scheme in Part IA. This scheme applies in relation to prescribed defence members (junior officers and lower ranks) who have contravened disciplinary infringement provisions (see Schedule 1). The most severe punishment that may be imposed is a fine.

More serious service discipline matters that are not suitable to be dealt with through the disciplinary infringement scheme are dealt with under the summary contravention scheme in Part IB. This scheme applies in relation to defence members holding a rank of, or below, rear admiral, major‑general or air vice‑marshal, and certain other persons, who have contravened summary contravention provisions (see Schedule 2). The most severe punishment that may be imposed is a reduction in rank, or in some active service situations, detention for up to 42 days.

Matters that are serious enough to warrant being dealt with as a service offence are dealt with by service tribunals. A service tribunal may deal with any person who has committed an offence in Part III. Part III includes offences for engaging in conduct in the Jervis Bay Territory that is a Territory offence. A service offence is an offence against a law of the Commonwealth. The most severe punishment that may be imposed is imprisonment.

Conduct may constitute a disciplinary infringement, a summary contravention and a service offence. If a person is dealt with under one scheme, the person cannot be dealt with under either other scheme for that conduct.

If a person is given an infringement notice or charged with a summary contravention, the person will have an opportunity to elect whether to be dealt with under that scheme. If the person doesn’t elect the person may be dealt with under a more serious scheme.

Decisions to impose a punishment or make certain other orders are subject to review before taking effect. Part IA decisions are reviewed by a commanding officer. Decisions of the other schemes are reviewed under Part VIIIA. Certain decisions of service tribunals are reviewable under the *Defence Force Discipline Appeals Act 1955*.

#### Division 7A—Disclosure of investigation information etc.

##### Subdivision A—Disclosure for proceedings before a service tribunal

###### 101ZBA Application of this Division

This Division applies to a service tribunal that is a court martial or a Defence Force magistrate.

###### 101ZBB Handling of sensitive material in proceedings before a service tribunal

(1) The regulations may make provision for, or in relation to, information, documents or things that are to be treated as sensitive material for the purposes of proceedings before a service tribunal.

(2) The Court Martial and Defence Force Magistrate Rules may make provision for, or in relation to, the handling of sensitive material in proceedings before a service tribunal.

###### 101ZBC Disclosure by investigating officer in charge to Director of Military Prosecutions

Pre‑charge disclosure

(1) The investigating officer in charge of investigating a service offence must, before a person is charged with the service offence, disclose to the Director of Military Prosecutions all information, documents and things mentioned in subsection (2).

(2) The following information, documents and things must be disclosed:

(a) any information, document or thing in the possession of or which exists to the knowledge of the investigating officer that:

(i) is relevant to a fact in issue or to the reliability or credibility of a witness; or

(ii) might reasonably be expected to assist the case for the accused person; or

(iii) is adverse to the credit or credibility of the accused person;

(b) a list of the names of witnesses;

(c) a list of exhibits;

(d) a list specifying exhibits that are to be treated as sensitive material.

Ongoing disclosure

(3) An investigating officer must provide the Director of Military Prosecutions with any information, documents or things that the Director of Military Prosecutions requires to comply with section 101ZBD in relation to a charge.

Certification

(4) The regulations may make provision for, or in relation to, certification of information provided to the Director of Military Prosecutions for the purposes of this section.

###### 101ZBD Disclosure by prosecution of relevant material to accused person

(1) If an accused person is charged with a service offence, the Director of Military Prosecutions must provide the accused person with a copy of, or access to, all information, documents and things mentioned in subsection (2) that are in the Director’s possession.

(2) The following information, documents and things must be provided:

(a) the charge sheet on which the charge is entered;

(b) if the matter has been referred from a summary authority—the record of evidence in relation to the charge taken at any proceedings before a summary authority, together with all documents relating to those proceedings;

(c) a case statement summarising the alleged facts and description of evidence that may be led by the prosecution in relation to each element of the charge;

(d) a list of the names of prosecution witnesses;

(e) a list of prosecution exhibits;

(f) a list specifying prosecution exhibits that are to be treated as sensitive material;

(g) each witness statement from a witness mentioned in paragraph (d);

(h) any information, document or thing in the possession of or which exists to the knowledge of the prosecuting officer that:

(i) is relevant to a fact in issue or to the reliability or credibility of a witness mentioned in paragraph (d); or

(ii) might reasonably be expected to assist the case for the accused person; or

(iii) is adverse to the credit or credibility of the accused person.

Note: For example, expert reports must be provided. See also section 101ZBE for disclosure of expert reports.

(3) Information is in the Director’s possession only if:

(a) it has been provided to the Director under section 101ZBC; or

(b) it is in the possession of the arresting officer or an investigating officer for the charge; or

(c) it is in the possession of a person appearing for the prosecution; or

(d) a person referred to in paragraph (b) or (c);

(i) is aware of the existence of the thing; and

(ii) is, or would be, able to locate the thing without unreasonable effort.

(4) Information must be provided to the accused person under subsection (1):

(a) within 28 days of the date the accused person is charged; and

(b) if information comes into the possession of the Director after the day information is first provided to the accused person under subsection (1)—as soon as reasonably practicable after the information comes into the possession of the Director.

###### 101ZBE Pre‑trial disclosure of expert reports

If a party to proceedings before a service tribunal intends to rely on an expert report, the party must give the other party a copy of the expert report:

(a) within a reasonable time before the trial commences or at such other time as directed by the judge advocate or Defence Force magistrate; or

(b) if the expert report is obtained by the party during proceedings—within a reasonable time after the party obtains the report or at such other time as directed by the judge advocate or Defence Force magistrate.

###### 101ZBF Adverse comment may be made if information not disclosed as required

A party (the ***affected party***) to proceedings before a service tribunal may apply to the judge advocate or Defence Force magistrate for adverse comment about the other party’s failure to comply with this Subdivision if:

(a) the affected party reasonably believes that the other party was required by this Subdivision to disclose to the affected party information, a document or a thing; and

(b) either the information, document or thing was not disclosed, or was not disclosed in the time required.

##### Subdivision B—Disclosure for law enforcement purposes etc.

###### 101ZBG Meaning of investigation information

***Investigation information*** is information, documents or things obtained by an investigating officer in the investigation of a service offence under this Act or the Regulatory Powers Act.

###### 101ZBH Disclosure to a court, tribunal etc.

(1) An investigating officer may disclose investigation information to a court exercising federal jurisdiction.

(2) An investigating officer may disclose investigation information to:

(a) a court; or

(b) a tribunal, authority or person that has the power to require the answering of questions or the production of documents;

for the purposes of the enforcement of a law of the Commonwealth or to assist the court, tribunal, authority or person to make or review an administrative decision that is required or authorised to be made or reviewed under a law of the Commonwealth.

(3) An investigating officer may disclose investigation information to a Commonwealth, State or Territory court or tribunal as required by an order or direction of the court or tribunal.

###### 101ZBJ Disclosure for the purposes of law enforcement

(1) An investigating officer may disclose investigation information to a body mentioned in subsection (2) if:

(a) disclosing the information is for:

(i) the enforcement of the criminal law; or

(ii) the enforcement of a law imposing a pecuniary penalty; or

(iii) the protection of public revenue; and

(b) the functions of the body include that enforcement or protection; and

(c) for a body mentioned in paragraph (2)(b) or (d)—the body has undertaken not to use or further disclose the information except in accordance with an agreement that:

(i) is in force between the Commonwealth and the State or Territory; and

(ii) applies in relation to the information.

(2) The bodies are the following:

(a) a Commonwealth entity (within the meaning of the *Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013*);

(b) a State or Territory body;

(c) the Australian Federal Police;

(d) the police force or police service of a State or Territory.

(3) For the purposes of paragraph (2)(b), a ***State or Territory body*** includes a Department of State, or an authority or agency, of a State or Territory.

###### 101ZBK Handling and disclosure of sensitive material

(1) The regulations may provide for, or in relation to:

(a) the handling of sensitive material obtained under this Act or the Regulatory Powers Act, including information, documents or things that are to be treated as sensitive material for the purposes of proceedings before a service tribunal; and

(b) the disclosure of such information as required or permitted under this Act.

(2) However, regulations for the purposes of subsection (1) may not make provision for, or in relation to, the handling or disclosure of sensitive material in proceedings before a service tribunal.

Note: For handling of sensitive material in proceedings, see 101ZBB.

(3) If regulations for the purposes of subsection (1) provide that sensitive information must not be disclosed in particular circumstances, then despite any other provision of this Act (other than subsection 190A(1A)), that information must not be disclosed in any of those circumstance.

(i) a person engaging in sexual intercourse or another form of sexual activity with another person of the same sex; or

(ii) a person procuring another person of the same sex to engage in sexual intercourse or another sexual activity with a person of the same sex.

Example: Offences under the *Army Act 1881* (Imperial) and th*e Naval Discipline Act 1866* (Imperial) are covered by this subsection to the extent those Acts were applied by or under an Act referred to in paragraph (a) of this subsection.

(3) An offence is also ***historical homosexual service offence*** if it is an offence consisting of attempting, conspiring or inciting, the commission of an offence referred to in subsection (2).

(4) An authorised officer must extinguish the conviction if:

(a) the authorised officer is not aware of any grounds for suspecting that the conduct was non‑consensual; and

(b) the authorised officer is satisfied that the conduct involved in the offence would not have constituted an offence under an Act referred to in paragraph (2)(b) if it were instead a person engaging in, or procuring, conduct with a person of a different sex; and

(c) if the application is by another person with a special interest in the conviction—the authorised officer is satisfied that it is appropriate in the circumstances for the application to be made by that person.

(5) Authorised officers and the Chief of the Defence Force may collect, use and disclose personal information (within the meaning of the *Privacy Act 1988*), including sensitive personal information (within the meaning of that Act), for the purposes of making a decision under this section.

(6) If an authorised officer or the Chief of the Defence Force extinguishes a conviction under this section:

(a) the conviction must be expunged from the person’s service record, conduct record and unit record; and

(b) the conviction must not be disclosed under section 190A or 190B or Division 7A of Part VI; and

(c) the person is not required to disclose to any person, for any purpose, the fact that the person has been charged with, or convicted of, the offence; and

(d) it is lawful for the person to claim on oath or otherwise, that the person was not charged with, or convicted of, the offence; and

(e) anyone else who knows, or could reasonably be expected to know, that the offence has been expunged must not:

(i) without the person’s consent, disclose the fact that the person was charged with, or convicted of, the offence to any other person, or to a Commonwealth authority or State authority; or

(ii) take account of the fact that the person was charged with, or convicted of, the offence.

Internal review of decision by the Chief of the Defence Force

(7) The applicant or another person affected by a decision of an authorised officer under this section may apply to the Chief of the Defence Force for review of the authorised officer’s decision.

(8) The Chief of the Defence Force must review the decision and:

(a) affirm the decision; or

(b) set aside the decision and make a new decision in substitution for the decision set aside.

## Schedule 1—Disciplinary infringement provisions

Note: See subsection 9D(2).

### Part 1—Preliminary

###### 1 Disciplinary infringement provisions

For the purposes of subsection 9D(2), this Schedule sets out disciplinary infringement provisions.

### Part 2—Minor disciplinary infringement provisions

###### 2 Disciplinary infringement provision—absent from duty

(1) A prescribed defence member contravenes this subsection if the member:

(a) is required to attend for duty; and

(b) does not attend for the duty.

(2) A prescribed defence member contravenes this subsection if the member:

(a) is required to perform a duty; and

(b) ceases to perform the duty before the member is permitted to do so.

###### 3 Disciplinary infringement provision—absent without leave not exceeding 3 hours

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if the member:

(a) is absent without leave; and

(b) the absence is for a period not exceeding 3 hours.

Note: A senior discipline officer may also have jurisdiction to deal with a contravention of the disciplinary infringement provision in section 9 of this Schedule which covers absence without leave for a period not exceeding 24 hours.

###### 4 Disciplinary infringement provision—disobeying a lawful command

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) a person gives the member a lawful command; and

(b) the person giving the command is a superior officer; and

(c) the member disobeys the command.

###### 5 Disciplinary infringement provision—failing to comply with a general order

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) a lawful general order applies to the member; and

(b) the member does not comply with the order.

###### 6 Disciplinary infringement provision—member on guard or on watch

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if the member is on guard duty or on watch and the member:

(a) sleeps at the member’s post or on watch; or

(b) is not on duty at a post but sleeps when the member’s duty requires the member to be awake; or

(c) is intoxicated; or

(d) leaves their post before being regularly relieved or otherwise absents themself from a place where it is the member’s duty to be.

Note: For the meaning of ***intoxicated***, see subsection 3(1).

###### 7 Disciplinary infringement provision—failure to perform duty or carry out activity

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) the member’s office or appointment, or the requirements of the Defence Force, require the member to perform a duty or carry out an activity; and

(b) the member fails to perform the duty or carry out the activity.

###### 8 Disciplinary infringement provision—prejudicial conduct

(1) A prescribed defence member contravenes this subsection if the member does an act that is likely to prejudice the discipline of, or bring discredit on, the Defence Force.

(2) A prescribed defence member contravenes this subsection if the member:

(a) omits to perform an act; and

(b) the omission is likely to prejudice the discipline of, or bring discredit on, the Defence Force.

### Part 3—Disciplinary infringement provisions only within jurisdiction of senior discipline officers

###### 9 Disciplinary infringement provision—absent without leave not exceeding 24 hours

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if the member:

(a) is absent without leave; and

(b) the absence is for a period not exceeding 24 hours.

Note: A senior discipline officer may also have jurisdiction to deal with a contravention of the disciplinary infringement provision in section 3 of this Schedule which covers absence without leave for a period not exceeding 3 hours.

###### 10 Disciplinary infringement provision—insubordinate conduct

(1) A prescribed defence member contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the member engages in conduct that is threatening, insubordinate or insulting to a person; and

(b) the person is a superior officer.

(2) A prescribed defence member contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the member uses language that is threatening, insubordinate or insulting about a person; and

(b) the language is used in that person’s presence; and

(c) the person is a superior officer.

###### 11 Disciplinary infringement provision—creating a disturbance etc.

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if the member creates a disturbance or takes part in creating or continuing a disturbance.

###### 12 Disciplinary infringement provision—insulting or provocative words

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if the member uses insulting or provocative words to another person or otherwise harasses another person.

###### 13 Disciplinary infringement provision—unauthorised discharge of weapon

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) the member engages in conduct; and

(b) the conduct causes, or contributes to, the discharge of a weapon; and

(c) the discharge of the weapon is not authorised.

###### 14 Disciplinary infringement provision—negligent discharge of weapon

(1) A prescribed defence member contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the member engages in conduct; and

(b) the conduct causes, or contributes to, the discharge of a weapon; and

(c) the member is negligent as to that result.

(2) For the purposes of paragraph (1)(c), in deciding whether the prescribed defence member is negligent, to the extent that it is required to have regard to the standard of care of a reasonable person to make that decision, regard is to be had to the standard of care that would have been exercised by a reasonable person who:

(a) was a defence member with the same training and experience in the Defence Force or other armed force as the prescribed defence member; and

(b) was engaged in the conduct in the course of the member’s duty or in accordance with the requirements of the Defence Force, as the case may be.

###### 15 Disciplinary infringement provision—intoxicated while on duty etc.

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) the member is on duty, or reports or should report for duty; and

(b) the member is intoxicated.

Note: For the meaning of ***intoxicated***, see subsection 3(1).

###### 16 Disciplinary infringement provision—driving a service vehicle for unauthorised purpose

A prescribed defence member contravenes this section if:

(a) the member:

(i) drives a service vehicle in any place, whether a public place or not; and

(ii) is not authorised to drive that vehicle; or

(b) the member uses a service vehicle for an unauthorised purpose.

###### 17 Disciplinary infringement provision—driving without due care or attention etc.

(1) A prescribed defence member contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the member drives a service vehicle in any place, whether a public place or not; and

(b) the member does so without due care and attention or without reasonable consideration for another person in that place.

(2) A prescribed defence member contravenes this subsection if:

(a) the member drives a vehicle on service land; and

(b) the member does so without due care and attention or without reasonable consideration for another person on that land.

## Schedule 6—Transitional rules

1 Transitional rules

(1) The Minister may, by legislative instrument, make rules prescribing matters:

(a) required or permitted by this Act to be prescribed by the rules; or

(b) necessary or convenient to be prescribed for carrying out or giving effect to this Act.

(2) Without limiting subitem (1), the rules may prescribe matters of a transitional nature (including prescribing any saving or application provisions) relating to the application of amendments or repeals made by this Act to proceedings under the *Defence Force Discipline Act 1982* at or after the commencement of this Schedule that commenced but were not concluded before the commencement of this Act.

(a) create an offence or civil penalty;

(b) provide powers of:

(i) arrest or detention; or

(ii) entry, search or seizure;

(c) impose a tax;

(d) set an amount to be appropriated from the Consolidated Revenue Fund;

(e) directly amend the text of this Act, the *Defence Force Discipline Act 1982* or the *Defence Force Discipline Appeals Act 1955*.
