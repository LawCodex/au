###### 40‑30 Notice of suspension under Subdivision 40‑A

Notice must be given within 14 days

(1) If the Board suspends your registration under Subdivision 40‑A, the Board must, within 14 days of the decision to suspend your registration, notify you in writing of:

###### 40‑45 Interim suspension of registration

(1) If you are a \*registered tax agent or BAS agent, the Board may suspend your registration if the Board is satisfied on reasonable grounds that:

(a) you have engaged in conduct that may constitute:

(i) an offence against a \*taxation law; or

(ii) a contravention of a civil penalty provision that is in a taxation law; and

(b) having regard to the conduct, either:

(i) there is or may be a significant risk that one or more of your clients will suffer material loss or damage if your registration is not suspended; or

(ii) it is in the public interest to suspend your registration because there is or may be a significant risk to the revenue of the Commonwealth, the integrity of the tax system or the integrity of the tax profession.

(2) The period of suspension is 90 days or a lesser period determined by the Board.

(3) The Board is not required to observe any requirements of the natural justice hearing rule in exercising the power under subsection (1).

(4) The Board may extend the period of suspension for a further period or further periods of 90 days or less determined by the Board if:

(a) the Board is satisfied on reasonable grounds of the matters mentioned in paragraphs (1)(a) and (b); and

(b) an investigation under Subdivision 60‑E about the conduct mentioned in subsection (1) has commenced.

(5) A decision under subsection (4) must be made not less than 7 days before the day on which the period of suspension ends.

(6) If, during the period of suspension, the Board makes a decision under subsection 60‑125(2) in respect of the conduct, the suspension ends at the time the Board makes the decision.

#### Division 51—Criminal offences

Table of Subdivisions

51‑A Conduct that is prohibited without registration

##### Guide to this Division

###### 51‑1 What this Division is about

This Division contains criminal offences.

It is a criminal offence to provide tax agent services or BAS services if you are unregistered and you provide them for a fee.

It is also an offence to advertise that you can provide such services if you are unregistered or to represent that you are a registered tax agent or BAS agent if it is untrue.

###### 51‑5 Providing tax agent services (other than BAS services) if unregistered

Fault‑based offence

(1) You commit an offence if:

(a) you provide a \*tax agent service; and

(b) the tax agent service is not a \*BAS service or a \*tax (financial) advice service; and

(c) you charge or receive a fee or other reward for providing the tax agent service; and

(d) you are not a \*registered tax agent; and

(e) if you provide the tax agent service as a legal service—either:

(i) you are prohibited, under a \*State law or \*Territory law that regulates legal practice and the provision of legal services, from providing that tax agent service; or

(ii) the service consists of preparing, or lodging, a return or a statement in the nature of a return.

Penalty: 200 penalty units or imprisonment for 40 months, or both.

(2) Strict liability applies to paragraphs (1)(c) and (d).

Exception

(3) Subparagraph (1)(e)(ii) does not apply if you provide the \*tax agent service as a legal service in the course of acting for a trust or deceased estate as trustee or \*legal personal representative.

Note: A defendant bears an evidential burden in relation to the matter in subsection (3): see subsection 13.3(3) of the *Criminal Code*.

###### 51‑10 Providing BAS services if unregistered

Fault‑based offence

(1) You commit an offence if:

(a) you provide a \*BAS service; and

(b) you charge or receive a fee or other reward for providing the BAS service; and

(c) you are not a \*registered tax agent or BAS agent; and

(d) if you provide the BAS service as a legal service—either:

(i) you are prohibited, under a \*State law or \*Territory law that regulates legal practice and the provision of legal services, from providing that BAS service; or

(ii) the service consists of preparing, or lodging, a return or a statement in the nature of a return; and

(e) if the BAS service relates to imports or exports to which an \*indirect tax law applies—you are not a customs broker licensed under Part XI of the *Customs Act 1901*.

Penalty: 200 penalty units or imprisonment for 40 months, or both.

(2) Strict liability applies to paragraphs (1)(b) and (c).

(3) Subparagraph (1)(d)(ii) does not apply if you provide the \*BAS service as a legal service in the course of acting for a trust or deceased estate as trustee or \*legal personal representative.

Note: A defendant bears an evidential burden in relation to the matter in subsection (4): see subsection 13.3(3) of the *Criminal Code*.

###### 51‑15 Advertising tax agent services (other than BAS agent services) if unregistered

Fault‑based offence

(1) You commit an offence if:

(a) you advertise that you will provide a \*tax agent service; and

(b) the tax agent service is not a \*BAS service or a \*tax (financial) advice service; and

(c) you are not a \*registered tax agent; and

(d) if the tax agent service would be provided as a legal service—either:

(i) you are prohibited, under a \*State law or \*Territory law that regulates legal practice and the provision of legal services, from providing that tax agent service; or

(ii) the service would consist of preparing, or lodging, a return or a statement in the nature of a return; and

(e) if the tax agent service would be provided on a voluntary basis—you would not provide the service under a scheme that the Commissioner has approved by notifiable instrument made under section 70‑53.

Penalty: 200 penalty units or imprisonment for 40 months, or both.

(2) Strict liability applies to paragraph (1)(c).

Exception

(3) Subparagraph (1)(d)(ii) does not apply if you would provide the \*tax agent service as a legal service in the course of acting for a trust or deceased estate as trustee or \*legal personal representative.

Note: A defendant bears an evidential burden in relation to the matter in subsection (3): see subsection 13.3(3) of the *Criminal Code*.

###### 51‑20 Advertising BAS services if unregistered

Fault‑based offence

(1) You commit an offence if:

(a) you advertise that you will provide a \*BAS service; and

(b) you are not a \*registered tax agent or BAS agent; and

(c) if the BAS service would be provided as a legal service—either:

(i) you are prohibited, under a \*State law or \*Territory law that regulates legal practice and the provision of legal services, from providing that BAS service; or

(ii) the service would consist of preparing, or lodging, a return or a statement in the nature of a return; and

(d) if the BAS service relates to imports or exports to which an \*indirect tax law applies—you are not a customs broker licensed under Part XI of the *Customs Act 1901*; and

(e) if the BAS service would be provided on a voluntary basis—you would not provide the service under a scheme that the Commissioner has approved by notifiable instrument made under section 70‑53.

Penalty: 200 penalty units or imprisonment for 40 months, or both.

(2) Strict liability applies to paragraph (1)(b).

Exception

(3) Subparagraph (1)(c)(ii) does not apply if you would provide the \*BAS service as a legal service in the course of acting for a trust or deceased estate as trustee or \*legal personal representative.

Note: A defendant bears an evidential burden in relation to the matter in subsection (3): see subsection 13.3(3) of the *Criminal Code*.

(4) The Commissioner may, by notifiable instrument, approve a scheme for the purposes of paragraph (1)(e).

###### 51‑25 Representing that you are a registered tax agent or BAS agent if unregistered

(1) You commit an offence if:

(a) you represent that you are a \*registered tax agent or BAS agent; and

(b) you are not a registered tax agent or BAS agent.

Penalty: 200 penalty units or imprisonment for 40 months, or both.

(2) Strict liability applies to paragraph (1)(b).

###### 855‑10 Renewable energy asset test

(1) A membership interest held by an entity (the ***holding entity***) in another entity (the ***test entity***) passes the renewable energy asset test if the sum of the market values of the test entity’s Australian renewable energy assets is equal to or greater than 3 times the sum of the market values of its assets that are other taxable Australian real property.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), treat an asset of an entity (the ***first entity***) that is a membership interest in another entity (the ***other entity***) as if it were instead the following 2 assets:

(a) an asset that is an Australian renewable energy asset (the ***RE asset***);

(b) an asset that is other taxable Australian real property (the ***non‑RE asset***).

(a) treat the market value of the RE asset as the product of:

(i) the sum of the market values of all the assets of the other entity that are Australian renewable energy assets; and

(ii) the first entity’s direct participation interest in the other entity; and

(b) treat the market value of the non‑RE asset as the product of:

(i) the sum of the market values of all the assets of the other entity that are other taxable Australian real property; and

(ii) the first entity’s direct participation interest in the other entity.

Note 1: It is necessary to work out the market value of any RE assets and non‑RE assets in relation to any membership interests held by the other entity before working out the value of the RE asset and non‑RE asset held by the first entity.

(1) The purpose of this section is to prevent double counting of the market value of the assets of a corporate group that:

(a) are Australian renewable energy assets; and

(b) are created under arrangements under which corresponding liabilities are created in other members of the group.

(a) the parties to an arrangement included the 2 entities referred to in subsection (3); and

(b) an effect of the arrangement was to create, before the CGT event happened:

(i) the asset as an asset of one of those 2 parties; and

(ii) a corresponding liability of the other (the ***other party***).

(3) The 2 entities are either:

(a) the first entity and the other entity (see subsection 855‑10(2)); or

(b) both:

(i) that first entity or that other entity; and

(ii) an entity that is a first entity or other entity for the purposes of a related application of subsection 855‑10(2).

(4) Disregard:

(a) if the other party is the test entity (see subsection 855‑10(1))—the asset’s market value; or

(b) otherwise—the percentage of the asset’s market value equal to the percentage that is the test entity’s total participation interest in the other party.

###### 51ABSB Acquisitions that do not result in control—meaning of associate

Meaning of associate—main rule

(1) For the purposes of paragraph 51ABSA(2)(b), a person (the ***second person***) is an ***associate*** of a person (the ***primary person***) in relation to a body corporate (the ***designated body***) if, and only if, one or more of the following paragraphs applies:

(i) a body corporate the primary person controls; or

(ii) a body corporate that controls the primary person; or

(iii) a body corporate that is controlled by an entity that controls the primary person;

(b) the second person is a person with whom the primary person has, or proposes to enter into, a relevant agreement for the purpose of controlling or influencing the outcome of decisions about the designated body’s financial and operating policies;

(c) the second person is a person with whom the primary person is acting, or proposing to act, in concert in relation to controlling or influencing the outcome of decisions about the designated body’s financial and operating policies.

Exception—is not an associate merely because of certain rights

(2) Despite paragraph (1)(b) or (c), the second person is *not* an ***associate*** of the primary person in relation to the designated body merely because:

(a) they have entered or propose to enter into a relevant agreement; and

(b) under the relevant agreement, one of them has or will have one or more rights:

(i) of a class set out in subsection (3); and

(ii) whether enforceable presently or in the future, and whether or not on the fulfilment of a condition.

(3) The classes of rights are as follows:

(a) a right to dispose of securities in the designated body;

(b) a right to control the exercise of a power to dispose of securities in the designated body;

(c) a right that is both:

(i) consistent with a right normally accorded to minority shareholders in order to protect their financial interests as investors; and

(ii) reasonably appropriate and adapted to achieving the purpose of protecting a minority shareholder’s financial interests, in their capacity as an investor, and not for some other purpose;

(d) a right to the distribution of the profits of the designated body in accordance with a policy for allocating such rights for classes of securities in the designated body;

(e) a right under the relevant agreement if the agreement:

(i) would be reasonable in the circumstances if the parties were dealing at arm’s length; and

(ii) is a financing agreement, or a standard shareholder (or member) agreement about governance processes; and

(a) protections relating to changes to a body corporate’s constitution or capital;

(b) protections relating to the liquidation, sale or winding up of a body corporate;

(d) rights to access information consistent with a minority shareholder’s investment;

### Part 2—Transitional provisions

16 References to ancillary funds etc.

(1) In any instrument under an Act or in any other instrument:

(a) a reference to an ancillary fund is taken to include a reference to a giving fund; and

(b) a reference to an ancillary or community charity trust fund is taken to include a reference to a giving or community charity trust fund; and

(c) a reference to a public ancillary fund is taken to include a reference to a public giving fund; and

(d) a reference to the public ancillary fund guidelines is taken to include a reference to the public giving fund guidelines; and

(e) a reference to a private ancillary fund is taken to include a reference to a private giving fund; and

(f) a reference to the private ancillary fund guidelines is taken to include a reference to the private giving fund guidelines.

(2) Subsection (1) is subject to a contrary intention.

17 Public ancillary fund guidelines

The public ancillary fund guidelines in force immediately before the commencement of this Schedule continue in force (and may be dealt with) after that commencement as if they were public giving fund guidelines.

18 Private ancillary fund guidelines

The private ancillary fund guidelines in force immediately before the commencement of this Schedule continue in force (and may be dealt with) after that commencement as if they were private giving fund guidelines.

## Schedule 8—Tax credits under the foreign resident capital gains withholding regime

### Part 1—Amendment of the Taxation Administration Act 1953

##### Taxation Administration Act 1953

**1 At the end of section 18‑15 in Schedule 1**

Add:

(3) For the purposes of subsection (1), if Subdivision 14‑D applies to a \*withholding payment made to the entity, then the withholding payment is taken to have been made to the entity in the income year for which the withholding payment is recognised for income tax purposes for the entity.

Note 1: This subsection ensures that the entity’s entitlement to the credit is determined for the income year in which the withholding payment is recognised for income tax purposes for the entity.

Note 2: The withholding payment may be recognised for income tax purposes for the entity at different times depending on whether the payment is recognised on revenue or capital account for the entity.

**2 After subsection 18‑20(1) in Schedule 1**

Insert:

(1A) For the purposes of subsection (1), if Subdivision 14‑D applies to a \*withholding payment made to the partnership, then the withholding payment is taken to have been made to the partnership in the income year for which the withholding payment is recognised for income tax purposes for the partnership.

Note 1: This subsection ensures that the entity’s entitlement to the credit is determined for the income year in which the withholding payment is recognised for income tax purposes for the partnership.

Note 2: The withholding payment may be recognised for income tax purposes for the partnership at different times depending on whether the payment is recognised on revenue or capital account for the partnership.

**3 After subsection 18‑25(1) in Schedule 1**

Insert:

(1A) For the purposes of subsection (1), if Subdivision 14‑D applies to a \*withholding payment made to the trustee of a trust, then the withholding payment is taken to have been made to the trustee in the income year for which the withholding payment is recognised for income tax purposes for the trust.

Note 1: This subsection ensures that the entity’s entitlement to the credit is determined for the income year in which the withholding payment is recognised for income tax purposes for the trust.

Note 2: The withholding payment may be recognised for income tax purposes for the trust at different times depending on whether the payment is recognised on revenue or capital account for the trust.

4 Application provision

The amendments made by items 1 to 3 apply in relation to withholding payments that are made on or after the day this Schedule commences.
