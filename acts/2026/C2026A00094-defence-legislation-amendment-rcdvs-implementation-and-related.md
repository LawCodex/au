### Part VIAB—Wellbeing, health and safety support for defence members, veterans and their families

#### Division 1—Preliminary

###### 70H Simplified outline of this Part

The purpose of this Part is to support the wellbeing, health and safety of members of the Defence Force, former members of the Defence Force, and their families.

The collection, use and disclosure of information about such persons is authorised:

(a) for research or data analysis or evaluation into wellbeing, health or safety; and

(b) to support wellbeing, health or safety outcomes.

###### 70J Object of this Part

The object of this Part is to support the wellbeing, health and safety of members and former members of the Defence Force, and their families, including through:

(a) facilitating research and data analysis or evaluation in relation to the wellbeing, health or safety of members, former members, and their families; and

(b) providing clarity and transparency in relation to the collection, use and disclosure of information about members, former members, and their families; and

(c) supporting the delivery of health services through the Defence health system to members of the Defence Force and other persons involved with, or significantly connected to, the Defence Force to:

(i) ensure members are fit to serve; and

(ii) support the lifetime wellbeing, health and safety of members and those other persons; and

(d) supporting members’ trust and confidence in the Defence health system, including through:

(i) providing clarity and transparency in relation to the collection, use and disclosure of Defence health services information; and

(ii) facilitating health monitoring activities and quality assurance and improvement activities; and

(e) facilitating the provision by the Commonwealth of services, payments and benefits to support members, former members, and their families.

#### Division 2—Information sharing to support wellbeing, health or safety

##### Subdivision A—Authorised collection, use and disclosure of information to support wellbeing, health or safety

###### 70K Collection, use or disclosure of information about relevant ADF persons for research or data analysis or evaluation

(1) An entrusted person may collect, use or disclose information (including personal information and sensitive information) about a relevant ADF person for the purposes of:

(a) undertaking research or data analysis or evaluation in relation to the wellbeing, health or safety of relevant ADF persons; or

(b) assisting another person to undertake research or data analysis or evaluation in relation to the wellbeing, health or safety of relevant ADF persons.

(2) A ***relevant ADF person*** is:

(a) a member of the Defence Force; or

(b) a person who has ceased to be a member of the Defence Force, whether by reason of death or otherwise; or

(c) a family member of a person referred to in paragraph (a) or (b); or

(d) a former family member of a person referred to in paragraph (a) or (b).

(3) For the purposes of this section, an ***entrusted person*** is:

(a) the Minister; or

(b) a member of the Defence Force; or

(c) a defence civilian (within the meaning of Part VIIIA); or

(d) the Secretary of, or an APS employee in, the Department; or

(e) a person engaged as a consultant, or contractor, to perform services for, or supply goods to, the Department or the Australian Defence Force; or

(f) an employee, or official of, a Commonwealth, State or Territory authority that is assisting the Department or the Australian Defence Force and has been made available by the authority for that purpose.

(4) An entrusted person may only collect, use or disclose information under subsection (1) if:

(a) to the extent the information does not concern medical research:

(i) guidelines under subsection 70KC(1) are in force; and

(ii) the collection, use or disclosure is in accordance with any applicable provisions of those guidelines; and

(b) to the extent the information concerns medical research—the collection, use or disclosure is in accordance with any applicable guidelines issued under subsection 95(1) of the *Privacy Act 1988*.

(5) An entrusted person may only disclose information under subsection (1) if the entrusted person is satisfied that all reasonable steps have been taken to de‑identify any personal information included in the information before the information is disclosed.

###### 70KA Disclosure of information held by Department or Defence Force about relevant ADF persons to support wellbeing, health or safety outcomes

(1) The Secretary must disclose information specified in an instrument under subsection (3) that is held by the Department or the Defence Force to an entity specified in the instrument.

(2) An entity specified in the instrument may collect, use or disclose the information if:

(a) the collection, use or disclosure is for a purpose specified in the instrument for the entity; and

(b) the collection, use or disclosure is in accordance with any conditions specified in the instrument that apply to the entity; and

(c) to the extent the information does not concern medical research:

(i) guidelines under subsection 70KC(1) are in force; and

(ii) the collection, use or disclosure is in accordance with any applicable provisions of those guidelines; and

(d) to the extent the information concerns medical research—the collection, use or disclosure is in accordance with any applicable guidelines issued under subsection 95(1) of the *Privacy Act 1988*.

(3) The Minister may specify, by legislative instrument, the following for the purposes of subsection (1):

(a) the information (including personal information and sensitive information) about relevant ADF persons that must be disclosed;

(b) the specified entities (which may be a person or a body, agency, authority or Department of the Commonwealth, a State or a Territory) to which the information must be disclosed to;

(c) the purposes for which a specified entity may collect, use or disclose the information;

(d) any other conditions on the collection, use or disclosure of the information by a specified entity.

(4) The Minister may only make an instrument under subsection (3) authorising the collection, use or disclosure of information if the Minister is satisfied that:

(a) the collection, use or disclosure would support wellbeing, health or safety outcomes of relevant ADF persons; and

(b) the impact on the privacy of any individual arising from the collection, use or disclosure is proportionate having regard to the benefits to other relevant ADF persons arising from the collection, use or disclosure.

Delegation

(5) The Minister may delegate the Minister’s powers under this section to:

(a) a person who holds, or is acting in, an SES Band 2 position, or an equivalent or higher position, in the Department; or

(b) a person who holds a rank not lower than the rank of Rear‑Admiral, Major‑General or Air Vice‑Marshal.

###### 70KB Collection, use and disclosure of information about relevant ADF persons to support wellbeing, health or safety outcomes authorised by a Minister

(1) A person covered by an instrument made under subsection (2), may collect, use or disclose information specified in the instrument if:

(a) the collection, use or disclosure is for a purpose specified in the instrument; and

(b) the collection, use or disclosure is in accordance with any conditions specified in the instrument that apply to the person; and

(c) to the extent the information does not concern medical research:

(i) guidelines under subsection 70KC(1) are in force; and

(ii) the collection, use or disclosure is in accordance with any applicable provisions of those guidelines; and

(d) to the extent the information concerns medical research—the collection, use or disclosure is in accordance with any applicable guidelines issued under subsection 95(1) of the *Privacy Act 1988*.

(2) A Minister administering a body, agency, authority or Department of the Commonwealth (the ***Commonwealth body***) may specify, by legislative instrument, the following for the purposes of subsection (1):

(a) the information (including personal information and sensitive information) about relevant ADF persons that may be collected, used or disclosed by the Commonwealth body;

(b) the persons (the ***entrusted persons***) employed or engaged by the Commonwealth body, or assisting the Commonwealth body, that may collect, use or disclose the information;

(c) the purposes for which the entrusted persons may collect, use or disclose the information;

(d) the persons to whom the entrusted persons may disclose the information;

(e) any other conditions on the collection, use or disclosure of the information.

Note: The reference to a Minister in this subsection includes the Minister administering this Act who may, for example, make an instrument for the Department, the Department of Veterans’ Affairs (if administered by the Minister) or the Australian Defence Force.

(3) A Minister may only make an instrument under subsection (2) authorising the collection, use or disclosure of information if the Minister is satisfied that:

(a) the collection, use or disclosure would support wellbeing, health or safety outcomes of relevant ADF persons; and

(b) the impact on the privacy of any individual arising from the collection, use or disclosure is proportionate having regard to the benefits to other relevant ADF persons arising from the collection, use or disclosure.

(4) The other provisions of this Part do not limit the matters that may be dealt with by an instrument under this section.

Delegation

(5) A Minister may delegate the Minister’s powers under this section to:

(a) the Secretary of a Department administered by the Minister; or

(b) a person who holds, or is acting in, an SES Band 2 position, or an equivalent or higher position, in a Department or other body administered by the Minister; or

(c) a person who holds a rank not lower than the rank of Rear‑Admiral, Major‑General or Air Vice‑Marshal.

###### 70KC Ministerial guidelines for research or data analysis or evaluation

(1) The Minister may, by legislative instrument, make guidelines for the collection, use or disclosure of information about relevant ADF persons for the purposes of undertaking, or assisting to undertake, research or data analysis or evaluation.

(2) Guidelines made under subsection (1) must:

(a) require that, unless otherwise specified, information about a relevant ADF person is only collected, used or disclosed in accordance with the consent of the relevant ADF person; and

(b) only specify otherwise if the Minister is satisfied that the impact on the privacy of an individual arising from the collection, use or disclosure is proportionate, having regard to the benefits to other relevant ADF persons arising from the collection, use or disclosure; and

(c) not make provision for any matter that is dealt with in any applicable guidelines issued under subsection 95(1) of the *Privacy Act 1988*.

(3) Before making guidelines under subsection (1), the Minister must seek advice from the Information Commissioner and must have regard to that advice.

(4) If the Minister is not the Veterans’ Affairs Minister, the Minister must make guidelines under subsection (1) jointly with the Veterans’ Affairs Minister.

#### Division 3—Defence health services

##### Subdivision A—The Defence health system and Defence health services

###### 70L The Defence health system

(1) The primary purposes of the Defence health system are:

(a) to provide Defence health services:

(i) to keep members of the Defence Force fit for the performance of their duties; and

(ii) to support the lifetime wellbeing, health and safety of members of the Defence Force; and

(iii) to other persons for the purposes of supporting the operation of the Australian Defence Force; and

(b) to provide Defence health services in a manner commensurate with, or to a higher standard than, what is required of civilian health practitioners when providing health services to the general community in Australia.

(2) ***Defence health services*** are health services provided, or arranged to be provided, by the Commonwealth:

(a) to a member of the Defence Force (whether rendering continuous full time service or otherwise); or

(b) to a member of a visiting force (within the meaning of the *Defence (Visiting Forces) Act 1963*); or

(c) to a person for the purposes of determining whether the person is fit to be a member of the Defence Force; or

(d) to a person who is not a member of the Defence Force but is:

(i) involved with an operation of the Australian Defence Force; or

(ii) involved in the provision of assistance mentioned in subsection 123AA(1); or

(e) to a person who is not a member of the Defence Force but is included in a prescribed class of persons who:

(i) are otherwise involved with the Australian Defence Force; or

(ii) have a significant connection to the Australian Defence Force.

(3) ***Defence health services*** include the supply of items, including pharmaceuticals, health consumables, equipment and therapeutic products, for the purposes of the provision of Defence health services.

(4) A ***Defence health system worker*** is a person:

(a) who:

(i) provides a Defence health service; or

(ii) assists a person to provide a Defence health service; or

(iii) performs functions or duties or exercises powers in the course of or for the purposes of planning, managing or administering the Defence health system; and

(b) who is:

(i) a member of the Defence Force; or

(ii) a defence civilian (within the meaning of Part VIIIA); or

(iii) the Secretary of, or an APS employee in, the Department; or

(iv) engaged as a consultant, or contractor, to perform services for, or supply goods to, the Department or the Australian Defence Force; or

(v) an employee, or official of, a Commonwealth, State or Territory authority that is assisting the Department or the Australian Defence Force and has been made available by the authority for that purpose; or

(vi) a member of a visiting force (within the meaning of the *Defence (Visiting Forces) Act 1963*); or

(vii) otherwise covered by an agreement or arrangement for the provision of Defence health services outside of Australia.

(5) Information is ***Defence health services information*** if it is obtained or generated by a Defence health system worker in the course of or for the purposes of:

(a) providing, or assisting a person to provide, a Defence health service; or

(b) planning, managing or administering the Defence health system; or

(c) performing a function or duty, or exercising a power, under this Division.

(6) A ***Defence health practitioner*** is a Defence health system worker who provides Defence health services as:

(a) a medical practitioner; or

(b) a dental practitioner; or

(c) a nurse; or

(d) an allied health professional; or

(e) a health professional of a prescribed kind.

###### 70LA Defence health services that must be provided to members rendering continuous full time service

(1) The Commonwealth must arrange provision to a member of the Defence Force rendering continuous full time service of Defence health services that are necessary to keep the member fit for the performance of the member’s duties.

(2) The Commonwealth must arrange for the supply of items required for the provision of such Defence health services to such members, including arranging associated activities such as transport, storage and possession of such items.

###### 70LB Obligations on members and Defence health practitioners in relation to fitness for duty

(1) A member of the Defence Force must inform the Surgeon General ADF of any health service rendered to the member that is not a Defence health service.

(2) If:

(a) a Defence health practitioner provides a Defence health service to a member of the Defence Force; and

(b) the Defence health practitioner is aware of a circumstance in relation to the member that is affecting, or may reasonably be likely to affect, the member’s fitness to perform the member’s duties;

the practitioner must inform the person who is in direct command of the member or is the member’s direct supervisor.

###### 70LC Recovery of the costs of Defence health services in certain circumstances

(1) If:

(a) the Commonwealth provides a Defence health service to a person (the ***patient***) in relation to an illness or injury; and

(b) the Secretary is satisfied that the patient may have an enforceable claim for damages against a person for the illness or injury; and

(c) the Secretary is satisfied that it is reasonable in the circumstances for the Commonwealth to recoup an amount for the service from the patient;

the Secretary may, in writing, require the patient to pay the amount to the Commonwealth.

(2) The amount must not exceed the expense incurred by the Commonwealth.

(3) The amount is a debt due to the Commonwealth.

(4) Without limiting the means by which the debt may be recovered, the Commonwealth may deduct the amount from any salary or allowances paid by the Commonwealth to the patient.

(5) Subsection (1):

(a) applies regardless of when the illness or injury occurred or when the enforceable claim for damages may have arisen; and

(b) continues to apply to a person who ceases to be a member at any time after receiving the service.

###### 70LD Application of State and Territory laws to Defence health services where Defence Instruction issued

If a Defence Instruction deals with a matter relating to:

(a) the provision of a Defence health service; or

(b) an activity associated with the provision of a Defence health service (such as transport, storage and possession of items required to provide the Defence health service);

the provision of the Defence health service, or the conduct of the activity, in a State or Territory is not required to comply with any law of a State or Territory dealing with that matter.

###### 70LE Approved health monitoring activities

(1) The Surgeon General ADF may, by legislative instrument, approve a health monitoring activity or class of health monitoring activities.

(2) A ***health monitoring activity*** is:

(a) an activity undertaken for the purposes of monitoring the health of the Defence Force population over time, including for the purposes of:

(i) identifying and analysing the incidence, prevalence or distribution of disease, injury or health risk factors in the population; and

(ii) identifying and analysing trends in the wellbeing, health and fitness of individuals in the population; and

(iii) developing, guiding, and assessing the effectiveness of, policies, programmes or interventions to improve health outcomes, or prevent disease or injury, in the population; and

(iv) supporting the operation of the Defence Force; or

(b) an activity undertaken for the purposes of monitoring the operation or performance of the Defence health system over time.

(3) An approval under subsection (1) may provide that the activity or class of activity may only be conducted:

(a) by specified persons or a class of specified persons; and

(b) in compliance with specified conditions.

###### 70LF Authorised disclosure to public health authorities

(1) The Surgeon General ADF may, by legislative instrument, approve the disclosure of specified Defence health services information to State, Territory or foreign public health authorities in specified circumstances if the Surgeon General ADF is satisfied that civilian health practitioners would be authorised or required in similar circumstances to disclose information of that kind to a public health authority.

(2) An approval under subsection (1) may provide that disclosure may only be made:

(a) by specified persons or a class of specified persons; and

(b) in compliance with specified conditions.

(3) However, a disclosure must only be made to a State or Territory public health authority in circumstances where the public health authority has undertaken not to use or further disclose the information except in accordance with an agreement that:

(a) is in force between the Commonwealth and the State or Territory; and

(b) applies in relation to the information.

(4) An instrument under subsection (1) may be made only to the extent that the instrument is with respect to one or more legislative powers of the Parliament.

(5) An instrument under subsection (1) must specify the legislative power or powers of the Parliament in respect of which the instrument is made.

(6) The other provisions of this Part do not limit the instruments that may be made for the purposes of subsection (1).

###### 70LG Immunity for Defence health system workers for authorised dealings with Defence health services information

(1) A Defence health system worker is not liable to a civil action or other civil proceeding for damages for or in relation to an act done or omitted in good faith:

(a) in relation to the collection, use or disclosure of Defence health services information as authorised by this Part; and

(b) in the performance or purported performance of the Defence health system worker’s duties.

(2) A person that wishes to rely on subsection (1) in relation to an action or other proceeding bears an evidential burden (within the meaning of the Regulatory Powers Act) in relation to that matter.

##### Subdivision B—Collection, use and disclosure of Defence health services information

###### 70M Authorised collection, use and disclosure of Defence health services information

A Defence health system worker may collect, use or disclose Defence health services information (including personal information and sensitive information) about a person if the collection, use or disclosure:

(a) is authorised under the *Privacy Act 1988*, another provision of this Act or the *Military Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 2004*; or

(b) is necessary to provide a Defence health service to the person; or

(c) if the information relates to the family, social or medical history of the person—is necessary to provide a Defence health service to another person; or

(d) is for the purposes of:

(i) planning, managing or administering the Defence health system; or

(ii) conducting an approved health monitoring activity; or

(iii) conducting a quality assurance and improvement activity; or

(iv) conducting an approved quality assurance and improvement activity; or

(v) a disclosure required by subsection 70LB(2); or

(vi) a disclosure approved under subsection 70LF(1).

###### 70MA Disclosure of Defence health services information about a deceased person

A Defence health system worker may disclose Defence health services information (including personal information and sensitive information) about a deceased person who was a member of the Defence Force:

(a) to an executor or an administrator of the deceased person’s estate; or

(b) to a family member of the deceased person where the Defence health system worker is satisfied that for compassionate reasons the information should be disclosed to the family member; or

(c) as required or authorised by a Commonwealth law; or

(d) as required by a State or Territory law.

###### 70MB Use and disclosure of Defence health services information for other individual health purposes

Disclosure where person incapable of giving consent

(1) A Defence health practitioner may disclose Defence health services information (including personal information and sensitive information) about a person if:

(a) the person is physically or legally incapable of giving consent to the disclosure; and

(b) another Defence health practitioner is satisfied that either:

(i) the disclosure is necessary to provide appropriate care or treatment of the person; or

(ii) the disclosure is made to a family member of the person for compassionate reasons; and

(c) the disclosure is not contrary to any wish:

(i) expressed by the person before the person became unable to give or communicate consent; and

(ii) of which the other Defence health practitioner is aware, or of which the other Defence health practitioner could reasonably be expected to be aware; and

(d) the disclosure is limited to the extent reasonable and necessary for a purpose mentioned in paragraph (b).

Use or disclosure—serious threat to life, health or safety of immediate family member

(2) A Defence health system worker may use or disclose Defence health services information that is health information about a member or former member of the Defence Force (the ***first person***) if:

(a) the Defence health system worker reasonably believes that the use or disclosure is necessary to lessen or prevent a serious threat to the life, health or safety of another person who is an immediate family member of the first person; and

(b) the use or disclosure is conducted in accordance with any applicable guidelines approved under section 95AA of the *Privacy Act 1988*; and

(c) in the case of disclosure:

(i) the recipient of the information is an immediate family member of the first person; and

(ii) the Defence health system worker is satisfied that the disclosure would support the wellbeing, health or safety of the first person or a family member of the first person.

##### Subdivision C—Quality assurance and improvement activities

###### 70N Quality assurance and improvement activities for Defence health services

Quality assurance and improvement activities

(1) Quality assurance and improvement activities may be conducted in relation to the provision of Defence health services.

(2) A ***quality assurance and improvement activity*** is:

(a) an assessment or evaluation of the quality of Defence health services or factors that may affect the quality of Defence health services; or

(b) an assessment or evaluation of an incident related to the provision of Defence health services; or

(c) the making of a recommendation to improve the provision of Defence health services or prevent incidents as a result of such an assessment or evaluation; or

(d) the monitoring of the implementation of such a recommendation.

Surgeon General ADF approved quality assurance and improvement activities

(3) The Surgeon General ADF may, by legislative instrument, approve a particular quality assurance and improvement activity, or a class of quality assurance and improvement activities, for the purposes of sections 70NA and 70NB.

(4) An approval under subsection (3) may provide that a quality assurance and improvement activity or class of activities may only be conducted:

(a) by specified persons or a class of specified persons; and

(b) in compliance with specified conditions.

###### 70NA Protection of information obtained in connection with an approved quality assurance and improvement activity

(1) A person commits an offence if:

(a) the person obtains information or a document:

(i) when the person is a Commonwealth officer (within the meaning of Part 5.6 of th*e Criminal Code*); and

(ii) in connection with an approved quality assurance and improvement activity; and

(b) the person discloses the information or document; and

(c) the disclosure is not:

(i) for the purposes of the approved quality assurance and improvement activity; or

(ii) otherwise authorised by the Surgeon General ADF; and

(d) none of the following apply:

(i) the information or document has been lawfully made available to the public;

(ii) the disclosure is to the person to whom the information or document relates;

(iii) the disclosure is to the person who provided the information or document.

Penalty: Imprisonment for 2 years.

(2) If a person has obtained information or a document in connection with an approved quality assurance and improvement activity, a Commonwealth, State or Territory court cannot require the person to disclose the information or produce the document.

(3) Despite subsections (1) and (2), this section does not apply to proceedings in a federal court exercising original jurisdiction in which a writ of mandamus or prohibition or an injunction is sought against an officer or officers of the Commonwealth.

###### 70NB Immunity for persons involved in approved quality assurance and improvement activity

(1) A person is not liable to a civil action or other civil proceeding for, or in relation to, an act done or omitted in good faith in connection with an approved quality assurance and improvement activity.

(2) A person that wishes to rely on subsection (1) in relation to an action or other proceeding bears an evidential burden (within the meaning of the Regulatory Powers Act) in relation to that matter.
